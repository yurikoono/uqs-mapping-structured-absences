## repeated_patterns

**RP-1: Catalytic cycle proposed as complete while at least one obligatory bimetallic/intermediate step is unobserved**

All four papers present a closed catalytic cycle whose narrative completeness exceeds the experimental support for at least one mechanistically necessary step. Kumada 1972: the Ni(0)/Ni(II) cycle (eqs 2–4) is constructed entirely by analogy with stoichiometric (dipy)Ni reactions; no intermediate is observed under [NiCl₂(dpe)] catalytic conditions; the black-mixture observation (B-12) suggesting Ni⁰ formation is recorded but not characterized (A-2, U-2). Breitenfeld 2014: the Ni(I)/Ni(II)/Ni(III) cycle (Figure 8) is presented with seven intermediates including an obligatory comproportionation step (Ni(I) 16 + Ni(III) 14 → 2 Ni(II)) for which no experimental or computational evidence is offered for the alkyl-aryl system; intermediate 12 is not spectroscopically detected (A-1, U-2, SRG-7). Cherney 2015: the Ni(I)/Ni(III) oxidation-state manifold underpins the mechanistic rationale for stereoconvergent coupling across sections 2.2.1–2.2.3 with no EPR, CV, or stoichiometric organometallic characterization cited anywhere in the 66-page review (U-1, SRG-2). Pengfei 2024: the Ni(I/II/III) cycle (Fig. 5c) is presented as supported, yet Path I vs. Path II cannot be discriminated by any reported experiment (A-1 vs. U-1) and the anodic half-reaction is structurally absent from the proposed cycle while critically governing yield (CLA-1, U-3).

```
evidence_chain: Kumada_1972 : A-2, U-2, B-12 (SRG-1) ; Breitenfeld_2014 : A-1, U-2, SRG-7 ; Cherney_2015 : U-1, B-6, C-5 (SRG-2) ; Pengfei_2024 : U-3 (CLA-1), U-1, U-6
```

**RP-2: Selectivity claimed without quantitative byproduct accounting / mass balance**

The titular selectivity claim of each paper rests on the relative magnitude of cross-product yield versus competing pathways, but no paper closes the mass balance against the predictions of its own proposed mechanism. Kumada 1972: eq 3 explicitly predicts homo-coupling product R–R as a stoichiometric byproduct of intermediate formation; no R–R is reported, quantified, or acknowledged as below detection limit for any Table I entry (B-6, U-1; SRG-2). Breitenfeld 2014: GC-MS yields and conversions for the radical-clock experiment with substrate 7 are generated by the described C₁₂H₂₆-internal-standard procedure but only the linear:cyclized ratio is reported (B-7); the catalyst order is reported as 1.7 (Fig. 5B) but the deviation from integer 2 is unexamined (B-4). Cherney 2015: no substrate has measured k_rac and k_coup; KR/DKR/DYKAT regime assignments rest on reagent-ratio dependence of ee (B-4, U-3, SRG-3); for Cu/L101 9-BBN, "excellent chemoselectivity" rests on a single aryl bromide competition experiment (B-11, SRG-5). Pengfei 2024: under Entry 1 the "highly selective" 85% cross product coexists with 17% secondary + 13% primary homocoupling (30% combined byproduct yield); the fate of ~15% of substrate 1 and ~66% of substrate 2 is not stated; the proposed selectivity model (concentration + electrochemical characteristics) is not derived against the observed distribution (U-1, B-1, B-6, SRG-4 + SRG-8).

## paradigm_competition

This group reveals a genuine **paradigm succession** (not competition) layered with a **residual paradigm competition** at the radical-mechanism level, all overlaid on a substrate-class progression sp² → sp³ → fully unactivated alkyl.

**Paradigm A: Two-electron Ni(0)/Ni(II) cycle (Kumada 1972; partially Cherney 2015 for some sec-alkyl Grignard cases)**
- Foundational mechanism for sp² cross-coupling.
- Systematically unanswered (retrospective): "Why does this cycle fail for sp³ electrophiles?" The retrospective answer (sp³ C–X bonds undergo SET more readily than concerted OA to Ni(0)) is not asked in Kumada 1972; the sp² restriction is framed as a positive feature (B-4).
```
evidence_chain: Kumada_1972 : B-4 ([REDACTED: substantial verbatim quotation; see Tier 9]) → "no textual basis in Kumada_1972" for why sp³ fails (retrospective gap)
```

**Paradigm B: Radical Ni(I)/Ni(II)/Ni(III) cycle (Breitenfeld 2014, Pengfei 2024; partially Cherney 2015 for stereoconvergent secondary-electrophile coupling)**
- Enables sp³ substrates via SET-generated carbon radicals.
- Systematically unanswered: [REDACTED: substantial verbatim quotation; see Tier 9] Breitenfeld 2014: redistributive transmetalation (eq 3) proposed without algebraic derivation (B-3, U-4). Pengfei 2024: Path I/Path II not discriminated; selectivity model not derived against observed 17%+13% homocoupling distribution (A-1, U-1, U-4).
```
evidence_chain: Breitenfeld_2014 : B-3, U-4 (rate law not derived for eq 3) ; Pengfei_2024 : U-1 (selectivity model unverified), A-1 ("Path II cannot be ruled out"), U-6 (mechanism rests on 2.8 kcal/mol DFT difference)
```

**Paradigm overlap zone (Cherney 2015):**
The 2015 review documents the transition zone where Paradigm A and Paradigm B coexist for different substrate classes within the same review. Stereoconvergent coupling of secondary alkyl electrophiles invokes Ni(I)/Ni(III) (Paradigm B logic); enantioselective coupling of secondary Grignard reagents via DKR/DYKAT invokes a configurationally labile organometallic (Paradigm A logic). The review does not articulate the coexistence as a paradigm transition; it presents both mechanisms as alternative explanations for different reaction types without asking whether the same Ni(I)/Ni(III) framework should apply across both.
```
evidence_chain: Cherney_2015 : U-1 (Ni¹/Ni³ for stereoconvergent), U-3 (DKR/KR for Grignard, Paradigm A logic)
```

**Critical observation — paradigm succession with retained blind spots:**
Paradigm B was developed precisely to answer Paradigm A's blind spot (sp³ substrate limitation), and Pengfei 2024 extends it further to fully unactivated alkyl halides without nucleophilic organometallic partners. However, Paradigm B inherits Paradigm A's mass-balance blind spot (RP-2) without modification. All four papers claim "selectivity[REDACTED: substantial verbatim quotation; see Tier 9]complete" or "preferred" cycles without characterizing every obligatory intermediate. The paradigm shift changed the catalytic mechanism (two-electron → radical), the substrate class (sp² → sp³ → unactivated), and the activation mode (thermal Grignard → thermal Grignard → electrochemical), but it did not change the evidentiary standard for claiming selectivity or for closing a catalytic cycle. This is paradigm succession with structural inheritance of the same evidentiary gap — the most analytically significant cross-paper finding in this group.

## divergences

**DV-1: Substrate class — sp² halides (Kumada 1972) → sp³ activated alkyl halides (Breitenfeld 2014) → both, surveyed (Cherney 2015) → fully unactivated alkyl halides without nucleophilic partner (Pengfei 2024)**

```

```
Accounts for divergence: This is the defining divergence of G2 — the progression from sp² restriction (Kumada 1972) to fully unactivated sp³–sp³ coupling without organometallic nucleophile (Pengfei 2024) constitutes the "boundary breakthrough" that organizes G2.

**DV-2: Mechanistic paradigm — two-electron (1972) → radical Ni(I/II/III) (2014, 2024) → both surveyed (2015)**

```
evidence_chain: Kumada_1972 : B-2 (Ni(0)/Ni(II) cycle) ; Breitenfeld_2014 : S0-1 ("bimetallic oxidative addition" via radical) ; Cherney_2015 : U-1 (Ni¹/Ni³ for stereoconvergent), U-3 (DKR for Grignard) ; Pengfei_2024 : S0-1 ("Ni(I/II/III) radical cycle")
```
Accounts for divergence: A necessary consequence of extending coupling to sp³ electrophiles whose C–X bonds do not undergo concerted OA to Ni(0). Cherney 2015 shows that the paradigm transition is not a simple replacement — older Ni(0)/Ni(II) logic survives for some Grignard/sec-alkyl cases.

**DV-3: Activation mode — thermal Grignard (1972, 2014) → thermal organozinc/organoboron (2015 surveys multiple) → electrochemical cross-electrophile (2024)**

```
evidence_chain: Kumada_1972 : S0-4 ("Grignard reagent + vinyl or aryl halide") ; Breitenfeld_2014 : S0-1 (PhMgCl) ; Cherney_2015 : S0-3 (organomagnesium, organozinc, organoboron, organotin, organosilicon, etc.) ; Pengfei_2024 : S0-1 ("cross-electrophile coupling", electroreduction)
```
Accounts for divergence: Pengfei 2024's electrochemical activation eliminates the need for an organometallic nucleophile entirely (cross-electrophile coupling), creating a fundamentally different selectivity challenge — selectivity between two electrophiles rather than between a nucleophile and an electrophile.

**DV-4: Evidentiary standard — analogy from stoichiometric (1972) → radical-clock + DFT + kinetics (2014) → review survey (2015) → CV/SWV + DFT + radical trapping + kinetics (2024)**

```
evidence_chain: Kumada_1972 : B-2 (analogy with (dipy)Ni only) ; Breitenfeld_2014 : U-3 (DFT as primary discriminant), B-4 (kinetics) ; Cherney_2015 : SRG-8 (DFT-without-experimental-validation across 3 systems) ; Pengfei_2024 : U-2 (DFT–CV reference electrode mismatch), U-6 (2.8 kcal/mol DFT difference, no uncertainty)
```
Accounts for divergence: Year and journal norms; also reflects the ascending role of DFT as a mechanistic tool that is adopted faster than its validation protocols. Of structural importance: the *quantitative size* of the DFT energy difference being discriminated *decreases* across the group (18.7 → 2.8 kcal/mol) while the rigour of uncertainty quantification does not increase. This is a case where the field's rhetorical confidence in DFT outpaces the methodology's resolving power.

**DV-5: Format — 3-page Communication (1972) → 8-page Article (2014) → 66-page Review (2015) → ~10-page Nature Catalysis full paper (2024)**

```
evidence_chain: Kumada_1972 : BIB-7 (communication) ; Breitenfeld_2014 : BIB-7 (full_paper, Organometallics) ; Cherney_2015 : BIB-7 (review, Chemical Reviews) ; Pengfei_2024 : BIB-7 (full_paper, Nature Catalysis)
```
Accounts for divergence: Each format imposes its own compression dynamics. Kumada's communication-format compression is era-tolerated. Cherney's review-format compression aggregates field-level norms (the systemic CLA-1 pattern is visible only at review scale). Pengfei 2024's Nature Catalysis full-paper format compresses mechanistic detail into supplementary material despite being formally a "full paper" — the displacement of the DFT methodology and the L11/DMA bifurcation to figure captions and supplementary figures is a high-impact-journal genre convention rather than a length constraint.
