## Paradigm structure of the integrated corpus

The integrated 9-paper set reveals a *layered* paradigm structure rather than a single succession or a single competition. Three orthogonal axes operate simultaneously, each spawning paradigm transitions that intersect but do not subsume each other.

**Axis 1 — Mechanistic paradigm (G2 spine):** two-electron Ni(0)/Ni(II) (1972) → radical Ni(I)/Ni(II)/Ni(III) (2014, 2024 ; partially 2015) → coexisting both for different substrate classes (review-scale 2015). Sequential succession with retained blind spot — Paradigm B inherits Paradigm A's mass-balance gap (CRP-2) without modification.

**Axis 2 — Substrate-class paradigm (G1 + G2 spine):** sp² halide (1972) → broader sp² + sp³ activated alkyl (1976b/2014/2015) → C(sp²)–O electrophile (2021) → fully unactivated sp³–sp³ without organometallic nucleophile (2024) → bis-electrophile cyclic sulfate desymmetrization (2016 Morken). Sequential succession with substrate-specific gap re-submergence — MgX₂ byproduct effects unaddressed in 1972, predicted-but-uncharacterized in 1976b, unaddressed in 2021 despite VTNA being sensitive to salt effects (G1 DV-3).

Genuine paradigm competition rather than succession — as of 2016 all three coexist, addressing different facets of the same parent limitation. Each paradigm's blind spot is structurally different: A leaves the rate-limiting step unidentified, B leaves the radical-vs-ionic discrimination untested, C leaves the reactive-conformation interpretation among multiple consistent alternatives.

**Critical integrated observation — paradigm transitions do not erase the corpus's recurring roots.** Across all three axes and across every paradigm transition in the 9-paper set, CRP-1 / CRP-2 / CRP-3 / CRP-4 / CRP-5 persist with the same severity profile. The mechanistic paradigm changes (two-electron → radical), the substrate class changes (sp² → unactivated sp³ → C(sp²)–O → cyclic sulfate), the catalyst-design philosophy changes (diphosphine → calixdiphosphine → photoredox/Ni dual → PyBox), and the activation mode changes (thermal Grignard → thermal organozinc/organoboron → visible-light photoredox → electrochemical). What does *not* change is the evidentiary standard for the central claim. This is the integrated corpus's most analytically significant finding: paradigm progress in Ni-catalyzed cross-coupling has been consistently extensive (new substrates, new mechanisms, new activation modes) but not consistently intensive (the depth of evidence for the central claim has not scaled with the technical capability of the field).

## Correspondence to Stage 3 §5 EP filling order

The integrated 2F reveals the following 9-paper filling landscape against Kumada 1972's eight Stage 3 EP packs (EP-01a through EP-09a):

- **EP-07a (Boundary test)**: Most filled. G2 papers (Breitenfeld 2014, Cherney 2015, Pengfei 2024) and G3 Morken 2016 collectively demonstrate that Kumada 1972's "Only Csp² halides" boundary was system-specific. Substantial fill.

- **EP-04a / EP-05a (Discrimination / Ranking)**: Weakly filled. Partial byproduct data (Kumada 1976b benzene for some entries), partial catalyst comparison (Monnereau 2009 single-substrate), and partial stereochemical discrimination (Morken 2016) exist but are insufficient for matched-pair quantitative ranking.
- **EP-08a / EP-09a (Replication / Benchmark)**: Not filled. No paper in the 9-paper corpus provides replicate measurements with uncertainty estimates for Kumada 1972's specific NiCl₂(dpe) system, and no paper performs a substrate-matched comparison between NiCl₂(dpe) and Tamura-Kochi Fe/Cu or Corriu/Masse Ni(acac)₂.

This inversion reflects the publication-incentive structure of the field: novel boundary-breaking chemistry and novel mechanisms are rewarded, while replicating or benchmarking existing systems is not. The cross-version stability of this finding — observed first under v17/Opus 4.6/Japanese-Stage-3 inputs and reproduced under v18.4/Opus 4.7/English-single-track inputs — supports its standing as a structural finding about the field rather than an artefact of any single prompt-version, model, or language pipeline.
