[S0-1] core_claim: The copper-free Heck–Cassar–Sonogashira (HCS) mechanism proceeds via direct coordination of the terminal alkyne onto the oxidative addition (Pd(II)) complex rather than through a Pd(II)–Pd(II) transmetalation pathway; depending on the palladium counterion and reaction conditions, two different catalytic pathways are supported — a neutral pathway (halides as counterion) and a cationic pathway (triflate as counterion).

[S0-2] evidence_type: ³¹P/¹H NMR spectroscopy (monitoring Pd(II) complex speciation, equilibrium constants, real-time reaction progress), HPLC and GC-MS (product quantification and detection of side products), stoichiometric and catalytic reactions with well-defined Pd(II) oxidative addition complexes, kinetic measurements (Vmax values from rate plots), and DFT calculations (PBE/def2-TZVP, Gibbs free energy profiles in DMF at 298 K for both neutral/anionic halide and cationic triflate pathways).

[S0-3] prior_work_position: The paper positions itself against the Pd(II)–Pd(II) transmetalation (TM) mechanism (involving bis-alkyne palladium complex M and two distinct palladium cycles) proposed by Košmrlj and co-workers and supported by kinetic studies, arguing that complex M is unstable in standard catalytic conditions and is not involved in a standard HCS coupling. The paper also revisits earlier mechanistic proposals (Heck 1975, Cassar 1975, Soheili 2003, Mårtensson 2008, Buchwald 2003) and concludes that the direct coordination mechanism is faster than the Pd(II)–Pd(II) transmetalation regardless of leaving group, solvent, alkyne substitution, or temperature.

[S0-4] methodology: The primary methodology is a combined experimental/computational approach: stoichiometric experiments using well-defined Pd(II) oxidative addition complexes (B^I_NO₂, B^Br_NO₂, B^Cl_NO₂, B^OTf_NO₂, B^I_CH₃) with defined equivalents of base (TMG, PYR, TEA) and alkyne (2a or 2b) monitored by ³¹P NMR spectroscopy, HPLC, and GC-MS; competitive reactions between the direct coordination pathway and the Pd(II)–Pd(II) transmetalation pathway using equimolar mixtures of B, M, and 2b; and DFT calculations at the PBE/def2-TZVP level with Gibbs free energy corrections in DMF solvent.
