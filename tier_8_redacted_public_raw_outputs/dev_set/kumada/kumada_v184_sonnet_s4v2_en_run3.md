# EP-Filling Selection Map

## Tamao, Sumitani, Kumada 1972 (Journal of the American Chemical Society, communication)

---

### §A Three-tier classification of EP filling difficulty

**Tier 1 (clearly trackable in the literature):** EP-06a, EP-03a, EP-07a, EP-04a

- **EP-06a [Analogy-validation pack]:** Synthesizing authentic [NiCl₂(dpe)]-derived diorganonickel intermediates and testing their reactivity is the core of multiple independent organometallic mechanistic papers published by the Kumada group and others in the mid-1970s; the experiment maps directly onto a published research program.
- **EP-03a [Direct-observation / state-assignment pack]:** In situ ³¹P NMR monitoring of Ni-phosphine species during catalytic turnover and characterization of the black precipitate as Ni⁰ or Ni-phosphine complex are experiments whose results are reported in independent mechanistic papers in the 1974–1985 period.
- **EP-07a [Boundary-test pack]:** Testing sp³ alkyl halides under [NiCl₂(dpe)] conditions to define the sp² scope boundary is an experiment that was performed and published as part of substrate-scope expansion papers; results (with Ni catalysis failing for unactivated sp³ halides and showing β-hydride elimination products) appear in multiple independent reports.
- **EP-04a [Discrimination / replication pack]:** Replicated cis/trans stereochemical ratio measurements for stilbene products using ¹H NMR with coupling constants were performed as part of Kumada group follow-up full papers and independent stereochemical mechanistic studies; these data exist as identifiable publications.

**Tier 2 (indirectly trackable):** EP-09a

- **EP-09a [Internal benchmark pack]:** Direct side-by-side comparison of [NiCl₂(dpe)] vs. Tamura-Kochi Fe catalyst on a shared substrate with cross:homo ratio reporting is not the main subject of any independent paper; it is discussed comparatively in reviews and in early cross-coupling methodology papers that establish Ni's advantages over Fe in passing, but no paper is dedicated solely to this head-to-head comparison.

**Tier 3 (filling may not be found):** EP-08a, EP-01a

- **EP-08a [Replication / uncertainty pack]:** Systematic triplicate replication of Table I entries with disclosed internal standard and response factors for the original glpc yields is not a subject that was re-examined as an independent publication; the field moved to reporting new substrate scope rather than re-validating original yields, and the mixed glpc/isolated yield convention persisted until modern standards emerged gradually.
- **EP-01a [Reporting-coherence pack]:** Disclosure of which characterization method (NMR vs. glpc) was applied to which Table I product is a reporting-format issue resolved partially in the 1974 full paper by Tamao et al. (JACS 1976 / JACS 1974) but not as an independent filling paper; it is an automatic-resolution case in the communication→Full Paper transition.

**EP-06a [Analogy-validation pack] (Tier 1)**

* **RI to be filled**: RI-06 [Imported model]
* **Filling Onset**: Tamao, Sumitani, Kiso, Zembayashi, Fujioka, Kodama, Nakajima, Minato, Kumada, *Journal of the American Chemical Society* 1976 — the Kumada group's full paper (JACS 98, 3384, 1976) included stoichiometric reactions of independently prepared trans-[NiCl(Ph)(dpe)] with PhMgBr to confirm that the (haloorgano)nickel complex 2 undergoes transmetalation with Grignard reagents, directly validating the transmetalation step of the catalytic cycle under conditions closely related to the catalytic system.
* **Filling Establishment**: Yamamoto, Akazawa, Oshima, Utimoto, *Journal of the American Chemical Society* 1978 (and related papers by the Kumada and Kochi groups ca. 1975–1978) — by the late 1970s, the synthesis and reactivity of authentic (dpe)Ni(R)(X) complexes had been independently reported, confirming that the (dipy)Ni analogy for the elementary transmetalation and reductive elimination steps holds for the (dpe)Ni system; the RI-06 analogy-validation is substantially achieved by 1978.
* **Nature of filling**: Partial
  — The full paper validates the transmetalation step (eq 4 in the paper's cycle) and the reductive elimination from diorganonickel species through stoichiometric model reactions, but the homogeneous vs. heterogeneous question (is the black precipitate Ni⁰ the actual catalyst?) was not definitively resolved until later; the cycle-level analogy with (dipy)Ni is confirmed for the core bond-forming steps, but the initiation mechanism (eq 2 → 3) and the R–R homo-coupling byproduct question are addressed only partially.
* **Confidence**: Medium
  — The Kumada 1976 JACS full paper (JACS 98, 3384) is recalled with high confidence as the primary vehicle for stoichiometric validation; the specific experimental content described (isolation and reactivity of trans-[NiCl(Ph)(dpe)]) is consistent with the cited paper's known content, but the precise table of stoichiometric experiments is from recalled knowledge and requires direct verification.
* **Notes**: Self-filling pattern — the original author group (Tamao, Kumada) fills EP-06a in their own 1976 full paper, which is the canonical communication → Full Paper transition for the Kumada coupling. This paper also partially fills EP-03a (in situ reaction monitoring). Cross-reference: the 1976 full paper is the most efficient bundle-filler in this EP set.

**EP-03a [Direct-observation / state-assignment pack] (Tier 1)**

* **RI to be filled**: RI-03 [Unobserved explanatory]
* **Filling Onset**: Kumada, Tamao, Sumitani, *Tetrahedron* 1978 (Tetrahedron 34, 1987, 1978) or the 1976 JACS full paper — the Kumada group's full papers from 1974–1978 describe isolation and characterization of [NiCl(R)(dpe)] and [Ni(R)(R')(dpe)] complexes (intermediates 2 and 3 in the 1972 paper's proposed cycle) and establish their identity by elemental analysis, IR, and NMR, providing the first direct characterization of the catalytic intermediates.
* **Filling Establishment**: Mori, Ikeda, Sato, Kawamoto, *Journal of Organometallic Chemistry* 1975 and related Ni-phosphine mechanistic papers (1975–1982) — characterization of the black precipitate as Ni⁰ nanoparticles or as Ni-phosphine decomposition products was addressed by Henrici-Olivé and Olivé and by Favero et al. in the mid-1970s; TEM/XRD characterization of Ni⁰ colloidal species in catalytic cross-coupling systems appeared in the organometallic catalysis literature ca. 1980.
* **Nature of filling**: Partial
  — Intermediates 2 (L₂Ni(R')(X'')) and 3 (L₂Ni(R)(R')) were characterized as authentic stoichiometric species by the Kumada group (RI-03 partially resolved for catalytically relevant species), but true in situ ³¹P NMR monitoring of intermediates during catalytic turnover in diethyl ether was not performed in the 1970s; that level of direct observation required multinuclear NMR instrumentation improvements of the 1980s–1990s. The heterogeneous Ni⁰ question (U-2) was addressed contextually (black precipitate = catalyst decomposition product under forcing conditions) but not definitively resolved with modern characterization techniques in the original program.
* **Confidence**: Medium
  — The characterization of Ni-phosphine intermediates by the Kumada group in their full papers (1974–1978) is recalled with high confidence; the specific in situ NMR experiment and TEM characterization of the black precipitate are recalled with medium confidence and require literature confirmation.
* **Notes**: Technology-driven filling (partially) — the resolution of RI-03 for in situ catalytic intermediates was technology-limited in 1972–1980; multinuclear in situ NMR characterization of catalytic Ni-phosphine intermediates became accessible with superconducting NMR instrumentation in the 1980s. Cross-reference: the 1976 JACS full paper (cited under EP-06a) is also the primary filling onset for EP-03a.

**EP-07a [Boundary-test pack] (Tier 1)**

* **RI to be filled**: RI-07 [Unsupported boundary]
* **Filling Onset**: Tamao, Kiso, Sumitani, Zembayashi, Fujioka, Kodama, Nakajima, Kumada, *Journal of the American Chemical Society* 1976 (JACS 98, 3384) — the 1976 full paper expanded the substrate scope table to include systematic tests with sp³ alkyl electrophiles, confirming that unactivated sp³ alkyl chlorides (n-alkyl chlorides) give very low yields under [NiCl₂(dpe)] conditions with β-hydride elimination products (alkenes) as the dominant outcome; this directly evidences the sp² boundary.
* **Filling Establishment**: Negishi, Baba, *Journal of the Chemical Society, Chemical Communications* 1976 (JCSCC 596, 1976); Yamamura, Moritani, Murahashi, *Journal of Organometallic Chemistry* 1975 — these papers on Ni- and Pd-catalyzed cross-coupling with sp³ organometallics established that the sp³ electrophile limitation of [NiCl₂(dpe)] is a mechanistic barrier related to β-hydride elimination from the oxidative addition product; by 1976–1977, the mechanistic rationale for the sp² restriction was established in the broader literature, providing the filling for RI-07's negative boundary claim.
* **Nature of filling**: Complete
  — The sp² scope boundary was confirmed experimentally by the Kumada group's own follow-up work and by the broader cross-coupling community's demonstration that sp³ alkyl electrophiles require different catalyst systems (e.g., Pd with bulky phosphines, Fe catalysts, or Ni with specific additives) to overcome β-hydride elimination; the RI-07 boundary is established by both positive (sp³ failure data) and mechanistic (β-hydride elimination explanation) evidence.
* **Confidence**: Medium
  — The failure of sp³ alkyl halides under [NiCl₂(dpe)] conditions is well-established in the Kumada cross-coupling literature; specific paper citations require verification, but the filling content (failed reactions + β-hydride elimination byproducts) is recalled with high confidence as the historically documented outcome.
* **Notes**: Self-filling (partially) by the Kumada group in their full paper(s); independent filling by the broader cross-coupling community who systematically mapped the scope of Ni vs. Pd catalysts for sp² vs. sp³ electrophiles in 1974–1980. This is the fastest-resolved EP: the sp² restriction was evidentially established within 4 years of the 1972 communication.

* **Confidence**: Medium

The 1976 JACS full paper is also a bundle-filler for this EP.

**EP-09a [Internal benchmark pack] (Tier 2)**

* **RI to be filled**: RI-09 [External superiority]
* **Filling Onset**: Tamura, Kochi, *Synthesis* 1971 — this is the prior art paper itself (ref 6 in the 1972 communication); a direct head-to-head comparison of [NiCl₂(dpe)] vs. Tamura-Kochi Fe catalyst on a shared substrate (n-BuMgBr + CH₂=CHCl) with reported cross:homo ratios for both systems is implicit in the comparison that motivated the original paper but is not the main subject of any identifiable independent paper. Partial filling occurs in the Kumada 1976 full paper's discussion section, which compares yields on shared substrates in prose without a formal side-by-side table.
* **Filling Establishment**: Corriu, Masse, *Chemical Communications* 1972; Corriu and Masse's subsequent full paper ca. 1973–1975 — the distinction between [NiCl₂(dpe)] and Ni(acac)₂ (the Corriu-Masse concurrent system) was addressed by the broader cross-coupling literature in reviews and methodology papers that compare ligand requirements; a formal side-by-side yield comparison with cross:homo ratios on a shared substrate is available in review-level sources (Kumada's review articles, 1980s) but not as a dedicated independent paper.
* **Nature of filling**: Indirect
  — The numerical comparison of [NiCl₂(dpe)] with Tamura-Kochi Fe on a shared substrate with cross:homo ratio reporting was never performed as the primary subject of an independent paper; the superiority claim is established indirectly through the broad adoption of Ni-phosphine methods and the field's departure from Fe-based systems, not through a controlled head-to-head experiment. The Corriu-Masse distinction (dpe ligand vs. acac) is addressed in mechanistic reviews but without a dedicated comparative experiment paper.
* **Confidence**: Medium
  — That no dedicated comparative paper exists for [NiCl₂(dpe)] vs. Tamura-Kochi Fe on a shared substrate is recalled with high confidence; that comparative data appear in the 1976 JACS full paper's discussion is recalled with medium confidence.
* **Notes**: Misaligned incentive structure — a dedicated paper comparing [NiCl₂(dpe)] with the Tamura-Kochi Fe catalyst on n-BuMgBr + CH₂=CHCl would have no novelty claim beyond the comparison itself, making it unpublishable under standard publication conventions; this is the canonical case of RI-09 remaining unfilled as an independent paper due to the field's novelty requirement. The filling is partial via the communication→Full Paper transition (discussed but not formally tabulated) and indirect via reviews.

**Selection rationale:**

The recommended list prioritizes the Kumada group's own 1976 full paper (Order 1) as the primary bundle-filler: it addresses EP-06a (stoichiometric validation of cycle intermediates), EP-03a (characterization of intermediates 2 and 3), EP-04a (replicated stereochemical ratios), and EP-01a (complete characterization disclosure) in a single publication, making it the most efficient single paper for EP-filling verification. ### §D Correspondence between VQ hierarchy and filling time order

**Filling time axis:**

- VQ-1 requirement (RI-02 product identity): EP-01a → 1976 [Tamao et al., JACS 1976 full paper] [Self-filling, automatic-resolution]
- VQ-2 requirement (RI-07 scope boundary): EP-07a → 1975–1976 [Yamamura 1975; Negishi 1976] [Self-filling + cross-field filling]
- VQ-3 requirement (RI-06 imported cycle): EP-06a → 1976 [Tamao et al., JACS 1976 full paper] [Self-filling]
- VQ-3 requirement (RI-03 unobserved intermediates): EP-03a → 1976 partial / 1980s establishment [Kumada group + technology-driven filling]
- VQ-3 requirement (RI-04 unreplicated discriminator): EP-04a → 1976–1982 [Kumada group; Hayashi 1982 establishment]
- VQ-4 requirement (RI-09 external superiority): EP-09a → indirect / 1980 [Kumada 1980 review; no dedicated paper]
- VQ-4 requirement (RI-08 replication): EP-08a → **unfilled** [field convention; no dedicated replication paper]

**Evaluation of hierarchy–time correspondence:**

The VQ hierarchy–time hypothesis is supported in broad outline but with an important inversion: the VQ-2 requirement (EP-07a, scope boundary RI-07) was filled at the same time or slightly earlier (1975–1976) than the VQ-3 requirements (EP-06a, EP-03a, EP-04a, 1976–1982), which is inconsistent with the predicted low-VQ-first order. This inversion reflects that the sp² electrophile restriction was a boundary question that the broader cross-coupling community addressed as part of method development, while the VQ-3 mechanistic requirements were addressed by the original author group in their own full paper. The highest-VQ requirements (VQ-4, EP-09a RI-09 and EP-08a RI-08) are precisely those that were never filled as independent papers, consistent with the hypothesis that incentive structures systematically leave high-VQ quantitative benchmarking and replication EPs unfilled.

**Notable patterns:**

- **Self-filling dominant:** The primary mechanism of EP resolution for Kumada 1972 is self-filling — the original author group (Tamao, Kumada, and collaborators) resolved EP-06a, EP-03a, EP-04a, EP-01a, and partially EP-07a in their own follow-up full paper(s). The 1972 communication functioned as a priority stake whose experimental gaps were specifically designed to be filled by the authors' own program.
- **Bundle filling — 1976 JACS full paper:** The Tamao et al. 1976 JACS paper (JACS 98, 3384) fills four EPs simultaneously (EP-06a, EP-03a, EP-04a, EP-01a), making it the single most efficient EP-filling paper and confirming that the communication → Full Paper transition was the intended resolution mechanism for the majority of UQS-visible gaps.
- **Technology-driven filling (EP-03a):** The RI-03 component for in situ observation of catalytic intermediates under actual reaction conditions was partially technology-limited in 1976; the full characterization of Ni-phosphine intermediate evolution during catalytic turnover required improvements in multinuclear NMR instrumentation (1980s superconducting magnets) and, ultimately, modern operando spectroscopic techniques.
- **Incentive-structure inversion (EP-08a, EP-09a):** The two EPs that address quantitative benchmarking and replication (VQ-4 RI-08 and RI-09) remain unfilled as independent papers more than 50 years after the original communication. This is not because the experiments are technically difficult — a triplicate glpc measurement or a parallel Fe-catalyst comparison is trivially feasible — but because they offer no novelty claim. The inversion between the difficulty of the scientific question (low) and the duration of non-filling (>50 years) is direct evidence of the misaligned incentive structure described in Stage 4 §E.
- **Comparison with author retrospective:** Kumada's 1980 *Pure Appl. Chem.* review and subsequent retrospective accounts describe the development of the cross-coupling reaction in terms of: (a) substrate scope expansion, (b) catalyst ligand optimization, (c) stereochemical control in asymmetric variants, and (d) mechanism. The UQS-visible EPs for which no retrospective attention is found are EP-08a (replication) and EP-09a (head-to-head benchmark comparison with Tamura-Kochi). These EPs — which address the paper's own primary selectivity claim quantitatively — are precisely the "structural absences invisible to field insiders" that UQS is designed to detect: Kumada's retrospective confirms the synthetic achievement, the mechanistic development, and the scope expansion, but the original cross:homo selectivity ratio (Table I's central claim) was never quantitatively validated against prior art in any paper by the original group or the broader community.

### §E Heuristic significance of unfilled EPs

**List of unfilled EPs:**

---

**EP-08a [Replication / uncertainty pack]:**

* **Reason for non-filling**: Misaligned incentive structure. Triplicate replication of the ten Table I entries with disclosed glpc calibration parameters is technically trivial by any era's standards — the experiment is standard laboratory practice. The obstacle is that this experiment offers no novel scientific finding beyond confirmation of existing yields; it cannot be published as an independent paper under conventions requiring novelty. The field moved directly from the 1972 communication to expanding substrate scope (new Grignard reagents, new electrophile classes, asymmetric variants) rather than validating original yields. The convention of single-determination yield reporting persisted throughout the 1970s–1980s and was not challenged retrospectively. Additionally, the mixed glpc/isolated yield basis was resolved partially by the 1976 full paper's expanded experimental sections, but without explicit cross-validation of the two methods for matched substrates.
* **Heuristic significance**: Yes. EP-08a represents a 54-year-long structural absence: the yield claims in the paper that established the Kumada cross-coupling — claims that were cited in the Nobel context and in thousands of subsequent applications — were never subjected to replicated measurement with quantified uncertainty. This is not an oversight by any individual but a field-convention gap: the "generally very high yields" claim in the 1972 communication became established fact through citation accumulation rather than through replication. The UQS finding here is that the evidentiary foundation of a field-defining methodology rests entirely on single-determination measurements from a preliminary communication, a gap that is invisible to insiders precisely because the method "worked" in downstream applications — success in application replaced validation of original evidence.
* **Future filling feasibility**: High. Any laboratory with access to the reagents (n-BuMgBr, PhCl, [NiCl₂(dpe)]) and modern GC-MS could replicate all ten Table I entries in triplicate within a single week. The experiment is technically straightforward; the only barrier is the absence of a publication incentive. A replication-focused journal (e.g., *Organic Syntheses*, *Synlett* Synpacts) could host this as a procedures paper with explicit reproducibility validation.

---

**EP-09a [Internal benchmark pack] (partial — independent paper unfilled):**

* **Reason for non-filling**: Misaligned incentive structure (primary) + symmetric comparison difficulty (secondary). A paper dedicated to comparing [NiCl₂(dpe)] with Tamura-Kochi Fe catalyst on n-BuMgBr + CH₂=CHCl under matched conditions would have no novelty claim beyond the comparison. By 1973–1975, the Kumada cross-coupling was already established as superior to Tamura-Kochi in the community's perception (Fe catalysis worked only for alkenyl halides, Ni catalysis worked broadly), making a formal comparison paper redundant in the field's view. The parallel comparison with the Corriu-Masse Ni(acac)₂ system was similarly never formalized: once the Kumada and Corriu-Masse systems were both shown to work, the ligand-specificity question (dpe vs. acac) was addressed mechanistically rather than by controlled yield comparison. The secondary difficulty: after 1972, the field moved to Pd-catalyzed cross-coupling (Heck, Negishi, Suzuki) as the dominant platform, making a retrospective Ni-vs-Fe comparison increasingly anachronistic as a publication target.
* **Heuristic significance**: Yes. The paper's central novelty claim — that [NiCl₂(dpe)] achieves selective cross-coupling where prior Tamura-Kochi methods produced homo-coupling "in substantial amounts" — was never verified by a controlled head-to-head experiment on a shared substrate. This is the specific quantitative gap at the paper's core: the claim "seldom employed in synthetic practice" characterizes prior art qualitatively, and the 1972 Table I data demonstrate that the Ni-dpe system works, but the cross:homo ratio improvement over Fe-catalyzed Grignard coupling was never measured directly. In 54 years, this gap has been filled only by the field's meta-level adoption pattern (Ni replaced Fe in synthetic practice) rather than by experimental measurement. UQS surfaces this as a gap in the paper's own evidence base that field consensus retrospectively assigned as settled without the underlying experiment.
* **Future filling feasibility**: Medium. The Tamura-Kochi Fe catalyst conditions (refs 5–6 in the 1972 paper) are reconstructible from the original papers. The comparison experiment (n-BuMgBr + CH₂=CHCl under [NiCl₂(dpe)] vs. FeCl₃ or Fe(acac)₃ in Et₂O) is technically feasible today. However, the relevance to modern practitioners is low because neither the Fe nor the 1972 Ni-dpe system is used in current synthesis (Pd-catalyzed cross-coupling methods dominate); the filling would be historically valuable but has negligible practical motivation. A replication/history-of-science paper could address this, but the target journal audience is narrow.

---

**VQ-hierarchy inversion diagnosis:**

A systematic inversion between expected and actual filling order is observed. The EP-filling records show:

- **High-VQ EPs (RI-03, RI-06, RI-07) were filled within 4 years** (1975–1976) through self-filling by the original author group and rapid uptake by the broader community.
- **Low-VQ EPs (RI-01, RI-08, RI-09) are either filled only by format transition (RI-01, 1976 full paper) or remain unfilled as independent papers (RI-08, RI-09) for >50 years.**

This inversion — high-VQ mechanistic gaps filled quickly, low-VQ quantitative-reliability and benchmarking gaps never filled — is structurally explained by publication incentives: mechanistic investigations (RI-03, RI-06) and scope demonstrations (RI-07) are publishable as independent contributions, while replication (RI-08) and head-to-head benchmark comparison (RI-09) offer no novelty and cannot be paperized under standard journal conventions. The UQS analysis reveals that the field's evidence base for the Kumada coupling's central selectivity claim (cross:homo ratio improvement over prior art) rests on qualitative assertion rather than measurement — not because the measurement is difficult, but because the incentive structure has not rewarded performing it.
