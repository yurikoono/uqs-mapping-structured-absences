# EP-Filling Selection Map

## Tamao, Sumitani, Kumada 1972 (JACS Communication)

---

### §A Three-tier classification of EP filling difficulty

**Tier 1 (clearly trackable in the literature):** EP-03a, EP-06a, EP-04a, EP-07a, EP-05a

- **EP-03a [Direct-observation / state-assignment pack]**: Independent synthesis and characterization of diorganonickel(II) and haloorganonickel(II) diphosphine intermediates (specifically [NiR₂(dpe)] and [NiR(X)(dpe)]) is precisely the kind of experiment that constitutes the main subject of organometallic synthesis and spectroscopy papers; the RI-03 gap (unobserved intermediates under catalytic conditions) and the U-2 gap (black mixture characterization) are concrete enough targets that independent papers focusing on Ni(II) diphosphine complex synthesis and reactivity would directly fill them.

- **EP-06a [Analogy-validation pack]**: Validation of the (dipy)Ni → (dpe)Ni mechanistic analogy via stoichiometric experiments with independently characterized [NiCl₂(dpe)] intermediates is the kind of experiment that the Kumada group itself would be expected to publish in a follow-up full paper, and that mechanism-focused papers in the Ni cross-coupling area would address directly.

- **EP-04a [Discrimination / replication pack]**: Quantitative determination of cross:homo product ratios in Ni-catalyzed Grignard cross-coupling reactions, together with identification of homo-coupling byproducts, is a tractable analytical experiment that would appear as a central table in a mechanistic or optimization paper on Ni-catalyzed coupling.

- **EP-07a [Boundary-test pack]**: Testing sp³ halide substrates (n-BuCl, BnCl) under [NiCl₂(dpe)] conditions to empirically establish the sp² scope restriction is the kind of substrate-scope experiment that would be the main topic of a paper extending or probing the substrate scope of Ni-catalyzed Grignard cross-coupling.

- **EP-05a [Quantitative-ranking substantiation pack]**: Systematic comparison of catalyst activity for a series of diphosphine and monodentate phosphine Ni complexes with quantitative yield data constitutes a ligand screening study that would be publishable as an independent study on ligand effects in Ni-catalyzed cross-coupling.

**Tier 2 (indirectly trackable):** EP-09a, EP-08a, EP-01a

- **EP-09a [Internal benchmark pack]**: Head-to-head comparison of the Ni-dpe system with Tamura-Kochi (Fe-based) catalysts and Et₂O vs. THF solvent effects on a shared substrate is less likely to be the sole focus of an independent paper and more likely to appear as a comparative section within a broader methodology paper or a review-style benchmark study.

- **EP-08a [Replication / uncertainty pack]**: Replication data (n ≥ 2) with standard deviations for selected Table I entries, and disclosure of glpc internal standard and response factors, is the kind of supporting information that would typically appear in a full-paper supplementary section rather than as the main result of an independent paper; however, it could appear as part of a full-paper expansion.

- **EP-01a [Reporting-coherence pack]**: Per-entry disclosure of the analytical method for yield determination (isolated vs. glpc) and glpc calibration details is fundamentally a reporting-completeness issue that would be resolved in a full-paper expansion by the original author group rather than by an independent paper.

**Tier 3 (filling may not be found):** (none)

All EPs listed in Stage 3 §5 have either Tier 1 or Tier 2 classification, reflecting the concrete experimental content of the case-specific implementations.

### §D Correspondence between VQ hierarchy and filling time order

**Filling time axis:**

- VQ-2 requirement (SRG-5: RI-01, RI-08 — reporting completeness): EP-08a, EP-01a → 1976 [Tamao et al., BCSJ] [self-filling / automatic-resolution pattern]

**Evaluation of hierarchy–time correspondence:**

The VQ hierarchy–time hypothesis is broadly supported: VQ-2 requirements (reporting completeness, ligand quantification) were filled within ~4 years (1976 BCSJ full paper and 1975 boundary testing), VQ-3 requirements (intermediate observation, analogy validation, mechanistic selectivity) were addressed in ~4–12 years (1976–1984), and VQ-4 requirements (prior-art numerical benchmark, definitive sp³ boundary) were either unfilled or filled only after decades. This confirms that the difficulty hierarchy (VQ-2 → VQ-3 → VQ-4) broadly corresponds to the temporal sequence of filling. The primary counterexample is EP-07a (sp² boundary test), which was addressed at the VQ-2 level by independent groups (Yamamura 1975) before the self-filling full paper (BCSJ 1976), suggesting that the scope boundary question attracted independent research interest faster than the reporting-completeness items.

**Notable patterns:**

- **Self-filling dominance**: The majority of EPs (EP-06a, EP-05a, EP-08a, EP-01a, EP-04a) are filled or onset by the Kumada author group itself in the BCSJ 1976 full paper and subsequent Hayashi-Kumada mechanistic papers. This is the expected pattern for a communication that explicitly states [REDACTED: substantial verbatim quotation; see Tier 9]
- **Long-delay filling**: EP-07a (sp³ boundary) and EP-04a (CLA-1: homo-coupling byproduct quantification) show long-delay filling patterns. The sp² restriction imposed by the 1972 paper required ~40–50 years to be definitively overcome experimentally in the sense demanded by EP-07a (with Ni-catalyzed sp³ cross-coupling emerging as a major subfield in 2010–2020 with the Weix and Fu groups). CLA-1 (the R–R homo-coupling byproduct predicted by eq 3) may remain only partially filled as a formal issue: the field accepted the selectivity claim on the basis of product yield data rather than by explicitly quantifying and bounding the predicted homo-coupling byproduct.
- **Bundle filling**: The BCSJ 1976 full paper is the dominant bundle-filler, covering six of the eight EPs either fully or partially. This makes it the single most important EP-filling paper for the 1972 communication.
- **VQ-hierarchy inversion signal**: EP-09a (prior-art numerical comparison, VQ-4 level, low VQ requirement in terms of experimental difficulty) appears to remain unfilled or only partially filled, while EP-03a and EP-06a (VQ-3 level, experimentally demanding intermediate characterization) were filled by the Kumada group within ~4–5 years. This is a partial inversion: the high-VQ mechanistic filling was incentivized by the Kumada group's own research program, while the low-VQ comparative benchmark remained disincentivized by the misaligned incentive structure (no novelty in publishing a head-to-head comparison after the field had accepted Ni superiority over Fe catalysis qualitatively).

**Comparison with author retrospective:**

Tamao (2002, *Journal of Organometallic Chemistry*, retrospective) and Kumada's Nobel-context retrospective accounts (via associated reviews) emphasized: (a) the synthetic utility of the method (Table I yields, β-hydrogen compatibility), (b) the catalyst design principle (bidentate diphosphine superiority), and (c) the subsequent mechanistic work establishing the catalytic cycle. These retrospectives do not explicitly address: (d) the absence of homo-coupling byproduct quantification (CLA-1 / EP-04a), (e) the unverified (dipy)Ni → (dpe)Ni analogy gap (EP-06a), or (f) the sp² restriction without failure examples (EP-07a). The EP-04a and EP-06a gaps — both classified as VQ-3 blocking items in Stage 3 — are invisible in the author retrospective, confirming the UQS finding that "structural absences invisible to field insiders" persist even in retrospective accounts by the discoverers themselves.

### §E Heuristic significance of unfilled EPs

**List of unfilled EPs:**

**EP-09a [Internal benchmark pack]:**
* **Reason for non-filling**: Misaligned incentive structure — a head-to-head numerical comparison of [NiCl₂(dpe)]/Et₂O with Fe(acac)₂/THF on a common substrate (the specific experiment demanded by EP-09a) is technically straightforward and feasible by 1974 technology, but lacks novelty value as an independent publication once the field has accepted the qualitative superiority of the Ni-dpe system. The solvent sub-requirement (Et₂O vs. THF with the same Ni catalyst) faces the same incentive barrier: the comparison appeared in lab notebooks and may appear in the BCSJ 1976 full paper as a footnote, but a systematic quantitative comparison was never the primary result of an independent paper. Additionally, by the time the BCSJ 1976 full paper appeared, the field had already begun moving toward Pd catalysis for cross-coupling (Heck, Negishi, Suzuki reactions emerging 1975–1979), reducing the scientific urgency of fine-grained Ni vs. Fe comparisons.
* **Heuristic significance**: Yes. EP-09a documents a 50-year absence of a formal numerical comparison between the Kumada 1972 Ni-dpe system and the Tamura-Kochi Fe system on a shared substrate — the benchmark comparison that is both the minimum requirement for validating the paper's central superiority claim (SRG-6 / RI-09) and the most structurally simple experiment demanded by any EP in this analysis. Its non-filling illustrates how misaligned publication incentives systematically preserve the weakest link in a landmark paper's claim architecture.
* **Future filling feasibility**: High — the experiment can be performed with current commercial materials and standard GC equipment in ~2–3 days; the only barrier is the absence of publication incentive, which could be overcome in a reproduction/benchmark study framed as historical verification of a foundational cross-coupling result.

**EP-04a (CLA-1 sub-requirement: R–R homo-coupling byproduct from eq 3):**
* **Reason for non-filling**: Misaligned incentive structure combined with field convention — the quantification of homo-coupling byproduct (Et-Et, n-Bu-n-Bu) from the catalytic reaction to verify or refute the prediction of eq 3 was not performed as an independent study, because the field's assessment of the Ni-dpe system's selectivity rested on the absence of reported homo-coupling product rather than on explicit quantification and bounding. The field convention in synthetic catalysis from 1972 onward treated unreported byproducts as implicitly absent, rather than requiring detection-limit statements for predicted mechanistic byproducts. Furthermore, the R–R homo-coupling byproduct question was reframed by subsequent mechanistic work on Ni(0)/Ni(II) cycles (replacing the original Ni(II)/Ni(II) cycle), which changed the mechanistic context in which eq 3's prediction applied.
* **Heuristic significance**: Yes. The self-contradictory absence of homo-coupling byproduct data (CLA-1, identified as the deepest structural tension in the paper by Stage 3) appears to remain partially unfilled 50+ years after publication: the R–R homo-coupling product predicted by eq 3 has not been explicitly quantified and bounded in a paper focused on the original 1972 reaction conditions ([NiCl₂(dpe)], Et₂O, Grignard reagent, sp² halide). This constitutes a formally unresolved internal logical inconsistency in a landmark paper's proposed mechanism that has been invisible to the field because the mechanism itself was revised (Ni(0)/Ni(II) paradigm shift) without the original prediction being explicitly falsified.
* **Future filling feasibility**: High — the experiment (quantitative GC analysis of Et-Et and Ph-Ph homo-coupling products in the crude EtMgBr/PhCl reaction mixture with calibrated authentic standards) is technically trivial by modern standards; the primary barrier is the absence of recognition that this is an open question.
