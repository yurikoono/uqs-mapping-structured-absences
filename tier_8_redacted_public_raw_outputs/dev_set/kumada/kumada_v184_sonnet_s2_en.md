[E] questions_of_scope

**Scope abstraction:**

The paper answered the question: Can nickel-phosphine complexes, operating as catalysts rather than stoichiometric reagents, enable selective formation of cross-coupling products between Grignard reagents and sp² organic halides?

The structural form of this question has three components: (1) a catalytic cycle (not stoichiometric) with a specific metal-ligand architecture; (2) a selectivity problem (cross vs. homo coupling among structurally different partners); (3) a substrate scope claim (sp² electrophiles enabling a bond class not accessible by alternative methods).

**Transformations into questions in other contexts:**

*Transformation A — Palladium/alkene cross-coupling (same structural form):*
Can palladium-phosphine complexes enable selective Heck-type C-C bond formation between organopalladium species and alkenes, suppressing competing β-hydride elimination pathways? This is "the same structure in a different guise" — the selectivity problem (desired cross-coupling pathway vs. an undesired competing pathway), the catalytic cycle architecture (oxidative addition / transmetalation or insertion / reductive elimination), and the sp² substrate constraint all recur. The ligand architecture question (bidentate vs. monodentate; bite angle; electronic tuning) plays the same structural role.

*Transformation B — Nucleophilic cross-coupling by C-H activation (extended structural form):*
Can a metal catalyst enable selective cross-coupling using C-H bonds as the nucleophilic partner (replacing Grignard reagents), and can a bidentate ligand architecture enforce the same cross-selectivity? This shares the selectivity problem and the catalyst architecture question, but relaxes the requirement for a pre-formed organometallic nucleophile. The structural similarity is genuine but the analogy is more distant — it requires different mechanistic steps (C-H activation replacing transmetalation).

*Transformation C — Asymmetric catalysis design principle (abstracted to design form):*
What structural features of a metal-ligand combination (coordination geometry, chelate ring size, electronic asymmetry) determine selectivity between two competing C-C bond-forming pathways from the same intermediate? This is "a figurative similarity" — it draws on the same structural intuition (bidentate ligand enforces cis geometry of two organic groups, enabling selective reductive elimination) but asks the question as a general design principle rather than a substrate-scope demonstration.

*Transformation D — Enzyme active-site selectivity (cross-domain, figurative):*
