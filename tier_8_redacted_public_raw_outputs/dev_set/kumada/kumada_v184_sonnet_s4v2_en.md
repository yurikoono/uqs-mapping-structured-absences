### §A Three-tier classification of EP filling difficulty

**Tier 1 (clearly trackable in the literature):** EP-06a, EP-03a, EP-07a, EP-04a

- **EP-06a** [Analogy-validation pack]: Independent synthesis and mechanistic characterization of dpe-Ni(II) intermediates (diorganonickel and haloorganonickel complexes) is sufficiently concrete to have generated multiple dedicated organometallic synthesis papers in the 1973–1985 period.
- **EP-03a** [Direct-observation / state-assignment pack]: Determination of the active catalyst species (homogeneous Ni(II) vs. heterogeneous Ni⁰) via mercury-drop tests, in-situ spectroscopy, or hot-filtration is a mechanistic question that became the subject of independent papers in the cross-coupling catalyst characterization literature.
- **EP-07a** [Boundary-test pack]: Testing sp³ alkyl halides under [NiCl₂(dpe)] conditions and the discovery of conditions under which sp³ substrates could or could not couple is the central question of a substantial subsequent research program.
- **EP-04a** [Discrimination / replication pack]: Quantification of cross:homo product ratios and the identity of homo-coupling byproducts (especially R–R from the initiation step) is a concrete analytical target that appears in both the Kumada group's follow-up papers and in independent mechanistic studies.

**Tier 2 (indirectly trackable):** EP-09a, EP-05a

- **EP-09a** [Internal benchmark pack]: Direct head-to-head comparison of the Ni-dpe system against Tamura-Kochi Fe and Corriu-Masse Ni(acac)₂ on shared substrates is more likely found in review articles and systematic methodology comparisons than in independent research papers, since the competitive landscape shifted quickly after 1972.
- **EP-05a** [Quantitative-ranking substantiation pack]: Numerical yield values for each catalyst in the ligand activity order (the six-complex ranking) are most likely found in the Kumada group's follow-up full papers as supplementary tables or methodological extensions, rather than as the subject of an independent paper.

**Tier 3 (filling may not be found):** EP-08a, EP-01a

- **EP-08a** [Replication / uncertainty pack]: Replicate yield determinations with standard deviations for Table I entries are a reporting-format issue (RI-08) that was not routinely addressed by independent replication papers; the communication→Full Paper transition partially resolves this but replication of individual yield entries as a study object is not publishable under standard conventions.
- **EP-01a** [Reporting-coherence pack]: Disclosure of the glpc internal standard, response factors, and per-compound analytical method specification is a reporting-format issue (RI-01) resolved only by the Full Paper (if it appeared), not by an independent publication.

**EP-09a [Internal benchmark pack]:**

* **Reason for non-filling**: Paradigm-shift + Misaligned incentive structure. The specific experiment EP-09a requires — running n-BuMgBr + PhCl under Tamura-Kochi Fe conditions in parallel with the Ni-dpe entry from Table I — was technically feasible from 1972 onward and was never performed as an independent study. The reason is two-fold: (1) the paradigm rapidly shifted to Pd-based catalysis (Heck, Negishi, Suzuki) after 1975, eliminating the scientific urgency of the Ni-dpe vs. Fe comparison; and (2) the experiment has negligible novelty value as an independent publication — a head-to-head comparison of two 1972 methods on a 1972 substrate is not publishable in its own right.
* **Heuristic significance**: Yes. This EP is a clean example of the UQS-visible structural absence created by the interaction of publication incentives and paradigm progression: the central comparative claim in the 1972 paper ("prior methods are seldom employed in synthetic practice") was never verified numerically on a common substrate, and this non-verification has persisted for 54 years without attracting scientific attention, because the comparison became scientifically obsolete before the novelty barrier could be overcome.
* **Future filling feasibility**: Medium — technically fully feasible with modern equipment; a motivated research group could run both reactions in one afternoon and obtain the required comparison data. However, the publishability barrier remains: a contemporary paper reporting this comparison would need to embed the historical result in a broader modern context (e.g., a systematic retrospective evaluation of cross-coupling catalyst development, or a computational benchmarking study) to meet novelty requirements.
