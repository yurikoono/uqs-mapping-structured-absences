### §A Three-tier classification of EP filling difficulty

**Tier 1 (clearly trackable in the literature):** EP-06a, EP-07a, EP-04a, EP-03a

- **EP-06a** (Analogy-validation pack): Independently synthesizing L₂NiR₂ (dpe-nickel dialkyl) intermediates and verifying their reactivity is the subject of dedicated organometallic synthesis papers — this experiment type is highly concrete (specific metal-ligand complex, defined reaction with organic halide) and likely exists as the central subject of independent papers from the Kumada group or cognate organometallic laboratories in the 1973–1980 period.

- **EP-07a** (Boundary-test pack): Testing sp³ alkyl halide reactivity in the Ni-dpe system is a natural scope-characterization experiment that multiple groups would have performed upon adopting the Kumada protocol; the result (sp³ halides fail under standard conditions) is concrete enough to be reported as a primary finding in scope-extension papers.

- **EP-04a** (Discrimination / replication pack — stereochemical and selectivity aspects): Cross:homo product ratio determinations and stereospecificity studies in Ni-catalyzed cross-coupling are the type of mechanistic follow-up that generates focused independent publications; the cis:trans stilbene outcome in particular is concrete enough (specific substrates, specific analytical question) to appear as a primary subject in stereomechanistic papers.

- **EP-03a** (Direct-observation / state-assignment pack): Characterization of the active catalyst species and intermediates in Ni-dpe-catalyzed cross-coupling — including the black precipitate identity question — is the main subject of mechanistic organometallic papers; the NMR/X-ray characterization of Ni(II) dialkyl and (halo)(organo)nickel complexes with diphosphine ligands is a tractable experimental question for which independent papers almost certainly exist.

**Tier 2 (indirectly trackable):** EP-05a, EP-09a, EP-01a, EP-08a

- **EP-05a** (Quantitative-ranking substantiation pack): The numerical yield values for the six-catalyst benchmark (footnote 21) may appear in the full-paper sequel rather than as the primary subject of an independent paper; ligand bite-angle / SAR correlations for Ni-dpe analogs are more likely to appear in broader organometallic review papers or as supplementary data in methodology papers.

- **EP-09a** (Internal benchmark pack): Head-to-head comparison of [NiCl₂(dpe)] with Tamura-Kochi Fe-catalyst on a shared substrate is unlikely to appear as the primary subject of a dedicated paper; such comparisons more commonly appear within the introduction or discussion of methodology papers that survey the field.

- **EP-01a** (Reporting-coherence pack — analytical method disclosure): The disclosure of the analytical method for cis:trans ratio determination and glpc calibration data for yield reporting is typically resolved in the full-paper sequel by the same author group, not in an independent paper by a different group.

- **EP-08a** (Replication / uncertainty pack — TON determination): TON and catalyst loading optimization experiments for [NiCl₂(dpe)] are likely reported in optimization-focused papers or as part of larger scope papers; they may not appear as the sole or primary subject of any independent paper.

**Tier 3 (filling may not be found):** None

*All EPs have at least plausible indirect filling candidates. No EP is grounded exclusively in RI-01 or RI-08 as primary RI without any substantive experimental target; the RI-01 aspects of EP-01a are partially filling via EP-01a's Tier 2 route.*

**EP-06a [Analogy-validation pack] (Tier 1)**

* **RI to be filled**: RI-06 [Imported model] (cycle-level import: the entire proposed Ni(II)/Ni(II) catalytic cycle is based on analogy with stoichiometric (dipy)Ni chemistry; validation requires independent synthesis of L₂NiR₂ and L₂Ni(R')(X'') under [NiCl₂(dpe)] conditions and confirmation that these intermediates exhibit the predicted reactivity)

* **Filling Establishment**: Tamao, Kodama, Nakajima, Kumada, Minato, Suzuki — a comprehensive mechanistic paper by the Kumada group establishing the Ni(0)/Ni(II) catalytic cycle (rather than the Ni(II)/Ni(II) cycle proposed in 1972) would constitute the decisive paradigm-shift filling of EP-06a. The recognition that [NiCl₂(dpe)] first reacts with 2 equivalents of RMgX to give L₂NiR₂ (intermediate 1), which then undergoes reductive elimination to give Ni(0) + R–R, followed by oxidative addition of R'X to give L₂Ni(0)→L₂Ni(R')(X'') — this Ni(0)/Ni(II) cycle rather than the Ni(II)/Ni(II) cycle of eqs 2–4 — was established through the work of the Kumada group and Stille's group in the late 1970s and early 1980s. [tentative_citation: possibly Tamao, Sumitani, Kiso, Zembayashi, Fujioka, Kodama, Nakajima, Kumada, *Bull. Chem. Soc. Jpn.* 49, 1958 (1976); exact citation uncertain for the mechanistic paradigm-shift paper]

* **Nature of filling**: Paradigm-shift — the Ni(0)/Ni(II) cycle that emerged from 1975–1985 research does not validate the Ni(II)/Ni(II) cycle of eqs 2–4 but replaces it; this means EP-06a is filled not by demonstrating isomorphism between (dipy)Ni stoichiometric chemistry and [NiCl₂(dpe)] catalytic chemistry (the EP's original framing) but by establishing a fundamentally different mechanistic cycle. The R–R byproduct predicted by eq 3 (CLA-1) is reframed: in the Ni(0)/Ni(II) cycle, R–R is produced during the induction phase (2 RMgX + [NiCl₂(dpe)] → L₂NiR₂ → Ni(0) + R–R) but not during the propagation cycle — which resolves CLA-1 (the self-contradictory absence of R–R in Table I).

* **Confidence**: Medium — confident that the mechanistic paradigm shift from Ni(II)/Ni(II) to Ni(0)/Ni(II) occurred in the literature; uncertain about which specific paper first established the Ni(0)/Ni(II) cycle for the [NiCl₂(dpe)] system as the definitive filling event.

* **Notes**: EP-06a is the most important EP in the entire set from the perspective of scientific-historical significance. The paradigm-shift from Ni(II)/Ni(II) to Ni(0)/Ni(II) reframes all five mechanistic features of the 1972 paper: feature 3 (cis geometry of intermediate 3) becomes a statement about the reductive elimination geometry from L₂Ni(R)(R') rather than the proposed diorganonickel-II intermediate reacting with R'X''. The R–R byproduct predicted by eq 3 is re-sited to the induction phase (catalyst activation, not propagation), which simultaneously resolves CLA-1 and U-1. Cross-references: EP-03a (active species identity), EP-04a (stereochemical outcome mechanistic basis). The RI-06 scope note (cycle-level import) is maximally exemplified here: the entire proposed Ni(II)/Ni(II) cycle is not just partially wrong but is replaced by a different metal-oxidation-state trajectory.

**EP-07a [Boundary-test pack] (Tier 1)**

* **RI to be filled**: RI-07 [Unsupported boundary] (the sp² electrophile restriction "Only C_{sp²} halides can be used" is asserted without negative-result evidence from any sp³ or allyl halide experiment)

* **Filling Onset**: Tamao, Sumitani, Kumada full paper (ca. 1976) — a scope-defining experiment testing sp³ alkyl chlorides under [NiCl₂(dpe)] conditions would be the natural inclusion in the full paper; the failure of n-BuCl or allyl-Cl to couple would constitute the minimal filling. [tentative_citation: Tamao, Sumitani, Kumada, *Bull. Chem. Soc. Jpn.* 49, 1958 (1976) — citation uncertain, pages tentative]

* **Filling Establishment**: Multiple laboratories (Kumada, Corriu, Negishi, Murahashi groups) — the sp³ halide restriction in Ni-catalyzed Grignard cross-coupling was established empirically through the collective failure to couple primary alkyl chlorides/bromides under standard [NiCl₂(dpe)] conditions; scope papers from 1974–1980 cumulatively establish that sp³ C–Cl bonds do not undergo oxidative addition to [NiCl₂(dpe)] under the conditions of Table I. Murahashi, Yamamura, Mita, Kondo (*J. Org. Chem.* 1979) demonstrated that changing the metal (Fe vs. Ni) or ligand framework enables sp³ coupling, which indirectly validates the sp² restriction by demonstrating the catalyst-dependence of the selectivity boundary.

* **Nature of filling**: Complete — the sp³ halide restriction is well-established in the cross-coupling literature through both direct (failed reaction reports) and indirect (mechanistic rationalization via oxidative addition selectivity) evidence. The restriction is confirmed at the level that VQ-2 (qualified) is satisfied: the scope boundary is both asserted and explained mechanistically (sp³ C–Cl oxidative addition disfavored vs. sp² at Ni(0)).

* **Confidence**: High — the sp³ halide restriction in Ni-dpe-catalyzed cross-coupling is a well-established feature of the cross-coupling literature that has been confirmed by multiple groups; the filling papers may not have been written specifically to confirm EP-07a, but the empirical record is clear.

* **Notes**: The paradigm-shift in mechanistic understanding (Ni(0)/Ni(II) cycle) provides a retrospective rationalization for EP-07a: sp² C–X bonds undergo oxidative addition to Ni(0) much more readily than sp³ C–X bonds, due to the lower activation energy for oxidative addition at sp² carbon (accessible π-complex transition state). This mechanistic grounding was not available in 1972 but fills EP-07a's RI-07 (unsupported boundary → supported boundary with mechanistic rationale) post-hoc. The allyl halide borderline case was explored by Kumada and others: allyl chloride under certain conditions undergoes coupling, constituting a partial exception to the "only sp²" restriction and indicating that the boundary is substrate-conditional rather than absolute.

**Evaluation of hierarchy–time correspondence:**

The hierarchy–time hypothesis — "higher VQ requirements take longer to fill" — is partially supported for the Kumada 1972 case. VQ-2 requirements (EP-01a, EP-07a) are filled by 1976 (4 years), primarily through the Kumada group's own full paper (self-filling). VQ-3 requirements (EP-03a, EP-04a, EP-05a, EP-06a) begin to be addressed in 1975–1977 but are not fully established until 1979–1982 (7–10 years), with the paradigm-shift filling of EP-06a representing the longest track within VQ-3. VQ-4 requirements (EP-08a, EP-09a) remain unfilled or only indirectly filled through distributed evidence, and this pattern persists past 2026 — consistent with the hierarchy–time hypothesis for the VQ-3→VQ-4 transition. However, the hypothesis is complicated by paradigm-shift dynamics: EP-06a (VQ-3) is not filled by validation of the original Ni(II)/Ni(II) cycle but by replacement of the entire framework, which means the EP's premise changes before it is "filled.[REDACTED: substantial verbatim quotation; see Tier 9]filling" and "paradigm shift" are distinct outcomes.

**Notable patterns:**

- **Self-filling (Papers 1, 3):** The Kumada group fills EP-01a, EP-07a (onset), and EP-04a (stereochemical dimension) through their own subsequent full paper and dedicated stereospecificity study. This is the most common filling pattern for communication-format papers in organometallic chemistry.

- **Paradigm-shift filling (EP-06a):** The proposed Ni(II)/Ni(II) catalytic cycle of eqs 2–4 is not validated but replaced by the Ni(0)/Ni(II) cycle in the 1979–1982 period. This simultaneously resolves CLA-1 (R–R absence explained: R–R is produced during catalyst activation from [NiCl₂(dpe)], not during propagation), partially resolves EP-03a (the black precipitate is Ni(0) generated during activation), and reframes the mechanistic conclusions about intermediate 3 geometry (feature 3 of the paper). Paradigm-shift filling is the signature filling pattern for foundational communication papers that propose mechanistic hypotheses ahead of available evidence.

- **Technology-driven filling (EP-03a, EP-05a):** The active-species question (EP-03a) and the SAR quantification (EP-05a) are eventually addressed through technologies not available in 1972: multinuclear NMR (¹³C, ³¹P) for organometallic intermediate characterization; X-ray crystallography for diphosphine-Ni complex structures; DFT calculations for bite-angle / reductive elimination energy correlations.

- **Long-delay filling (EP-06a, EP-03a):** EP-06a's establishment (Ni(0)/Ni(II) paradigm) is at 7–10 years; EP-03a's definitive characterization of the Ni(0) nanoparticle vs. molecular Ni(0) question is arguably still incompletely resolved at the level the 1972 communication's specific reaction mixture is concerned. These qualify as long-delay fillings (>5 years for establishment, >20 years for definitive characterization with modern techniques).

- **Misaligned incentive structure (EP-08a, EP-09a):** The TON determination and head-to-head benchmark comparison are technically feasible but were never performed as primary subjects of independent publications, because (a) the Kumada coupling was rapidly superseded by Pd-catalyzed couplings for aryl halides, and (b) the scientific community did not require explicit TON curves for cross-coupling catalysts as a standard datum in the 1970s–1980s. This is an inversion pattern: VQ-4 EPs (RI-08, RI-09) remain unfilled not because of technical difficulty but because publication incentives prioritized new reactions over benchmarking established ones.

- **Comparison with author retrospective:** The authors' subsequent publications and reviews (Kumada's retrospective accounts of the Kumada coupling; Hayashi's accounts of asymmetric cross-coupling; Tamao's later work on the Tamao-Fleming oxidation and organosilane chemistry) emphasize the synthetic utility of the coupling and the ligand design principles (feature 3) as the key contributions. The mechanistic cycle (eqs 2–4) is not prominently featured in retrospective accounts — consistent with the UQS finding that the mechanistic proposal was the weakest pillar of the 1972 paper. The stereochemical observation (feature 4: anomalous cis enrichment from both cis and trans-DCE) receives less retrospective attention than its mechanistic significance warrants, which is consistent with EP-04a and EP-06a being the most historically productive questions in the paper's gap structure.

### §E Heuristic significance of unfilled EPs

**List of unfilled EPs:**

**EP-08a [Replication / uncertainty pack]:**

* **Reason for non-filling**: Misaligned incentive structure. A dedicated TON determination paper for [NiCl₂(dpe)] in the n-BuMgBr + m-DCB system of Example 1 does not exist as an independent publication because (a) once the Kumada coupling was validated through widespread synthetic application, the TON question was operationally answered by use rather than by explicit measurement; (b) the scientific community's primary interest shifted to Pd-catalyzed couplings, making Ni-dpe TON optimization a secondary priority; (c) the catalyst loading range (10⁻²–10⁻³ mol fraction) is high by modern standards, making TON > 100 a modest claim that would not support an independent publication in the modern cross-coupling literature; (d) the Ni(0)/Ni(II) paradigm shift (EP-06a) reframed the precatalyst activation efficiency question, making the simple TON of [NiCl₂(dpe)] a precatalyst-efficiency question rather than a catalytic-cycle efficiency question.

* **Heuristic significance**: Yes. EP-08a represents the class of "legitimacy-establishing data" whose absence from a founding paper is systematically not filled by the subsequent literature because the community accepts the method's utility through widespread use rather than through formal efficiency certification. The TON gap in the Kumada 1972 paper remains visible in the UQS framework as evidence that "useful preparative method" claims were accepted on qualitative grounds in the founding period of cross-coupling catalysis — a field-level convention that persisted until the 2010s, when the cross-coupling community began requiring explicit TON and turnover frequency (TOF) data for new catalysts.

* **Future filling feasibility**: High — a TON determination for [NiCl₂(dpe)] in the Example 1 conditions (n-BuMgBr + m-DCB, diethyl ether, reflux) is technically straightforward by modern methods; catalyst loading can be varied from 0.001 mol% to 1 mol% with GC-MS product quantification. However, this experiment has no independent publication incentive in the current literature; it would need to be embedded in a historical verification study or methodology comparison paper.

**EP-09a [Internal benchmark pack]:**

* **Reason for non-filling**: Misaligned incentive structure combined with lack of interest (field evolution). The specific head-to-head experiment — n-BuMgBr + PhCl under Tamura-Kochi Fe-catalyst conditions vs. [NiCl₂(dpe)] conditions in the same laboratory — was never published because (a) the comparison was made obsolete by the rapid development of Pd-catalyzed Suzuki, Negishi, and Heck couplings, which displaced both the Kumada coupling and the Tamura-Kochi system for aryl electrophiles; (b) the Corriu-Masse Ni(acac)₂ system also rapidly faded in the face of Pd catalysis; (c) the scientific community retrospectively validated the Kumada coupling's superiority through widespread adoption, not through explicit benchmarking. The specific comparison with the Tamura-Kochi system is further complicated by the fact that the Tamura-Kochi system targets alkyl halides (with Fe catalyst) rather than aryl chlorides, making the comparison chemically heterogeneous.

* **Heuristic significance**: Yes. EP-09a is a UQS-visible structural absence that reflects a recurring field-level convention: in the founding period of a new method, competitive advantage over prior methods is established through qualitative contrast language and subsequent adoption, not through explicit comparative benchmarking. The absence of a head-to-head Kumada vs. Tamura-Kochi comparison is evidence that the cross-coupling field's founding papers were accepted on the basis of demonstrated utility (Table I yields) rather than on demonstrated superiority (comparative yield + selectivity on a shared substrate). This convention is now recognized as a limitation in modern cross-coupling literature reviews, where comparative reaction scope tables are expected.

* **Future filling feasibility**: Medium — a head-to-head comparison of [NiCl₂(dpe)] and Tamura-Kochi Fe catalyst (from 1971, which is not commercially available as a defined catalyst) is technically possible using the original conditions, but the Tamura-Kochi catalyst system definition is ambiguous enough (soluble Fe in THF, from specific refs 5–6) that reproducing it requires accessing 1971 experimental details. A modern Fe-catalyzed cross-coupling system (e.g., Fürstner's aryl chloride Fe couplings) could serve as a partial analog but would not be the historical comparison EP-09a requires.
