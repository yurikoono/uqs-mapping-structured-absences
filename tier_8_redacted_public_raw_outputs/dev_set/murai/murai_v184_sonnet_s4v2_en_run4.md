### §A Three-tier classification of EP filling difficulty

**Tier 1 (clearly trackable in the literature):** EP-03a, EP-04a, EP-06a, EP-07a

- **EP-03a [Direct-observation / state-assignment pack]**: Stoichiometric characterization of the cyclometallated Ru intermediate and identification of the active Ru(0) species are concrete mechanistic experiments that became the subject of independent organometallic publications after 1993; the Kakiuchi/Chatani group and multiple independent groups pursued exactly this question as follow-up research to the landmark Murai 1993 paper.
- **EP-04a [Discrimination / replication pack]**: Kinetic isotope effect measurements (kH/kD) and time-resolved yield profiles for Ru-catalyzed C–H functionalization are standard kinetic experiments that would be reported as independent mechanistic papers; the discrimination of rate-determining step in C–H activation is a recurring subject in organometallic kinetics literature from 1993 onward.
- **EP-06a [Analogy-validation pack]**: Identification of Ru(0) aromatic C–H oxidative addition precedents and bridging of the Mn(I)/Ru(II) stoichiometric analogy to the proposed catalytic mechanism is a concrete mechanistic question that organometallic chemists would address in independent mechanistic papers on Ru C–H activation.
- **EP-07a [Boundary-test pack]**: Expansion of the Murai reaction substrate scope beyond aromatic ketones to aldehydes, esters, amides, imines, and other directing groups is a clearly trackable experimental question; dozens of independent papers systematically extended the directing-group scope from 1994 onward.

**Tier 2 (indirectly trackable):** EP-01a, EP-05a, EP-09a

- **EP-01a [Reporting-coherence pack]**: GC calibration methodology improvement and isolated yield reporting for all runs are format-convention issues most likely resolved in the Kakiuchi/Chatani group's own full-paper expansion of the original communication; may appear as internal supplementary matter in a subsequent full JACS paper rather than as a standalone analytical publication.
- **EP-05a [Quantitative-ranking substantiation pack]**: Quantified regioselectivity ratios and olefin mass balance accounting are more likely to appear as part of a mechanistic or synthetic full paper than as a standalone quantitative methodology paper; partial filling is expected through substrate-scope expansion papers that report regioisomer identification by NMR.
- **EP-09a [Internal benchmark pack]**: Numerical TOF comparison between the Murai system and refs 11–12 (Jordan/Taylor; Moore et al.) is most likely to appear in review articles or mechanistic overview papers rather than as an independent experimental paper, since the reaction systems use different metals and substrates; a dedicated benchmark comparison on a common substrate is unlikely to have been published as a standalone paper.

**Tier 3 (filling may not be found):** EP-08a

- **EP-08a [Replication / uncertainty pack]**: Replicate yield measurements (n ≥ 3) with standard deviations for Murai 1993's substrate scope table are a reporting-convention issue (RI-01/RI-08) that was not customarily resolved in independent papers; replication of GC yields for the exact Table 1 conditions was never the subject of an independent publication, and the field accepted GC yields as reported; this is a misaligned-incentive-structure case where filling is technically feasible but unpublishable under novelty-demanding conventions.

### §D Correspondence between VQ hierarchy and filling time order

**Filling time axis:**

- **VQ-2 requirement (RI-01, RI-07, RI-05):** EP-01a + EP-07a → 1995 [Kakiuchi, Murai et al., *Bull. Chem. Soc. Jpn.* 1995] [Self-filling] — The full-paper expansion auto-resolves the GC methodology and characterisation compression issues within 2 years of the original publication; the scope-expansion component (RI-07) begins here and continues as partial fills through ~2005.
- **VQ-3 requirement (RI-03, RI-06):** EP-03a + EP-06a → 1997 [Matsubara, Morokuma et al., *Organometallics* 1997] [Technology-driven filling: computational MO methods] — Intermediate characterization and analogy validation achieved computationally rather than by stoichiometric experimental observation; the computational study fills the RI-03/RI-06 gaps at VQ-3 but via a different experimental modality than EP-03a originally specified (computation vs. ³¹P{¹H} NMR / X-ray).
- **VQ-4 requirement (RI-08, RI-09):** EP-08a + EP-09a → **unfilled / not identified** — No paper reporting replicate GC yields with standard deviations or numerical TOF benchmarking against refs 11–12 on a common substrate basis is identifiable; VQ-4 remains effectively unachieved in the primary literature.

**Evaluation of hierarchy–time correspondence:**

The hierarchy–time hypothesis is broadly supported for VQ-2 and VQ-3: the VQ-2 auto-resolution (RI-01, RI-07) occurred within 2 years (1993 → 1995) via the communication→Full Paper self-filling pattern, while the VQ-3 mechanistic requirements (RI-03, RI-06) required 4 years (1993 → 1997) and new computational technology (ab initio MO methods with transition-state optimization). This ordering is consistent with the hierarchy–time hypothesis. However, VQ-4 has not been achieved and shows no sign of impending achievement, suggesting the hierarchy is not uniformly followed: the reporting-convention and replication requirements (RI-08) at VQ-4 are structurally unfillable under publication incentive conventions, creating a permanent inversion where the methodologically simpler requirements (VQ-4's RI-08/RI-09) remain unfilled while the scientifically deeper requirements (VQ-3's RI-03/RI-06) were filled by motivated research programs.

**Notable patterns:**

- **Self-filling (VQ-2):** The Kakiuchi/Murai/Chatani group self-filled EP-01a, EP-04a, EP-05a, and EP-07a through their own full-paper series — the most concentrated self-filling pattern visible in Murai-reaction Stage 4 analysis.
- **Technology-driven filling (VQ-3):** EP-03a and EP-06a were filled computationally (Matsubara/Morokuma 1997) rather than by stoichiometric experimental characterisation of the proposed intermediate. This is a technology-driven filling in which ab initio MO methods (not available in 1993 at the required level of computational detail) provided the intermediate characterisation that the experimental approach had not yet achieved. The computational filling satisfies the VQ-3 requirement at the level of "theoretical validation of intermediate existence" rather than "experimental isolation and NMR/X-ray confirmation," creating a Partial rather than Complete filling of EP-03a in the strictest sense.
- **Long-delay filling (EP-07a scope):** The complete empirical grounding of the "variety of aromatic systems" claim required approximately 10 years of directed scope-expansion work (1993–2003), constituting a long-delay partial filling distributed across many papers.
- **Unfilled (VQ-4):** EP-08a (replication) and EP-09a (numerical TOF benchmark) remain unfilled; this constitutes VQ-hierarchy inversion (see §E).
- **Paradigm-shift relevance:** The emergence of Pd-catalyzed directed C–H functionalization from ~2005 onward (Daugulis, Glorius, Fagnou, and others) partially reframed the Murai reaction's VQ context — the Ru-specific mechanistic questions became less urgently pursued as Pd became the dominant metal for preparative C–H functionalization. Some EP-filling that might have occurred for Ru-specific systems instead occurred for Pd analogs.

### §E Heuristic significance of unfilled EPs

**List of unfilled EPs:**

**EP-08a [Replication / uncertainty pack]:**
* **Reason for non-filling**: Misaligned incentive structure + field convention. Reporting replicate GC yields (n ≥ 3) with standard deviations for the Murai 1993 substrate scope table is technically feasible with off-the-shelf analytical chemistry: any laboratory equipped with a GC could perform n = 3 replicate runs for each of the 13 substrate combinations. However, such a study would constitute pure replication with no new substrate, no new catalyst, no new directing group, and no new mechanistic insight — and would be unpublishable under the novelty criteria of synthetic chemistry journals. The field convention of accepting GC yields as reported, without demanding replicate confirmation, was established in the 1993 era and has not been systematically challenged for landmark historical papers.
* **Heuristic significance**: Yes. EP-08a represents a case where the quantitative reliability of the efficiency, selectivity, and generality claims in one of the most highly cited synthetic methodology papers of the 1990s (>4,000 citations) has never been independently confirmed by replicate measurement. The "bilateral quantitative yields" claim and the "virtually 100% regioselectivity" claim rest on single-point GC determinations for each run, with isolated yields provided for only 3 of 13 runs, and no uncertainty quantification for any run. This is a structural absence that UQS renders visible: the field's citation network treats Murai 1993's GC yields as established facts without their having been independently replicated. The significance is not that the results are incorrect — subsequent application papers confirm the reaction's generality — but that the precision and reproducibility of the quantitative claims have never been audited.
* **Future filling feasibility**: High. The replicate yield measurements require only access to the original substrates (commercially available: acetophenone, β-tetralone, acetopyridine, triethoxyvinylsilane) and RuH2(CO)(PPh3)3 (commercially available), a calibrated GC, and standard laboratory conditions. The cost of the experiment is negligible. The barrier to filling is purely institutional (unpublishability under novelty conventions), not technical. A formal replication study or an independent preprint could fill this gap immediately and would represent high scientific value, particularly given the paper's paradigmatic status.

---

**EP-09a [Internal benchmark pack]:**
* **Reason for non-filling**: Misaligned incentive structure + chemical incomparability. The numerical TOF comparison required by EP-09a demands that the Murai 1993 system (Ru, aromatic ketone, terminal silylolefin, toluene, 135 °C) be compared with refs 11 (Jordan/Taylor: Zr, α-picoline, propene, conditions unspecified) and 12 (Moore et al.: Ru, pyridine, CO, 1-hexene) on a common substrate basis. These systems are not chemically interconvertible: neither the Jordan/Taylor nor Moore et al. system can accept aromatic ketone substrates, and the Murai system has not been reported to function with pyridine or α-picoline substrates under the original conditions. A common-substrate TOF comparison is therefore experimentally impossible without developing new catalyst/substrate combinations, which would constitute new research rather than a benchmark paper. The "highest reported TOF" claim was therefore effectively rendered unverifiable by the chemical specificity of each system.
* **Heuristic significance**: Yes. The unverifiability of the "highest reported TOF" claim in Murai 1993 is a structural absence of a specific type: the comparative efficiency claim was asserted in a context where no common experimental basis for verification was available or feasible, and the field accepted the claim on the authority of the paper's other results rather than on quantitative grounds. This pattern — superlative efficiency claims in communications without verifiable numerical basis — is recurring in organometallic catalysis, and Murai 1993 provides a historically significant example of how such claims propagate through citation networks without ever being numerically audited.
* **Future filling feasibility**: Low. The chemical incompatibility between the substrate systems makes a common-substrate TOF comparison infeasible without fundamental redesign. Contemporary DFT-calculated reaction energy profiles could provide a theoretical basis for comparing barrier heights across the three systems (Zr/α-picoline, Ru/pyridine/CO, Ru/aromatic ketone) at a mechanistic level, which would constitute an indirect filling of EP-09a — but this would be a Tier 2 / Indirect filling rather than the direct experimental comparison the EP requires.
