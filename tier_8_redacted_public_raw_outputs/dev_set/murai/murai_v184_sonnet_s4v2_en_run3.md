# EP-Filling Selection Map

## Murai et al. 1993 (Nature, Articles)

---

### §A Three-tier classification of EP filling difficulty

**Tier 1 (clearly trackable in the literature):** EP-03a, EP-06a, EP-04a, EP-07a

- **EP-03a [Direct-observation / state-assignment pack]** — The stoichiometric isolation or spectroscopic observation of a ruthenacyclic intermediate, and the confirmation of Ru(0) active-species formation, are mechanistic experiments sufficiently concrete and experimentally central to appear as independent papers in organometallic chemistry journals.
- **EP-06a [Analogy-validation pack]** — Structural comparison of the proposed cyclometallated intermediate with stoichiometric Mn(I)/Ru(II) analogues from refs 14 and 15 involves well-defined organometallic synthesis and characterisation; this type of mechanistic analogy validation is routinely published as an independent paper in Inorganic Chemistry or Organometallics.
- **EP-04a [Discrimination / replication pack]** — The kinetic isotope effect (kH/kD) experiment with ortho-deuterated acetophenone is a standard organometallic mechanism experiment; it is concrete enough (specific substrate, specific measurement target) to constitute the central experiment of an independent mechanistic paper.
- **EP-07a [Boundary-test pack]** — Testing directing-group scope beyond aromatic ketones is a substrate-scope expansion study; in directed C–H functionalization chemistry, scope expansions to new directing groups are consistently published as independent communications or articles.

**Tier 2 (indirectly trackable):** EP-05a, EP-09a

- **EP-05a [Quantitative-ranking substantiation pack]** — Mechanistic resolution of 1:1 vs. 1:2 selectivity control (steric vs. mass-action) is an experiment that may appear within a broader mechanistic or scope paper rather than as a standalone publication; it is detectable but the experiment may be embedded rather than leading.
- **EP-09a [Internal benchmark pack]** — Systematic numerical comparison of TON/TOF for the Murai system against refs 11 (Jordan & Taylor 1989) and 12 (Moore et al. 1992) on a common substrate is more likely to appear in a review or a benchmark-focused methodology paper than as a standalone paper; filling may be indirect.

**Tier 3 (filling may not be found):** EP-01a, EP-08a

- **EP-01a [Reporting-coherence pack]** — GC calibration details, full isolated yield tables, and TOF column data are format-driven omissions (RI-01) that are most naturally resolved in the Full Paper expansion by the original author group; the probability that an independent paper addresses GC calibration methodology for the Murai system specifically is low.
- **EP-08a [Replication / uncertainty pack]** — Catalyst recyclability and replicate yield measurements with error bars (RI-08) are not the subject of independent papers under normal publication conventions; these may be partially addressed in a Full Paper but are not independently paperizable.

**EP-05a [Quantitative-ranking substantiation pack] (Tier 2)**

* **RI to be filled**: RI-05 [Qualitative overread]

The case-specific implementation requires: a competition experiment distinguishing steric inhibition from mass-action kinetics as the mechanism of 1:1 vs. 1:2 selectivity control (add isolated 1:1 adduct to fresh olefin under run 9 conditions and test whether 1:2 product forms).

Search terms derived: [REDACTED: substantial verbatim quotation; see Tier 9]; "ruthenium ketone olefin coupling double insertion selectivity"; "directed C–H functionalization double coupling stoichiometry control steric"

The 1:1/1:2 selectivity question in the 1993 paper arises from runs 1 and 2: under standard equimolar conditions (run 1: ketone/olefin = 2/2 mmol), both 1:1 (75%) and 1:2 (8%) coupling products are observed, whereas using 6 equiv. olefin with extended reaction time (run 2: ketone/olefin = 2/6 mmol, 90 h) drives selective 1:2 product formation (94%). The paper attributes the 1:1 selectivity under equimolar conditions to [REDACTED: substantial verbatim quotation; see Tier 9] This steric attribution is mechanistically distinct from a mass-action (kinetic dilution) explanation: under mass-action kinetics, using excess olefin drives the equilibrium toward 1:2 product by Le Chatelier's principle, without invoking steric blocking at all. The competition experiment specified in EP-05a (add isolated 1:1 adduct to fresh olefin under run 9 conditions) directly discriminates between these: if the 1:1 adduct is converted to the 1:2 product, steric blocking is absent (mass-action applies); if it is not converted, steric blocking is confirmed. **Evaluation of hierarchy–time correspondence:**

The hierarchy–time hypothesis ("higher VQ level requires longer filling time") is supported in the Murai 1993 case with one notable inversion. The VQ-3 mechanistic requirements (EP-03a, EP-04a, EP-06a) were filled across 1995–2001 (2–8 year interval), consistently later than the VQ-2 reporting requirements (EP-01a, 1995). The VQ-4 benchmark requirement (EP-09a) presents the inversion: the filling *data* existed in the cited primary literature (refs 11, 12) *before* the 1993 paper was published. The paper's failure to assemble these data into a numerical comparison is not a consequence of the data being unavailable; it is a consequence of the reporting convention that accepted qualitative comparisons. EP-09a is therefore a case where VQ-4 filling data existed at time-zero but were never assembled, creating a permanent inversion in the expected VQ-hierarchy-time sequence.

**Notable patterns:**

- **Self-filling (dominant pattern):** Five of seven papers in the selection list are from the Murai/Kakiuchi/Chatani group at Osaka University. This is characteristic of a research group that recognised the 1993 Nature communication as a platform for a sustained mechanistic and scope program. The self-filling rate is high because the Murai group continued active research on this system for more than a decade.
