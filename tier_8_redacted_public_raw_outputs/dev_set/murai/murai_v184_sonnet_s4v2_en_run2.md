### §A Three-tier classification of EP filling difficulty

- **EP-04a** [Discrimination / replication pack]: KIE experiments using deuterated aromatic substrates and regioselectivity detection-limit studies are well-defined experiments that appeared as targeted mechanistic papers; the Murai reaction's popularity guaranteed that discrimination experiments were published independently.
- **EP-06a** [Analogy-validation pack]: DFT computational validation of the Ru(0) OA cyclometallated intermediate versus the Mn(I)/Ru(II) stoichiometric precedents is a concrete computational study type that multiple groups have published.

- **EP-08a** [Replication / uncertainty pack]: Time-resolved yield data and duplicate runs for turnover-frequency determination are straightforward and are the kind of data typically included in the follow-up Full Paper from the same group.
- **EP-09a** [Internal benchmark pack]: Numerical TOF comparison with refs 11 (Jordan & Taylor) and 12 (Moore et al.) on the Zr/α-picoline and Ru/pyridine systems is concrete enough that both the original authors and subsequent reviewers could provide the comparison.

**Tier 2 (indirectly trackable):** EP-01a, EP-05a

- **EP-01a** [Reporting-coherence pack]: GC calibration methodology, internal standard specification, and isolated yields for all 13 runs are the kind of data typically supplied in a Full Paper extension rather than an independent study; they are trackable indirectly through the Kakiuchi/Murai Full Paper series rather than through independent publications from other groups.

**Tier 3 (filling may not be found):** (none)

No EP was assigned Tier 3. All EPs have either a concrete experimental target (Tier 1) or an indirect route through full papers and mechanistic surveys (Tier 2). The paper's EPs arise predominantly from RI-03, RI-06, RI-07, RI-05, RI-09, and RI-01 combinations — none of the pure RI-01/RI-08 reporting-format Tier-3 defaults applies cleanly here, because the paper's format-driven omissions (SRG-1, CLA-2) were structurally resolved by the Kakiuchi et al. 1993 Full Paper and subsequent systematic papers.

### §E Heuristic significance of unfilled EPs

**List of unfilled EPs:**

EP-05a [Quantitative-ranking substantiation pack]:
* **Reason for non-filling**: Misaligned incentive structure — The specific experiment required (time-resolved yield profiles for α-tetralone (run 10) vs. benzocycloheptanone (run 11) under identical conditions, reporting kobs ratio) generates insufficient novelty to publish as an independent paper. The rate difference between runs 10 and 11 (0.5 h vs. 20 h to equivalent yields) is qualitatively obvious from Table 1 and was accepted as a plausible mechanistic demonstration by the field; a dedicated paper reporting kobs for this pair would not pass the novelty threshold of any major journal. The incentive structure rewards substrate scope extension and directing-group innovation over precision quantification of a rate ratio already informally established.
* **Heuristic significance**: Yes. EP-05a represents a case where a selectivity-attribution claim ([REDACTED: substantial verbatim quotation; see Tier 9]) has been accepted by the field for 30+ years as demonstrated mechanistic evidence without the underlying quantitative kinetic data being reported. The field's collective judgment that this qualitative argument is "good enough" is itself evidence of how publication incentives maintain a structural absence in the mechanistic record: the ring-strain rate-ratio claim for Murai 1993 remains, strictly speaking, experimentally unquantified.
* **Future filling feasibility**: High — The experiment is technically straightforward by current standards: time-resolved GC yield monitoring for two substrate runs under identical conditions, processed to give kobs, is a one-day experiment. DFT barrier calculations for the two cyclometallation ring sizes (5-membered vs. 6-membered metallacycle) would provide the theoretical complement. The only barrier to filling is incentive, not technology.

EP-09a [Internal benchmark pack — strict matched-substrate dimension]:
* **Reason for non-filling**: Technical difficulty (structural mismatch) combined with misaligned incentive structure — The Jordan & Taylor 1989 Zr/α-picoline/propene system and the Moore et al. 1992 Ru/pyridine/CO/hexene system use substrates incompatible with the Murai 1993 aromatic ketone system. A common-substrate matched comparison would require developing a catalyst applicable to both pyridine and aromatic ketone C–H functionalization under comparable conditions — a different research program, not a single experiment. The qualitative superiority narrative (Murai 1993 is more general and selective) was so convincing to the field that no group invested in the methodologically difficult matched comparison. Separately, refs 11 and 12 operate at VQ-2 level and the numerical comparison would be approximate even if attempted.
* **Heuristic significance**: Yes. The comparative efficiency claim [REDACTED: substantial verbatim quotation; see Tier 9] has been cited thousands of times without a numerical comparison being supplied. This is a case where a field-founding paper's benchmark claim was taken at face value for 30+ years because the claim was directionally correct (the Murai reaction is highly efficient) even if the supporting numerical comparison was never performed. The absence is invisible because the conclusion is accepted; the supporting data remain unverified.
* **Future filling feasibility**: Low — The substrate mismatch between the three systems makes a genuine matched-substrate TOF comparison structurally difficult. A substrate-normalized comparison (e.g., relative TOF per mol catalyst on a common simple olefin, with each system's directing-group requirement met) is feasible in principle but would require a bespoke experimental design. The filling is unlikely to appear as an independent paper; it is more likely to appear as a footnote in a future comprehensive catalysis comparison review.
