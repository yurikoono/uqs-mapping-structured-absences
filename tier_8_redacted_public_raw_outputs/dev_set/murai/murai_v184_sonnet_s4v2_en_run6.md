# EP-Filling Selection Map

## Murai et al. 1993 (Nature, vol. 366, pp. 529–531)

---

### §A Three-tier classification of EP filling difficulty

**Tier 1 (clearly trackable in the literature):** EP-03a, EP-04a, EP-05a, EP-06a, EP-07a

- **EP-03a [Direct-observation / state-assignment pack]**: The experiment demanded — stoichiometric synthesis and spectroscopic characterisation of the five-membered Ru–C metalacycle intermediate, plus characterisation of Ru(0) activation from RuH₂(CO)(PPh₃)₃ — is concrete enough (specific complex, specific NMR probe ³¹P{¹H}, specific substrate acetophenone) to be the main subject of independent mechanistic papers by the Murai/Chatani/Kakiuchi group and by groups specialising in Ru organometallic chemistry. This corresponds to RI-03 (unobserved explanatory), which regularly generates Tier 1 filling papers.

- **EP-04a [Discrimination / replication pack]**: The kinetic experiment demanded — initial-rate measurements for runs 10, 11, and 1-indanone, plus kH/kD kinetic isotope effect with deuterated aromatic — is a concrete, independent mechanistic measurement that forms the core of dedicated kinetics papers; KIE studies on C–H functionalization by Ru are published as stand-alone research.

- **EP-05a [Quantitative-ranking substantiation pack]**: Synthesis of the three non-observed regioisomers and GC confirmation of "virtually 100%" selectivity is a concrete substrate-synthesis-plus-analysis experiment that would constitute the main content of a synthetic/analytical paper; regioisomer identification in Murai-type reactions is tracked in the literature.

- **EP-06a [Analogy-validation pack]**: Explicit comparison of the Mn(I)/Ru(II) carboxylate cyclometallation mechanism (refs 14/15) with the proposed Ru(0) OA cycle is a mechanistic-comparison experiment that is directly trackable: papers that validate or refute the mechanistic isomorphism are independent research contributions in organometallic chemistry.

- **EP-07a [Boundary-test pack]**: Testing non-ketone directing groups (aryl aldehyde, aryl ester, aryl amide, 2-arylpyridine) under standard Murai conditions is a concrete substrate-scope expansion experiment whose results would be published as independent papers (e.g., "Ru-catalysed C–H functionalization with aldehyde directing groups"). The directing-group boundary question is one of the most-studied aspects of the Murai reaction.

**Tier 2 (indirectly trackable):** EP-09a

- **EP-09a [Internal benchmark pack]**: Reporting actual TOF values for runs 1/9/10 and cross-comparing numerically with refs 11 (Jordan & Taylor 1989) and 12 (Moore et al. 1992) requires compilation and normalisation of data across heterogeneous substrate classes; this appears within review articles and benchmarking sections of full-paper follow-ups rather than as the main subject of an independent paper, making it indirectly trackable.

**Tier 3 (filling may not be found):** EP-01a, EP-08a

- **EP-01a [Reporting-coherence pack]**: The demand for GC internal standard specification, response factors, and isolated yields for all 13 runs is an RI-01 reporting-format issue; it is typically resolved automatically in a communication→Full Paper transition (e.g., Kakiuchi et al. full paper in JACS or JACS-style journal) rather than being the main subject of an independent paper. A relevant full paper exists but it "fills" by automatic format expansion, not by independent experimental design.

- **EP-08a [Replication / uncertainty pack]**: Independent monitoring of olefin conversion (RI-08 unreplicated reliability) by ¹H NMR aliquots or GC headspace is an RI-08 analytical-completeness question whose resolution is subsumed into full-paper follow-up experiments; this does not generate an independent paper and is partially addressed by olefin mass-balance data in full-paper scope expansions.

### §D Correspondence between VQ hierarchy and filling time order

**Filling time axis:**

- VQ-2 requirement (RI-01, RI-05, RI-08 partial): EP-01a → 1995 [Kakiuchi et al., BCSJ] — self-filling, automatic-resolution
- VQ-2 requirement (RI-07, directing-group scope): EP-07a → 1997–1998 [Satoh/Miura; Chatani/Murai] — self-filling onset + cross-field onset
- VQ-3 requirement (RI-06, analogy validation): EP-06a → 1998 [Matsubara/Morokuma] — technology-driven filling (DFT)
- VQ-3 requirement (RI-03, intermediate observation onset): EP-03a → 1999 [Kakiuchi/Yamada, Organometallics] — self-filling (stoichiometric approach)
- VQ-3 requirement (RI-03, Ru(0) activation sub-question): EP-03a (CLA-1) → 2004 [Oxgaard/Goddard] — technology-driven filling (DFT)
- VQ-2/VQ-4 requirement (RI-09, external benchmark): EP-09a → 2002 [Kakiuchi/Murai, Acc. Chem. Res.] — self-filling, indirect (retrospective)
- VQ-3 requirement (RI-04, kinetic rate attribution): EP-04a → **unfilled as specific study** (partially addressed in retrospective Account papers, 2002+) — systematic avoidance
- VQ-4 requirement (RI-08, olefin mass balance): EP-08a → **unfilled** — systematic avoidance / misaligned incentive

**Evaluation of hierarchy–time correspondence:**

The hypothesis that higher VQ levels require longer filling times is broadly supported but with a notable inversion. VQ-2 requirements (reporting coherence, scope boundary) were addressed first and most rapidly: EP-01a self-filled in 1995 (2 years), EP-07a began filling in 1997–1998 (4–5 years). VQ-3 requirements (mechanism, analogy validation) required longer: EP-06a (DFT validation) arrived in 1998 (5 years), EP-03a (intermediate characterisation) began in 1999 and extended to 2004+ (6–11 years). VQ-4 benchmark requirements (EP-09a, quantitative cross-system comparison) were only partially addressed via retrospective Account papers and remain incomplete in the strict quantitative sense demanded by RI-09. The inversion occurs for EP-08a (VQ-4, olefin mass balance): this RI-08 question has not been filled despite its relatively low technical difficulty — the misaligned incentive structure, not technical complexity, explains why a VQ-4 question remained unfilled while VQ-3 mechanistic questions (more technically demanding) were addressed.

**Notable patterns:**

- **Self-filling (dominant pattern)**: The Murai/Chatani/Kakiuchi group themselves filled the largest proportion of EPs through planned research programs — BCSJ 1995 (EP-01a), Organometallics 1999 (EP-03a onset), Acc. Chem. Res. 2002 (EP-09a), and scope expansion papers (EP-07a). This is characteristic of a high-profile Nature communication that establishes a new research program: the authors have strong incentive to fill their own gaps because doing so extends the field they defined.

- **Technology-driven filling (VQ-3 specific)**: The VQ-3 mechanistic EPs (EP-03a Ru(0) question, EP-06a analogy validation) were addressed primarily via DFT computational chemistry (Matsubara/Morokuma 1998; Oxgaard 2004), a technology that did not exist at the accessible computational cost level in 1993. This is a classic technology-driven filling pattern: the question was present from 1993 but could only be answered when DFT methodology became routine for transition-metal systems.

- **Cross-field filling (EP-07a)**: The directing-group scope question (EP-07a, U-3) was answered not only by the Murai group but by independent groups (Miura, Fagnou, and others) approaching C–H functionalization from different starting points. This cross-field filling generated an entire research sub-field (directing-group-controlled C–H functionalization), confirming that the scope question identified by UQS was scientifically productive.

- **Long-delay / systematic avoidance (EP-04a, EP-08a)**: The kinetic rate attribution question (EP-04a: rate constants for runs 10/11, kH/kD for 1-indanone) and the olefin mass-balance question (EP-08a) have not been filled by any identified independent paper even 30+ years after the 1993 publication. The rate attribution question (B-8, B-9: ring-strain hypothesis for run 10 vs. 11) was discussed qualitatively but never subjected to dedicated kinetic study. This long-delay / systematic-avoidance pattern is consistent with a misaligned incentive structure: repeating the Murai reaction with added kinetic monitoring would be difficult to publish as novel research.

- **Author retrospective comparison**: The Kakiuchi/Murai Acc. Chem. Res. 2002 Account identifies the reaction's key contributions as efficiency, generality, and chelation assistance. The retrospective does not acknowledge the scope overstatement (U-3, B-5: "variety of aromatic systems" vs. aromatic ketones only), the analytical incompleteness of the TOF claim (B-2), or the uncharacterised pre-catalyst activation (CLA-1/U-2) as structural gaps in the original paper. This gap between author-acknowledged contributions and UQS-identified structural absences is direct evidence that the gaps identified in U-1, U-2, B-2, and B-5 were invisible to the field insiders at the time of retrospective writing.

### §E Heuristic significance of unfilled EPs

**List of unfilled EPs:**

EP-04a [Discrimination / replication pack]:

* **Reason for non-filling**: Misaligned incentive structure — The specific experiments demanded (initial rates for runs 10, 11, and 1-indanone under identical conditions; kH/kD isotope effect for C–H cleavage rate-limitation) are technically feasible with methods available since 1993, but constitute a "replication-and-extension-of-conditions" study rather than a novel catalytic discovery. A paper whose primary content is [REDACTED: substantial verbatim quotation; see Tier 9] would have faced severe difficulty in publication as an independent contribution. The kinetic data that would fill EP-04a are most likely embedded in unpublished laboratory notebooks from the Murai group and were never published as a dedicated study.

* **Heuristic significance**: Yes — The ring-strain-controlled reactivity hypothesis (B-8/B-9) has been cited in thousands of downstream papers and mechanistic textbooks as an established principle of chelation-directed C–H functionalization, yet the specific rate constants and kH/kD value that would establish the rate-determining step for this system have never been reported. UQS reveals that one of the field's canonical mechanistic rationalisations (ring-strain control via the five-membered metalacycle) rests on a single qualitative rate comparison (0.5 h vs. 20 h) that has never been quantitatively supported in 30+ years of follow-up.
