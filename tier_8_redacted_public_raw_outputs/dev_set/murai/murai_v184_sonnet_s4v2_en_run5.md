### §A Three-tier classification of EP filling difficulty

**EP inventory from Stage 3 §5 (Shortest-reinforcement route):**

VQ-3 route: EP-03a, EP-04a, EP-06a
VQ-4 route: EP-01a, EP-08a, EP-09a, EP-07a

---

**Tier 1 (clearly trackable in the literature):** EP-03a, EP-04a, EP-06a, EP-07a

- **EP-03a** [Direct-observation / state-assignment pack]: The experiment demanded — spectroscopic characterization of the cyclometallated Ru intermediate from RuH₂(CO)(PPh₃)₃ and an aromatic ketone — is concrete enough to be the main subject of independent mechanistic publications in organometallic chemistry. RI-03 intermediate-trapping experiments are routinely published as full papers.
- **EP-04a** [Discrimination / replication pack]: Time-resolved kinetic comparison between ring-constrained vs. ring-strain-free ketone substrates (run 10 vs. run 11), with rate constants and 1-indanone negative-result quantification, is the kind of experiment that constitutes standalone mechanistic papers in synthetic organometallic catalysis.
- **EP-06a** [Analogy-validation pack]: Validation of whether Mn(I)/Ru(II) electrophilic cyclometallation precedents are mechanistically isomorphic to Ru(0) OA is a tractable experimental question (synthesis of a genuine Ru(0) aryl ketone cyclometallate, or DFT mechanistic comparison) that would be published as independent research.
- **EP-07a** [Boundary-test pack]: Testing non-ketone directing groups (aldehydes, amides, imines) to bound the scope claim "variety of aromatic systems" is a concrete experimental expansion that generates standalone substrate-scope papers in C–H functionalization.

**Tier 2 (indirectly trackable):** EP-09a

- **EP-09a** [Internal benchmark pack]: Side-by-side quantitative TOF comparison with Jordan & Taylor (ref. 11) and Moore et al. (ref. 12) on a common substrate — or explicit boundary acknowledgment — would likely appear within the efficiency section of a full paper or review rather than as the primary subject of an independent paper; however, individual full papers in the Murai group trajectory may include this comparison.

**Tier 3 (filling may not be found):** EP-01a, EP-08a

- **EP-01a** [Reporting-coherence pack]: GC calibration methodology (internal standard, response factors, TOF numerical values) and "standard methods" specification — these items are resolved automatically by the full paper that followed the communication but do not constitute the subject of an independent paper; filling is via the communication→Full Paper automatic-resolution pattern.
- **EP-08a** [Replication / uncertainty pack]: Duplicate/triplicate yield measurements and mean ± SD reporting reflect field convention rather than a research question answerable by an independent paper; the catalyst-loading anomaly in runs 5–6 is subsidiary to the main reaction demonstration and would be addressed in supplementary material of the full paper.

**Filling time axis:**

**Evaluation of hierarchy–time correspondence:**

The hierarchy–time hypothesis is supported with a noteworthy Technology-driven inversion. The VQ-2 EPs (EP-01a, EP-08a) were filled fastest (2 years, 1995) via the automatic communication→Full Paper mechanism, consistent with the hypothesis. The VQ-3 EPs (EP-03a, EP-04a, EP-06a) required 5–7 years (1998–2000) and were filled not by the stoichiometric experiments the EPs nominally demanded but by DFT computation, which emerged as a feasible method for organometallic mechanism in the mid-1990s. The VQ-4 EP (EP-09a) remains the most persistently unfilled due to a structural infeasibility (incompatible substrate classes), consistent with the hypothesis that higher VQ requirements are harder to fill. However, the mechanism of filling inverts the EP's original framing: EP-06a was written demanding a Ru(0) aryl ketone OA precedent by stoichiometric isolation (RI-06 analogy-validation), but the actual filling bypassed isolation entirely through DFT energy profiles — a technology-driven paradigm shift within the filling rather than a paradigm-shift of the research framework.

**Notable patterns:**

- **Self-filling (VQ-2):** The BCSJ 1995 paper by the Kakiuchi/Murai group is the canonical self-filling of EP-01a and partially EP-08a; the Chatani/Murai group's scope expansion papers (1998) self-fill EP-07a. The original Osaka group maintained a continuous program addressing their own communication's gaps.
- **Technology-driven filling (VQ-3):** EP-03a, EP-04a, and EP-06a were all filled computationally rather than by the stoichiometric isolation experiments the EPs specifically demanded. The Matsubara/Morokuma program (1998–2000) constitutes a Technology-driven bundle filling of the entire VQ-3 requirement. This is a documented instance of the Technology-driven filling pattern where the new technology (DFT for organometallics, circa 1995–2000) fills EP requirements that were written against experimental methods that remained technically challenging.
- **Bundle filling (VQ-3):** The Matsubara/Morokuma 1998 JACS + 2000 Organometallics papers together fill EP-03a (partial), EP-04a (partial), and EP-06a (complete) — three EPs addressed by two papers from the same computational group. This is the most efficient single-program filling detected in this cartography.
- **Long-delay / partial unfilling (VQ-4):** EP-09a remains structurally partially unfilled because the cross-class TOF comparison it demands (Murai reaction vs. refs 11–12 on a common substrate) is infeasible due to substrate class difference; this EP represents a case where the EP's specific demand could not be met even given good faith efforts over 30 years, and the appropriate filling is a clarifying boundary statement rather than an experimental measurement.
- **Comparison with author retrospective:** The Kakiuchi/Murai *Acc. Chem. Res.* 2002 retrospective account (tentative_citation: Kakiuchi, F.; Murai, S. *Acc. Chem. Res.* **2002**, 35, 826–834) emphasizes the development of substrate scope and the mechanistic picture as the primary subsequent achievements. The EP map identifies three items the retrospective would likely not foreground: (a) the Ru(II)→Ru(0) pre-catalyst activation gap (EP-03a component for U-2), which remains incompletely resolved even in the retrospective framing; (b) the detection-limit basis for the "did not react at all" 1-indanone result (EP-04a component for B-9), which may never have been published as a quantitative lower bound; (c) the bilateral quantitative yield olefin-fate question (EP-01a / B-7 component), which is structurally absent from the 1993 paper and likely addressed only implicitly in the full paper. These three items are the "structural absences invisible to field insiders" predicted by UQS: they are real evidential gaps in the 1993 paper that subsequent literature addressed obliquely at best.

**EP-08a [Replication / uncertainty pack]:**

* **Reason for non-filling**: Misaligned incentive structure. Replicate yield measurements with mean ± SD are technically feasible for all 13 runs in Table 1 and the elevated-loading runs 5–6; the determination of whether the threefold catalyst loading increase for allylsilane/vinylsilane substrates can be reduced to standard loading at the cost of yield is a well-defined experimental question. However, publishing a paper whose primary contribution is [REDACTED: substantial verbatim quotation; see Tier 9] has no viable publication pathway under current conventions — the novelty threshold cannot be cleared. The field convention that single-run GC yields are acceptable in synthetic scope tables means this EP is systematically unaddressed. The runs 5–6 loading anomaly was likely noted in the BCSJ 1995 full paper as a parenthetical remark but was not the subject of dedicated optimization experiments published independently.
* **Heuristic significance**: Yes. EP-08a is a UQS-visible structural absence that the field has systematically avoided not because the experiment is impossible but because the publication incentive structure renders it unpublishable as an independent paper. The runs 5–6 catalyst loading anomaly (0.12 mmol vs. standard 0.04 mmol, no stated rationale) is an unresolved practical question about the generality of the "practical organic synthesis" efficiency claim for allylsilane substrates. From the UQS perspective, this is evidence that the field's incentive structure maintains structural silences about negative substrate-class effects within landmark papers. A replication study in 2025 using the now-routine triplicate reporting convention would fill this EP completely.
* **Future filling feasibility**: High — the experiment is technically trivial by current standards; any replication or total-scope systematic study could include triplicate runs for Table 1 substrates; the catalyst loading anomaly for runs 5–6 would be the natural focus of a substrate-optimization note in any contemporary re-examination of the Murai reaction scope.

**EP-03a [Direct-observation / state-assignment pack] (residual unfilled component: U-2 activation mechanism):**

* **Reason for non-filling (residual component)**: Technical difficulty. The specific sub-question within EP-03a — why does the genuine Ru(0) source Ru₃(CO)₁₂ fail to catalyze the reaction despite the proposed cycle requiring Ru(0) as the active species? — was addressed computationally by the Matsubara/Morokuma papers but may not have been addressed by direct experimental characterization of the Ru(II)→Ru(0) pre-catalyst activation mechanism. The H₂ evolution monitoring experiment demanded by EP-03a for the U-2 activation question (head-space GC or manometry during induction period with RuH₂(CO)(PPh₃)₃ + ketone, no olefin) is technically feasible but requires conditions (inert atmosphere, gas-tight setup, defined temperatures) that create practical barriers. The Matsubara/Morokuma DFT work established that the Ru(II) dihydride undergoes reductive elimination of H₂ to generate the Ru(0) active species — thereby resolving the Ru₃(CO)₁₂ paradox computationally (Ru₃(CO)₁₂ cannot easily generate the same Ru(0) mononuclear species with the right ligand set).
