# EP-Filling Selection Map

## Cherney, Kadunce, and Reisman 2015 (Chemical Reviews, review)

---

### §A Three-tier classification of EP filling difficulty

**Tier 1 (clearly trackable in the literature):** EP-03a, EP-04a, EP-06a

- **EP-03a** [Direct-observation / state-assignment pack]: The experiments demanded — EPR detection of Ni¹/Ni³ intermediates and saturation kinetics for precoordination — are discrete mechanistic studies likely to exist as the primary subject of independent papers in the Ni-catalyzed cross-coupling mechanistic literature (2015–2025).
- **EP-04a** [Discrimination / replication pack]: Radical cyclization rate constants and TEMPO inhibition experiments for Ni-catalyzed alkyl coupling are the kind of quantitative mechanistic experiments reported as independent contributions (radical clock calibration papers, mechanistic studies of Ni catalysis); these are well-defined experimental targets.
- **EP-06a** [Analogy-validation pack]: The direct experimental test of the DET mechanism for Cu/L32 (running enantiopure (R)-313 and (S)-313 separately) is a clean single-experiment test that would naturally appear as part of a mechanistic study of Cu-catalyzed allylic substitution stereoconvergence.

**Tier 2 (indirectly trackable):** EP-05a, EP-07a, EP-08a, EP-09a

- **EP-05a** [Quantitative-ranking substantiation pack]: Measured k_rac/k_coup ratios for secondary Grignard reagents are quantitative mechanistic data more likely to appear embedded in mechanistic sections of broad reaction development papers than as the main subject of independent dedicated kinetics papers; however, follow-up studies from the Fu and related groups may contain such data.
- **EP-07a** [Boundary-test pack]: A systematic inventory of natural products bearing all-carbon quaternary centers or vicinal tertiary centers inaccessible by enantiocontrolled cross-coupling is a claim-type more likely to appear in review articles or perspective pieces than as the primary subject of an independent research paper.
- **EP-08a** [Replication / uncertainty pack]: Functional group tolerance surveys for the Cu/L101 allylic alkylation system are likely included in expanded substrate scope papers from the originating research group or application papers, rather than as independent standalone contributions.
- **EP-09a** [Internal benchmark pack]: A direct quantitative comparison of enantiocontrolled cross-coupling vs. Nobel-winning achiral cross-coupling performance on identical substrates is more likely to appear in a review or perspective than as primary research; the data exist in the literature but the comparison is not assembled as an independent paper.

**Tier 3 (filling may not be found):** none of the EP codes assigned by Stage 3 §5 fall cleanly into Tier 3. All six EP types have at least some experimental content that is trackable either directly (Tier 1) or indirectly through reviews, expanded scope papers, or mechanistic follow-ups (Tier 2).

**Selection rationale:**

The selection prioritizes papers that address the highest-priority EPs (EP-03a for SRG-2 and EP-04a for SRG-4, both at VQ-3 level) first, followed by papers addressing the VQ-2–level boundary and benchmark EPs (EP-07a, EP-08a, EP-09a). The ordering reflects both EP coverage and filling decisiveness (Complete or Partial preferred over Indirect).

Two bundle-filling patterns are notable. Entry 1 (Breitenfeld 2013, tentative) fills both EP-03a and EP-04a simultaneously because EPR characterization of Ni¹ alkyl complexes directly addresses both the state-assignment requirement (EP-03a) and the radical-character discrimination (EP-04a/SRG-4). Entry 2 (photoredox/Ni EPR cluster) similarly bundles EP-03a and EP-06a because the same body of work that confirms Ni¹/Ni³ by EPR also provides the experimental isomorphism confirmation that CLA-1 requires (RI-06). The VQ-2 entries (3, 4, 6, 7) address boundary and benchmarking EPs that are necessary for VQ-4 but less foundational than the VQ-3 mechanistic entries.

### §D Correspondence between VQ hierarchy and filling time order

**Filling time axis:**

- VQ-2 requirement: EP-05a (k_rac/k_coup for Grignard 13, SRG-3) → **unfilled** as of recalled literature through 2026

**Evaluation of hierarchy–time correspondence:**

The hierarchy–time hypothesis — that higher VQ requirements take longer to fill — receives mixed support from the Cherney 2015 data. The VQ-3 EP-03a (Ni¹/Ni³ EPR) was partially addressed in the 2013–2020 window, consistent with the expectation that mechanistic validation at VQ-3 lags the original claim. However, the VQ-2 EP-07a (biological prevalence) was indirectly filled earlier (2009) than the VQ-3 EP-03a, which formally inverts the expected hierarchy. The explanation is that the biological prevalence filling (Lovering 2009) was not motivated by the Cherney 2015 paper — it was produced independently by the medicinal chemistry community and constitutes a pre-existing indirect resource rather than a targeted response to the review's gap. The hierarchy hypothesis applies more cleanly to targeted, response-driven fillings than to cross-field fillings that arise independently.

The most striking pattern is that two of the highest-priority EPs (EP-03a for SRG-1/precoordination, and EP-05a for SRG-3/k_rac) remain unfilled despite being technically feasible. This constitutes a selective non-filling of foundational mechanistic requirements at VQ-3, consistent with the misaligned incentive structure diagnosis in §E: publishing "we measured k_rac to confirm the DKR assignment" or "we confirmed precoordination by saturation kinetics" does not carry the novelty premium that the field's incentive structure rewards.

**Notable patterns:**

- **Cross-field filling (technology-driven):** The most decisive partial filling of EP-03a (Ni¹/Ni³ EPR establishment) came not from mechanistic follow-up on the Ni-catalyzed cross-coupling systems reviewed in Cherney 2015, but from the explosive growth of photoredox/Ni dual catalysis (2016–2020), which created a scientific demand for understanding Ni oxidation states that motivated EPR and electrochemical characterization across the field. This is a classic cross-field filling pattern: the incentive for the filling came from a different subfield.

- **Pre-existing indirect filling:** EP-04a (radical clock reference rate constants) and EP-07a (biological prevalence of quaternary centers) were partially filled by literature existing before the Cherney 2015 review, but the filling was not recognized as such because the papers were not written with the cross-coupling EP in mind. This is an "unrecognized filling" pattern — the relevant data existed but was not assembled to fill the gap.

- **Selective unfilling of foundational kinetic EPs:** EP-03a (SRG-1 precoordination saturation kinetics) and EP-05a (SRG-3 k_rac/k_coup) remain unfilled despite being experimentally feasible. Both require measurements that are standard in physical organic chemistry but that have not been performed on the specific systems in question. The non-filling is consistent with misaligned incentive structure: the measurements confirm existing qualitative assignments rather than enabling new reactions, reducing their publication value.

- **Bundle filling via photoredox/Ni catalysis community:** The photoredox/Ni literature (Entry 2 of §C) fills EP-03a and EP-06a simultaneously because establishing Ni¹/Ni³ spectroscopically inherently validates the DFT-derived mechanistic model (RI-06 isomorphism). This bundle efficiency was not planned by the filling authors; it arose from the logical co-dependency of the two EP requirements.

EP-03a (SRG-1) [Ni–heteroatom precoordination saturation kinetics]:

* **Reason for non-filling**: Misaligned incentive structure.
