**Scope abstraction and transformations:**

The paper answered this question: *Can nickel–phosphine complexes catalyze selective cross-coupling of Grignard reagents with C(sp²) halides, and does the proposed mechanistic cycle serve as a generalizable template?* The deepest unasked question concerns the evidentiary gap between a proposed mechanistic cycle (constructed by analogy from stoichiometric reactions) and its claimed status as a catalytic prototype adopted by an entire field.

This structure — a mechanistic proposal assembled from stoichiometric precedents, asserted as operative under catalytic conditions, and subsequently adopted as a conceptual template without independent experimental validation of the catalytic-manifold elementary steps — recurs across chemistry and beyond:

1. **Same structure in organometallic catalysis:** Proposed catalytic cycles in C–H activation chemistry are frequently assembled by analogy from stoichiometric insertions and eliminations; the question of which intermediates are kinetically accessible under turnover conditions is routinely deferred or treated as resolved by analogy. (Same structure in a different chemical domain)

2. **Same structure in enzymatic catalysis:** Enzyme mechanisms are often proposed from X-ray structures of stable ground-state or inhibitor-bound complexes; the question of whether the proposed transition state is actually traversed under physiological turnover conditions is structurally analogous. (Same structure in a biological domain)

3. **Same structure in historiography of science:** The claim that a conceptual framework "became the prototype" for a subsequent line of research is a historiographic claim requiring documentation of intellectual transmission; the absence of such documentation is a structural gap that recurs whenever scientists write retrospective accounts of their own influence. (Figurative similarity — the evidentiary gap between assertion of intellectual lineage and documented conceptual adoption)

4. **Same structure in technology transfer:** When an algorithm or design pattern developed in one context is described as the "prototype" for subsequent implementations in other contexts, the claim of genealogical influence requires documentation of which specific features were adopted and which were independently rediscovered. (Figurative similarity — same epistemological structure in a non-chemical domain)
