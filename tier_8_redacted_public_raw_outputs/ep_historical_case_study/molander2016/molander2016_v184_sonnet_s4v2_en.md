# EP-Filling Selection Map

## Tellis, Kelly, Primer, Jouffroy, Patel, and Molander 2016 (Accounts of Chemical Research, full_paper)

---

### §A Three-tier classification of EP filling difficulty

**Background:** Stage 3 §5 identified ten EP codes across two objective functions (VQ-3 mechanism and VQ-4 benchmark). The ten EPs, drawn from the shortest-reinforcement route, are: EP-01a, EP-03a, EP-04a, EP-05a, EP-06a, EP-07a, EP-08a, EP-09a (from §5), plus the SRG-level EPs EP-03a / EP-04a for SRG-2 and SRG-4. The consolidated distinct set for classification is: **EP-01a, EP-03a, EP-04a, EP-05a, EP-06a, EP-07a, EP-08a, EP-09a.**

---

**Tier 1 (clearly trackable in the literature):** EP-03a, EP-04a, EP-05a, EP-07a, EP-09a

- **EP-03a [Direct-observation / state-assignment pack]**: The concrete experimental demands are: (i) variable-temperature enantioselectivity (VT-ee) measurements for the stereoconvergent trifluoroborate coupling (to validate the Curtin–Hammett DKR model), and (ii) Stern–Volmer fluorescence quenching experiments distinguishing Ir*/BnBF₃K vs. Ir*/Ni quenching rates. Both experiments are sufficiently specific and standardized that papers addressing them — even if not written to fill this EP — are independently identifiable through keyword searches: "photoredox nickel stereoconvergent variable temperature" for (i) and "Stern–Volmer Ir nickel photoredox organoboron" for (ii). Numerous independent papers in the mechanistic photoredox literature (2015–2022) address exactly these measurements. Tier 1 classification is warranted because the experimental apparatus is a standard fluorimeter plus chiral HPLC, the substrate system is unambiguously specified (PhCH(Me)BF₃K / 4-CN-C₆H₄Br / ligand L2 from Figure 10B), and the required information is the main subject of dedicated mechanistic papers rather than a sub-section of scope papers.

- **EP-04a [Discrimination / replication pack]**: The radical-clock probe experiment (cyclopropylmethyl potassium trifluoroborate ring-opening to homoallylic product under Ni/photoredox conditions) is a canonical mechanistic diagnostic tool in radical chemistry. Papers employing radical-clock substrates in the context of Ni/photoredox catalysis exist as independent publications with "radical clock" in their titles or abstracts. The experimental design is highly specific: one substrate (ring-strained trifluoroborate precursor), one reaction condition (standard Ni/photoredox), one observable (ratio of ring-closed cross-coupling product vs. ring-opened homoallylic product by GC). This concreteness makes Tier 1 classification appropriate.

- **EP-05a [Quantitative-ranking substantiation pack]**: Cyclic voltammetry characterization of organotrifluoroborate oxidation potentials is a standard electroanalytical measurement that multiple research groups report as part of their photoredox methodology development. The specific experiment (CV of benzyltrifluoroborate 33 and aryl trifluoroborate 34 from eq 3 in the Accounts paper, measured in MeCN / 0.1 M TBAPF₆ vs. SCE) is the main subject of dedicated electrochemical characterization sections in primary methodology papers from the Molander group and the Fensterbank/Goddard groups. The data are reported as a table of E_ox values across substrate classes — a format that is directly trackable in primary papers' Supporting Information sections.

- **EP-07a [Boundary-test pack]**: This EP encompasses two boundary claims: (a) the mechanistic role of 2,6-lutidine across the entire trifluoroborate program, requiring a BF₃·OEt₂ competition experiment and a pKa-matched non-amine base screen; and (b) the competition experiment (eq 3) selectivity robustness across substrate ratios. For (a), the BF₃·OEt₂ competition and base-screen experiments are simple modification of the standard reaction conditions and are commonly embedded in "control experiments" sections of primary methodology papers. For (b), the substrate-ratio variation test for the sp³/sp² competition selectivity is a straightforward mechanistic probe that dedicated mechanistic studies address explicitly. The concreteness of both sub-experiments and their compatibility with standard synthetic methodology paper formats qualifies Tier 1.

- **EP-09a [Internal benchmark pack]**: Head-to-head substrate comparisons between photoredox/Ni and conventional secondary alkyl Suzuki–Miyaura protocols for the same substrate are the explicit research question of comparative methodology papers. The required experiment is well-defined: select five substrates from refs 7a–7f (prior Molander or Biscoe or Dreher secondary alkyl Suzuki–Miyaura papers), run them under both the new photoredox/Ni conditions and the prior Pd-catalyzed conditions, and report yields side by side. Papers with explicit benchmark comparison framing ([REDACTED: substantial verbatim quotation; see Tier 9]) are independently identifiable in the synthetic methodology literature. The substrate set and reference protocol set are well-specified, making this Tier 1 in principle, even if the filling paper is rare in practice (see §E).

---

**Tier 2 (indirectly trackable):** EP-01a, EP-06a, EP-08a

- **EP-01a [Reporting-coherence pack]**: The yield-type (isolated vs. GC) and mass balance information demanded by EP-01a is present in the SI documents of the primary papers (refs 16 and 18) that the Accounts article reviews. These primary papers are independently available and identifiable; however, the specific data demanded by EP-01a is not the *main subject* of those papers — it is embedded in their SI. The "no polymerization" mass balance claim for compound 11 (61% yield, 4-bromostyrene substrate) specifically requires GPC data that may not exist anywhere in published form. EP-01a falls into Tier 2 because the filling information is embedded within papers whose primary purpose is different (substrate scope demonstration), and because the mass balance sub-claim may not have been addressed anywhere.

- **EP-06a [Analogy-validation pack]**: The solvation-sensitivity test (PES recomputation under SMD-acetone, SMD-dioxane) and the ⟨S²⟩ reporting for open-shell Ni intermediates at each stationary point in Figure 9 are specialized DFT benchmarking analyses. Such analyses are occasionally the main subject of dedicated computational benchmarking papers (e.g., "DFT performance for 3d metal radical mechanisms"), but more commonly appear as sub-sections within broader mechanistic or computational papers. The probability that an independent paper exists whose primary purpose is to validate the Gutierrez 2015 PES under alternative solvation models is moderate but not high, making this Tier 2. Additionally, the secondary alkyltrifluoroborate PES extension (SRG-10) demands a computation that no paper has been written specifically to perform.

- **EP-08a [Replication / uncertainty pack]**: Yield reproducibility data (replicate measurements, standard deviations) for the specific scope compounds of the Accounts paper are embedded within the SI of the primary papers (refs 16 and 18) as standard experimental sections. However, the targeted attribution of yield outliers (compound (±)-43 at 59%, compound 64 at 32%, compounds 83/84 at 50–53%) is unlikely to be the main subject of any independent paper. The outlier attribution would be embedded in a primary paper's "mechanistic studies" section or its SI optimization tables, making this Tier 2. The compound 11 GPC data sub-claim is the same as EP-01a's Tier 2 mass balance issue.

---

**Tier 3 (filling may not be found):** None

No EP in the Stage 3 §5 shortest-reinforcement route maps to RI-01 (reporting integrity alone) or RI-08 (unquantified reliability alone) as the *sole* filling requirement for a standalone independent paper, with the exception of EP-01a and EP-08a which both reference RI-01/RI-08 but are classified Tier 2 (not Tier 3) because their filling information exists within the primary papers underlying this Accounts review. No EP arises purely from a paper-specific reporting format issue that would categorically prevent independent paper identification.

The VQ hierarchy–time hypothesis is partially supported, with important nuances. **EP-06a [Analogy-validation pack] (partial — solvation sensitivity component):**

* **Reason for non-filling**: Two combined factors. **Supplementary analysis: Era-norm context for EP filling patterns**

A nuanced reading of the EP filling landscape for this paper requires distinguishing between absences that were era-norm-tolerated in 2016 and those that are modern-era-deficient (see Stage 3 §4). This distinction carries direct implications for how the EP-filling timeline should be interpreted.

For EP-01a (yield type, mass balance): The yield-type omission in Accounts format was era-norm-tolerated in 2016; the modern expectation that even review articles specify "isolated yields unless noted" emerged after 2018 in direct response to reproducibility discussions in organic synthesis. The compound 11 "no polymerization" claim is classified as modern-era-deficient in §4: it is a positive mechanistic assertion that requires mass balance evidence regardless of era. For EP-filling purposes, this means the EP-01a yield-type component is filled pre-emptively by primary papers (refs 16, 18 SI); the compound 11 mass balance component is genuinely unfilled and its absence reflects a field convention that is now recognized as deficient.

For EP-05a (E_ox data for trifluoborates): The asymmetry between silicate E_ox (reported) and trifluoroborate E_ox (unreported) is classified as modern-era-deficient in §4. In 2016, reporting CV for one substrate class but not another within the same paper was within the range of accepted practice; by 2018, the field's photoredox community had established the norm that E_ox should be reported for all substrate classes whose reactivity is mechanistically attributed to SET. The EP-05a filling trajectory is therefore one of gradual norm tightening: the absence was tolerated in 2016, became increasingly visible as deficient through 2018–2020, and is now a straightforward gap to fill by consulting the primary papers' SIs or by targeted CV measurement.

For EP-06a (solvation sensitivity, ⟨S²⟩ reporting): Also classified as modern-era-deficient. The 2015 practice of reporting DFT results without explicit ⟨S²⟩ values for open-shell 3d metal systems was within the era norm; by 2020, journals specializing in computational organometallic chemistry (JCTC, Inorganic Chemistry, JACS) routinely required ⟨S²⟩ reporting. The EP-06a gap is thus an era-norm transitional case: it was tolerated in 2015 but became identifiably deficient by 2020, creating a 5-year window during which the absence was systematically overlooked before becoming visible as a gap.

For EP-09a (benchmark comparison): Not classified as era-norm-tolerated or modern-era-deficient — instead, it is a structurally incentive-deferred absence that transcends era norms. The benchmark comparison was technically achievable at any point from 2014 onward but was never conducted, not because of reporting format constraints or field convention, but because the publication incentive structure makes it difficult to publish. This is the most UQS-significant EP in the set: it identifies a question that is both scientifically well-posed and technically executable, yet remains unanswered specifically because the incentive to answer it is structurally negative for the most natural executing parties (the original authors and their affiliated groups).

**Supplementary analysis: Self-reinforcing computational loop and EP filling implications**

Stage 3 §6 proposed PRI-12 as a new RI candidate ("Self-reinforcing computational loop"): the DFT PES, the DKR kinetic hierarchy, and the enantioselectivity prediction in this paper all derive from a single computational framework, creating the appearance of multi-layered mechanistic support from a single unvalidated source. The EP-filling analysis in §B provides concrete evidence for this structural coupling's effect on filling difficulty.

EP-03a, EP-04a, and EP-06a collectively address the three faces of the PRI-12 loop: EP-06a addresses the PES reliability (the computational foundation of the loop), EP-04a addresses the pathway discriminability (the kinetic conclusion drawn from the PES), and EP-03a addresses the DKR model validation (the stereochemical conclusion derived from the same PES topology). All three EPs require different experimental approaches (computational solvation benchmarking, radical-clock organic synthesis, fluorescence quenching spectroscopy), but they share a common root in the need to validate the single Gutierrez 2015 PES independently of the computational framework that generated it.

The filling difficulty for these three EPs is thus correlated: a paper that fills EP-06a (by recomputing the PES with alternative solvation models) provides the necessary precondition for reinterpreting the filling status of EP-03a and EP-04a. If the solvation-corrected PES shows that the B1/A1 barrier preference narrows to ~2 kcal/mol, then even a positive Stern–Volmer result (consistent with B1) cannot conclusively establish the pathway preference, because the thermodynamic driving force from the corrected PES may be insufficient to discriminate B1 from A1 on kinetic grounds. Conversely, if EP-06a confirms that the ~6 kcal/mol preference is robust across solvent dielectric ranges, then EP-03a and EP-04a become much easier to certify: a positive Stern–Volmer result under those conditions would be consistent with a large and robust computational preference, strongly supporting B1.

This coupling means that the optimal filling order for VQ-3 certification is: EP-06a first (computational PES validation) → EP-04a second (experimental pathway discrimination with interpretation conditioned on corrected PES) → EP-03a third (VT-ee / Stern–Volmer with interpretation conditioned on the established pathway preference). This is the scientifically motivated order. However, the publication-incentive order reverses this: EP-03a-type papers (VT-ee, synthetic mechanistic study) are more independently publishable than EP-06a-type papers (computational benchmarking), so the incentivized filling order is EP-03a → EP-04a → EP-06a (or EP-06a never), which is the opposite of the scientifically required order for VQ-3 certification. This represents a second PRI-12-consistent structural absence: the self-reinforcing loop not only creates the appearance of multi-layered computational support, it also creates a publishing-incentive order that inverts the logically required evidence accumulation sequence.

**Supplementary analysis: Contemporaneous independent corroboration as filling context**

The Accounts article explicitly acknowledges two groups as contemporaneous independent corroborators of the photoredox/Ni paradigm: (a) Doyle/MacMillan (ref 25, Science 2014, Csp³–Csp¹ coupling) and (b) Fensterbank/Goddard (refs 33–34, alkylbis(catecholato)silicates). Both groups' papers appear in the §C recommended selection list as filling candidates for EP-03a / EP-05a respectively. This contemporaneous corroboration structure creates an unusual filling topology: the EPs of the Accounts article are partially filled not by papers written in response to the article (which was published in 2016) but by papers published simultaneously with or before the foundational primary papers (Science 2014, JACS 2015) that the Accounts article reviews.

This creates a retroactive filling situation: some EPs of the 2016 Accounts article were already partially filled in 2014–2015 by the concurrent research programs that the article itself acknowledges as independent corroboration. Specifically:

- Zuo et al. (Science 2014, ref 25) fills EP-03a partially via analogical mechanistic discussion, written before the Accounts article.
- Corcé et al. (Angew. Chem. Int. Ed. 2015, ref 34) fills EP-05a partially for the silicate E_ox, written before the Accounts article.

This retroactive filling structure — where the filling papers for an Accounts review predate the review itself — is a characteristic feature of the Accounts format: the review compresses and synthesizes a research program that has already generated multiple independent papers, so some EPs of the compressed review are pre-filled by papers already in the literature at the time of publication. The EP-filling map for an Accounts review therefore has a distinct topology from the EP-filling map of an original primary paper: the primary filling candidates are concentrated in the 2014–2016 period (before publication) rather than in the post-publication period, and the unfilled EPs are predominantly those that neither the Molander group nor the contemporaneous groups addressed within that pre-publication window.

The topology distinction has a practical implication for EP-filling verification protocol: for a primary paper, the filling paper search is conducted forward in time from the paper's publication year; for an Accounts review, the search must also extend backward in time to the papers cited within the review, since the review's cited primary sources are the principal filling candidates for the review's EPs. In the present case, this means that refs 16, 18, 24, 25, and 34 — all cited in the Accounts article — are the most important papers to access in their full primary form (including SI) for EP-filling verification, rather than papers published after the Accounts article. This is an instance of "citation-based retroactive filling" unique to the review-article EP topology and should be flagged as a methodological note for UQS analysis of review-format papers in general.
