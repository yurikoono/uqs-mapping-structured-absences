[S0-1] core_claim: Addition of a catalytic amount of a 1,3-diene (10 mol%) to the NiCl₂-catalyzed reaction of primary alkyl Grignard reagents with primary alkyl halides (Cl, Br, I) and tosylates enables efficient cross-coupling; the paper presents the first reported nickel-catalyzed cross-coupling of inactivated alkyl chlorides using this approach.

[S0-2] evidence_type: Substrate scope table (Table 1, effect of diene additive with various dienes; Table 2, scope across alkyl chlorides/bromides/tosylates and Grignard partners with optimized conditions); mechanistic experiments (no hexadecane from NiBr₂ + 2 n-OctMgCl; n-decyl bromide does not react with Ni(COD)₂ in THF/1,3-BD; radical-clock experiment with cyclopropylmethyl bromide + n-OctMgCl → n-nonylcyclopropane 87%, no ring-opened product); NMR characterization of products; GC yield quantification.

[S0-3] prior_work_position: The paper notes that previous Ni-catalyzed cross-coupling reactions of alkyl halides with Grignard reagents showed limited success and that unactivated alkyl chlorides (a cheaper, more abundant electrophile class) had not been coupled with Grignard reagents by nickel catalysis before this work. It also positions the 1,3-diene role as "remarkable" and unprecedented relative to existing Ni/organyl-halide cross-coupling protocols.

[S0-4] methodology: Primary synthetic method: NiCl₂ (1 mol%) + 1,3-diene (10 mol%) in THF, 0 °C, 0.5–1 h. Mechanistic probing by three experiments: (1) stoichiometric reaction of NiBr₂ with 2 equiv n-OctMgCl to test for C–C homocoupling vs. dialkyl-Ni formation; (2) attempted oxidative addition of n-decyl bromide to Ni(COD)₂ in THF/1,3-BD; (3) radical-clock substrate (cyclopropylmethyl bromide) under catalytic conditions to probe for radical intermediates. A reaction mechanism involving an η¹,η³-octadienediylnickelate intermediate is proposed.

[S0-5] conclusion_reach: The paper claims broad scope for primary alkyl electrophiles (alkyl Cl, Br, I, OTs) and primary alkyl Grignard reagents under catalytic conditions (1–3 mol% Ni), with yields of 81–100% (GC) for alkyl bromides, 87–96% for tosylates, and 79–96% for alkyl chlorides.
