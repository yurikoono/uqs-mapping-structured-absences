"Selective carbon–carbon bond formation by cross-coupling of Grignard reagents with organic halides. Catalysis by nickel–phosphine complexes,"
*J. Am. Chem. Soc.*, **1972**, *94*, 4374–4376.

### Background

# Item 4. Is it fair to state that substrate-matched head-to-head benchmarking against predecessor catalysts was not sufficiently identified in the surveyed academic literature?

### Background
Reaction-development papers often state that a new catalyst or new condition outperforms prior methods. They do not always perform **substrate-matched head-to-head benchmarking** in which **the same substrates, the same evaluation axis, and the same conditions** are compared against predecessor systems. For Kumada 1972's NiCl₂(dpe) system, the natural comparators include Corriu's Ni(acac)₂ system and Tamura–Kochi's Fe catalyst system. Tamao 1976 (Full Paper I) and Monnereau 2009 provide partial comparisons.

**"Internal screening" vs "external benchmark":** Many literature comparison tables are either (a) **within-system ligand/condition screening** (e.g., Tamao 1976's dpe / dppm / dppb / PPh₃ comparison within the same lineage), or (b) **one-sided comparisons where the predecessor catalyst is tested only under the new catalyst's optimal conditions**, rather than (c) **a multi-metric comparison on a fair common ground—identical substrate lots, identical operator, multiple evaluation axes (yield, TOF, robustness, cost)**. The manuscript's claim is restricted to (c)-type head-to-head benchmarking and does not deny the existence of (a)/(b)-type comparisons.

**Relation to Item 3:** Item 3 deals with **"vertical reliability" (within-reaction reproducibility)**; this item deals with **"horizontal fairness" (cross-system same-axis comparison)**. They are independent dimensions of the manuscript's data-architecture audit.

*Bull. Chem. Soc. Jpn.*, **1976**, *49*, 1958–1969.
DOI: [10.1246/bcsj.49.1958](https://doi.org/10.1246/bcsj.49.1958)

*J. Am. Chem. Soc.*, **1975**, *97*, 7262–7270.
DOI: [10.1021/ja00858a011](https://doi.org/10.1021/ja00858a011)

### Background
Morrell and Kochi 1975 is a classical mechanistic study that placed arylalkylnickel(II) species at the centre of the mechanistic picture for nickel-catalysed cross-coupling. Therefore, although the **conceptual building blocks** of the catalytic cycle (aryl-alkyl Ni(II) intermediate, reductive elimination) are shared, the kinetics specific to the dppe-Ni system and the magnesium-salt byproduct effects (Item 8) are *not* tested in Kochi 1975.

# Item 6. Can the 2021 paper by Mazet and co-workers be treated as a "decisive mechanistic filling for the dppe-Ni system"? Is the wording "49 years after" appropriate?

### Reference
**P.-A. Poisson, G. Tran, C. Besnard, C. Mazet**,

At the same time, the **ligand class (bidentate bisphosphine)** and **catalytic-cycle backbone (Ni(0)/Ni(II))** are identical, so the manuscript treats Mazet 2021 as **"system-isomorphic" at the catalyst-identity level** rather than at the leaving-group level.

**Scope of the "49-year" timeline:** This timeline is restricted to **decisive experimental demonstrations**. Computational (DFT) mechanistic predictions over the same 49 years are extensive but are categorised separately, because the manuscript is tracking the *experimental-evidence arrival* timeline.

### Claim to evaluate
The manuscript treats Mazet 2021 as a **"decisive mechanistic filling for a system isomorphic to dppe-Ni, appearing 49 years after the original 1972 communication."**

**Important limitation:** The "49 years" refers specifically to the **decisive experimental demonstration in a system isomorphic to dppe-Ni**. It **does not** mean that no mechanistic work occurred in between. Earlier mechanistic contributions in related Ni systems exist (Morrell-Kochi 1975), as do partial mechanistic fillings via different pathways (Breitenfeld 2014 radical Ni(I)/Ni(III), Chen 2019, Dawson 2020).

### Questions
1. Is it fair to treat Mazet 2021 as a major and relatively direct mechanistic study for the dppe-Ni Kumada system?
2. Is "decisive mechanistic filling" too strong? Would "most direct" or "system-isomorphic mechanistic support" be safer?
3. Is the "49 years after" wording potentially misleading (the manuscript currently couples it with the limitation above)?
4. Should the distinction between broader mechanistic studies (e.g., Morrell-Kochi 1975) and direct dppe-Ni evidence (Mazet 2021) be emphasised more in the main text?

6. The "49 years" timeline is scoped to **experimental** decisive evidence; computational (DFT) mechanistic work over the same period is in a separate category. Should this scope be made explicit (e.g., in the figure caption or the main-text wording)?

# Item 9. Is it natural to motivate a homogeneous-vs-heterogeneous question for the Kumada reaction from the "black mixture" observation?

### Reference
**Kumada 1972 (see Item 1).**

### Background
Kumada and other Ni-catalysed reactions can produce a black mixture or visible metal deposition. Kumada 1972 itself contains a reference to a **"black mixture,"** and no systematic follow-up has appeared in the subsequent Kumada lineage. This raises the question of whether the active species is truly a homogeneous Ni complex or whether heterogeneous, nanoparticle, or decomposition contributions are involved as the actual catalytic species.

**Roles of the three proposed tests:** The mercury-drop test poisons metal-nanoparticle surfaces; the hot filtration test isolates the contribution of insoluble black species; TEM directly visualises any nanocolloids in the active mixture. The three tests are *complementary* and provide independent lines of evidence.

**Ni-specific risk of mercury-drop test:** Unlike for Pd or Pt, in Ni systems mercury can drain electrons from even homogeneous Ni complexes (especially Ni(0) species), causing false-positive poisoning. The three-test combination is therefore essential—mercury alone is not enough.

### Claim to evaluate
The manuscript proposes, on the basis of this "black mixture" observation, **whether the dpe-Ni Kumada reaction is truly homogeneous or whether heterogeneous contributions are operative** as a still-unresolved question. # Item 10. Is it reasonable to raise actual operating cathode potential during constant-current electrolysis as a missing-data point?

### Reference
**P. Li, Z. Zhu, C. Guo, G. Kou, S. Wang, P. Xie, D. Ma, T. Feng, Y. Wang, Y. Qiu**,
"Nickel-electrocatalysed C(sp³)–C(sp³) cross-coupling of unactivated alkyl halides,"
*Nat. Catal.*, **2024**, *7*, 412–421.
DOI: [10.1038/s41929-024-01118-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41929-024-01118-3)

### Background
Under constant-current electrolysis, the applied current is fixed but the actual operating electrode potential drifts continuously as the solution composition evolves. This means the reducing driving force (overpotential) seen by the Ni complex changes over time, which obstructs a strict identification of the active species (Ni(0), Ni(I), or over-reduced species). The manuscript identifies that Li 2024 does not measure the actual operating potential under 5 mA constant-current electrolysis, reports a **360 mV discrepancy between DFT-calculated Ni(I)/Ni(0) and SWV (square-wave voltammetry) data**, and uses **reference electrodes inconsistently across figures**—a double data-integrity issue.

**Measurement set-up:** A standard two-electrode synthetic cell cannot measure the actual operating potential. A **three-electrode cell** (working / counter / reference electrodes, with a Luggin capillary placing the reference electrode close to the working electrode) and a potentiostat are required for in-situ potentiometric monitoring during constant-current electrolysis.

**Requirement for accurate thermodynamic interpretation:** In organic solvent systems containing concentrated Grignard reagents and supporting electrolyte, the **iR drop (ohmic loss)** is the dominant error source for potential measurements. **iR compensation by the potentiostat** is necessary for accurate thermodynamic discussion.

### Claim to evaluate
The manuscript proposes that **measurement of the actual operating cathode potential during Li 2024's 5 mA constant-current electrolysis is a mechanistically important missing datum**. The proposed experiment is **direct in-situ potential measurement at the working electrode during catalysis**.

### Questions

4. Does this question sit too far from the core Kumada case (classical organometallic mechanism)?
