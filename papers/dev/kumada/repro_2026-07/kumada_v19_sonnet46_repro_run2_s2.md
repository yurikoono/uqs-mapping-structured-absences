**Immediate horizon (1–5 years post-publication, ~1973–1977):** The absences most immediately addressable were the mechanistic cycle verification items (A-1 cluster). Within five years of this communication, the Kumada group and, in parallel, the Corriu and Hayashi groups published detailed mechanistic studies establishing oxidative addition, transmetalation, and reductive elimination as genuine elementary steps in the (dpe)Ni and related systems, using stoichiometric reaction characterization, ³¹P NMR monitoring, and kinetic measurements. The black-mixture/catalyst-state ambiguity (U-1) was partially addressed by these mechanistic studies, which confirmed that well-defined Ni-phosphine complexes do serve as precatalysts — though the heterogeneous/homogeneous boundary remained a subject of investigation in later palladium-catalyzed cross-coupling literature. The beta-hydride elimination suppression mystery (B-7) was addressed as part of the mechanistic work, with the relative stability of Ni(II) over Ni(IV) and ligand geometry being invoked to rationalize suppression.

**Medium horizon (5–20 years, ~1977–1992):** The scope restriction to sp² halides (B-1) and the ligand activity order (B-2) became major research questions as the field expanded. The Kumada coupling's substrate scope was mapped more completely, sp³ electrophiles were found to be less successful, and the diphosphine ligand landscape was elaborated by systematic studies. The cis-selectivity anomaly in vinyl halide reactions (A-1 anchor) was resolved by the discovery that vinyl-Ni intermediates undergo geometric isomerization, connecting to the broader problem of stereocontrol in cross-coupling. The selectivity claim (B-3) became quantitatively established as the field developed analytical standards for Pd-catalyzed cross-coupling (which superseded Kumada coupling for many applications), and homo-coupling quantification became routine.

**Long horizon (20–50 years, ~1992–2022):** The concurrently claimed priority (C-4, Corriu-Masse) was eventually examined historically: the 1972 Corriu and Tamao/Sumitani/Kumada papers are now jointly cited as the founding communications of transition-metal-catalyzed cross-coupling, the priority question resolved in favor of simultaneous independent discovery. The catalyst-state question (U-1) gained new relevance with the recognition that Pd-catalyzed cross-couplings often involve Pd(0) nanoparticles as the true active species — a heterogeneity question that this paper's "black mixture" observation presaged but could not resolve. The epistemological gap between a communication-format scope demonstration and a fully characterized mechanistic catalyst has been retrospectively recognized as a defining feature of early cross-coupling literature, and the 2010 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for cross-coupling (Heck, Negishi, Suzuki) crystallized the historical significance of the 1972 papers while also foregrounding what had been left implicit.

（English translation）

Viewed as a whole, the cluster of questions this paper did not ask reveals a coherent interlocking structure. The claim of selective nickel-bisphosphine-catalyzed cross-coupling of Grignard reagents rests on three mutually reinforcing silences. First, selectivity is evidenced indirectly through the absence of reported byproduct yields, not through quantification. Second, the proposed catalytic cycle (eqs 2–4) functions as a model-based explanation, unsupported by direct observation of any elementary step in the NiCl₂(dpe) system. Third, the "black mixture" observation in Example 1 is recorded but neither identified nor evaluated, despite being potentially inconsistent with the homogeneous molecular catalyst cycle proposed. These three silences interlock: if the true catalyst is colloidal Ni(0) rather than an intact Ni-phosphine complex, each step of the proposed cycle requires reexamination; if the R–R byproduct predicted by eq 3 is in fact produced, the selectivity claim requires recalibration. What the paper provided was a first demonstration opening the door to a new chemical possibility, and all further questions were explicitly — and legitimately — assigned to subsequent work. The topography of unasked questions constitutes one of the earliest recorded instances of the research mode in which method demonstration precedes and authorizes mechanistic investigation.
