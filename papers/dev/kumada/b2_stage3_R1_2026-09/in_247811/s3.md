## Tamao, Sumitani & Kumada 1972 (J. Am. Chem. Soc., communication)

### §1 Claim Architecture

**Note:** Stage 2 D-1 is not present in the record. The following is reconstructed from Part I items, Part II SRGs, and the PDF.

**Core claim:** This communication establishes a new preparative cross-coupling method in which a Grignard reagent reacts with a vinyl or aryl (Csp²) [REDACTED: verbatim source text; see the source paper via its DOI] nickel–diphosphine complex (optimally [NiCl₂(dpe)]) to give the cross-coupled product in generally high yield. The **Novelty pillar** rests on two connected claims: (i) selective cross-coupling over homo-coupling—in contrast to prior iron, copper, and silver methods—enabled by the nickel–diphosphine catalyst, and (ii) unexpectedly high yields even with β-H-containing primary alkylmagnesium reagents and especially with chloride electrophiles. The **Utility pillar** rests on the preparative accessibility of compounds (alkylbenzenes, alkenylnaphthalenes, stilbenes, vinylidenes) not readily obtainable by conventional means in a single step. A working-hypothesis catalytic cycle (Ni(II) → diorganonickel 1 → haloorganonickel 2 → mixed diorganonickel 3 → cross-product + regenerated 2) is proposed to explain selectivity.

**Layered structure:** The claim architecture has two distinguishable layers. **Layer A (synthetic/preparative):** The reactions produce high yields of cross-coupled products (Table I); this is directly supported by reported yields and physical data for the two exemplified products. **Layer B (mechanistic):** The Ni(II) cycle with intermediates 1, 2, 3 explains the selectivity; this is entirely inferential. The two layers are not tightly coupled: Layer A can stand independently of Layer B's validation. A refutation of the mechanistic assignment (e.g., demonstration that Ni(0) is the active species) would not collapse Layer A. However, selectivity claims in Layer A (β-H suppression, Csp² restriction) draw on mechanistic reasoning from Layer B, making their *explanations* dependent on B even if the *observations* are in A. The inter-layer dependency is therefore asymmetric: A does not require B to hold, but A's selectivity explanations do require B's cycle to be at least plausible. A single refutation of Layer B alone does not collapse the paper; a refutation of Layer A's selectivity claim (e.g., large homo-coupling product quantities) would collapse both.

### §4 Era-Norm Annotation

**Note:** Stage 2 D-2 (format_exploitation, timing_pressure) and Stage 2 D-3 (journal_constraint, field_competition_state) are not present in the record. The following classification draws on BIB-4 (year = 1972) and BIB-7 (format = communication, 3-page JACS communication) from the PDF, and contextual evidence in the paper itself (Note Added in Proof about Corriu–Masse signals timing pressure; "working hypothesis" label signals deliberate restraint).

**communication-era tolerated:**

- *Working-hypothesis mechanism without intermediate isolation (items 1–4, SRG-1, SRG-14)*: In 1972 JACS communications reporting first examples of a new catalytic transformation, proposing a mechanistic cycle by analogy with stoichiometric precedent, with explicit "working hypothesis" labelling and deferral to future studies, was standard practice; the penultimate sentence of this paper explicitly acknowledges the limitations.

- *Abbreviated product characterisation (items 51, 61, 67, SRG-13)*: The "characterized by glpc, nmr, ir, and/or mass spectra" shorthand and the omission of numerical spectral data from the communication body were standard for 3-page JACS communications in 1972; supplementary experimental sections did not exist as a requirement, and full data were expected in the follow-up full paper.

- *Conditions labeled "not necessarily optimum" (item 39, SRG-15)*: For an initial communication announcing a new reaction type, deferring optimisation to a follow-up paper was expected and accepted; the explicit caveat signals appropriate honesty rather than evasion.

- *No head-to-head comparison with prior methods (items 52, 56, SRG-18)*: Initial communications in 1972 were not required to provide direct experimental comparison with prior art; citing literature reports of prior limitations was the accepted standard.

- *Deferred mechanistic and stereochemical studies (item 71)*: The closing deferral ("Investigations are continuing…") was the standard communication-format close for a new reaction discovery, signalling an active full-paper programme.

**modern-era deficient:**

- *No carbon balance or homo-coupling quantification (items 49, 50, SRG-3)*: A selectivity claim in a modern submission requires explicit quantification of all products and a molar balance; "selective cross-coupling" without reporting homo-coupling byproduct yields would be rejected by reviewers as an unsupported central assertion.

- *Csp² restriction asserted via external references without failure example in this system (items 21, 22, SRG-7)*: A boundary-of-scope claim ("only Csp² halides can be used") requires at minimum one attempted and failed alkyl halide reaction in the current system; citing external Ni or Pd systems of different composition is not accepted as direct evidence in modern practice.

- *Ligand activity ranking without quantitative data for the ranked catalysts (items 12, 13, SRG-4)*: A modern submission claiming an activity order would require yield or rate data for each catalyst; a qualitative ranking derived from a single substrate pair is insufficient for a structure-activity conclusion.

- *"Definitely superior" solvent claim without comparative data (item 43, SRG-9)*: Modern submissions require direct numerical comparison for any strong comparative assertion; "definitely" implies a confidence level not supported by the total absence of ether-vs.-THF yield data in this system.

- *Mechanism of β-H elimination suppression unexplained and unquantified (item 28, SRG-11)*: A modern cross-coupling paper claiming β-H elimination suppression as a key advance would be required to quantify alkene byproduct or provide a kinetic mechanistic comparison.

**Items belonging to both tags:**

- *Working-hypothesis mechanism, SRG-14 (items 1, SRG-1)*: Communication-era tolerated (explicit "working hypothesis" label, standard 1972 practice), but modern-era deficient in that the follow-up full paper did not resolve RI-06; the automatic-resolution expectation was only partially fulfilled across the subsequent literature.

In SRG-5 (RI-08), the situation is more primitive than PRI-11 specifies.
