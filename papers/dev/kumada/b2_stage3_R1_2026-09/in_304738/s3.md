### §1 Claim Architecture

**Core claim.** The paper reports a preparative method for selective cross-coupling of Grignard reagents with vinyl or aryl (Csp²) halides, catalyzed by Ni-diphosphine complexes, with "generally very high" yields even from alkylmagnesium reagents bearing β-hydrogen atoms. The **Novelty pillar** rests on two axes: (i) scope superiority over the closest prior catalytic system (Tamura-Kochi iron catalyst, limited to alkenyl halides) extended to aryl halides, and (ii) a catalyst design principle — bidentate diphosphine ligands enforce a cis-diorganonickel intermediate (complex 3) that enables cross selectivity. The **Utility pillar** rests on preparative isolation of otherwise inaccessible compounds (di-n-butylbenzene isomers, α-vinylnaphthalene) at gram scale in a single step from cheap Grignard reagents and organic chlorides.

**Layered structure.** The novelty rests on at least three independently refutable layers: (a) scope superiority over iron catalyst (vinyl + aryl vs. only alkenyl halides); (b) high yields compatible with β-H-bearing alkylmagnesium reagents; (c) catalyst designability via bidentate phosphine tuning (dpe > dpp > dmpe > monodentate). A single failed substrate does not collapse all three layers simultaneously — layers (a)–(c) are logically independent. However, layers (a) and (c) share a common vulnerability: both rest on the same unverified mechanistic assumption (cis geometry of complex 3), so a disproof of that structural hypothesis would weaken both simultaneously while leaving layer (b) intact. The paper's architecture is therefore partially, not fully, modular.

**VQ-2 (Method) — Fail.** On the premise that VQ-1 RI-02 is non-fatal: RI-01 remains unresolved. **VQ-4 (Benchmark) — Fail.** Inherits VQ-3 failure. ### §4 Era-Norm Annotation

**communication-era tolerated:**

- **B-2** (GC conditions absent — internal standard, column, temperature program): The 1972 JACS communication format (D-3 journal_constraint: approximately two pages main text) structurally precluded detailed method reporting; chromatographic reproducibility standards were not codified at the communication level in 1972 analytical practice.
- **B-6** (inconsistent yield basis — Grignard in Example 2, halide in Table I): D-2 format_exploitation and D-3 journal_constraint confirm the format compressed experimental detail; uniform yield basis specification across a communication's table and examples was not enforced at 1972 JACS submission level.
- **B-7** (TON not calculated despite catalyst loading range 10⁻²–10⁻³): Turnover number reporting became a benchmarking standard for transition-metal-catalyzed reactions substantially after 1972; specifying catalyst/substrate ratio in mol/mol was the prevailing practice for a new catalytic method communication.
- **B-9** (ether "definitely superior" to THF without comparative data): D-2 format_exploitation identifies this as a communication-format omission zone; a comparative solvent entry in Table I would have required additional space not available in the two-page limit.
- **A-1, A-2** (explicit deferrals — "further studies required," "investigations continuing"): D-3 entry_criteria confirms that in 1972, mechanistic completeness was not an entry criterion for preparative catalysis communications; the JACS communication genre explicitly anticipated a full paper to follow, making deferral of mechanistic and scope details structurally appropriate.

**modern-era deficient:**

- **B-3** (selectivity claim without any homo-coupling quantitation): Any modern claim of "selective" cross-coupling requires quantitative byproduct analysis or at minimum a "not detected" statement with GC detection limit; the D-2 format_exploitation note that the communication format enables compression of mechanistic weaknesses directly conflicts with present-day VQ-2 requirements for RI-01 (reporting integrity).
- **U-1** (eq 3 predicts R–R, absent from Table I without acknowledgment): A catalytic cycle that explicitly predicts a byproduct at one elementary step must account for that byproduct's absence in product tables; the logical inconsistency between eq 3 and Table I would not pass peer review today.
- **U-4** (Ni oxidation states unspecified in proposed catalytic cycle): Present-day organometallic catalysis papers require formal oxidation state notation and electron count at every step of a proposed cycle, even in short communications; a cycle without oxidation states does not function as a mechanistic hypothesis.
- **B-8** (cis geometry of complex 3 attributed from activity order without structural support): A mechanistic attribution connecting ligand geometry to catalytic selectivity requires at minimum a spectroscopic experiment (³¹P NMR of complex 3 analog, or reference to a crystal structure) under contemporary standards.
- **B-1** (sp² scope restriction "Only Csp² halides can be used" without failed examples): Contemporary substrate-scope papers require experimental data for claimed scope boundaries; asserting a negative boundary without failed examples is insufficient by modern peer-review standards (RI-07 extended use: negative boundary claim).

**Items in both tags (tolerated then, deficient now):**

- **B-2** (GC conditions): Tolerated in 1972 under the format constraint; deficient now because current reproducibility and metrology standards require full chromatographic method specification for GC-yield data.
- **B-6** (inconsistent yield basis): Tolerated in 1972 under the format constraint; deficient now because a consistent denominator across all Table I entries and all examples is required for any cross-entry performance comparison.

### §6 Contribution to institutional design

**1. New representative case for RI-01 — cross-procedure denominator mismatch subtype.**
B-6 (Example 2 yield based on Grignard reagent, Table I entries based on organic halide) is a specific instance of RI-01 internal inconsistency that is distinct from the within-entry inconsistency described in the v0.2a subtype note. **2. Canonical exemplar for self-contradictory-absence CLA — mechanism-predicted byproduct absent from product table.**

This co-occurrence illustrates the general principle: when a model is imported (RI-06) and its intermediates are uncharacterized (RI-03), resolving the model-import issue without characterizing the intermediates leaves the mechanism unsubstantiated at the molecular level.

**6. PRI-11 boundary clarification (no new PRI candidate).**
PRI-11 (Mechanism–kinetics decoupling) does not apply here, because no kinetic data (rate orders, kobs, induction periods) are reported at all. PRI-11 requires that rate orders have been observed but are not algebraically connected to the proposed cycle; in this paper, the entire kinetic layer is absent (RI-03 total absence of kinetic data), placing the issue at RI-03 rather than PRI-11. This case clarifies the RI-03/PRI-11 boundary: **PRI-11 requires partial kinetic characterization (rate orders present but mechanistically disconnected); RI-03 applies when kinetic characterization is entirely absent.** No new PRI candidate is proposed.
