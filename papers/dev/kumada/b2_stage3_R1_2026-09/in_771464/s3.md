# Re-certification Assessment Sheet for Scientific Knowledge

## Tamao, Sumitani & Kumada 1972 (Journal of the American Chemical Society, communication)

### §1 Claim Architecture

The paper's **Novelty pillar** is the first preparative-grade, catalytic, selective cross-coupling of Grignard reagents (including β-H-bearing alkyl Grignard reagents) with sp² organic halides — particularly chlorides, which prior work had seldom used — using Ni-diphosphine complexes, claiming synthetic access to compounds "not readily accessible by conventional methods." The **Utility pillar** is demonstrated by ten substrate-scope entries (Table I, 76–98% yield) and two preparative-scale worked examples (m-di-n-butylbenzene 94%; α-vinylnaphthalene 80%). The paper also contributes a mechanistic rationalization (a three-step Ni(II)/Ni(0) catalytic cycle, eqs 2–4) as a "working hypothesis" grounding the selectivity observation.

The claim exhibits a **three-layer structure** identified in D-1: **Layer 1** (synthetic utility — high-yield selective cross-coupling) is independently refutable by yield failure or demonstration of significant homo-coupling; **Layer 2** (mechanistic hypothesis — Ni(II)/Ni(0) cycle via diorganonickel intermediates 1, 2, 3) is explicitly labeled provisional and independently refutable without collapsing Layer 1; **Layer 3** (scope boundary — Csp² restriction as a defined feature) is refutable by showing sp³ halides function. The independence of Layer 2 from Layer 1 confers genuine robustness: a subsequent revision of the mechanism does not invalidate the preparative discovery. However, a structural dependency runs in one direction — Layer 1's selectivity claim (cross over homo coupling) is partially buttressed by Layer 2's mechanistic framing, such that a refutation of Layer 2 would weaken but not collapse Layer 1. A single refutation of Layer 1 (e.g., discovery of significant homo-coupling in the Table I reactions) would collapse the core novelty claim entirely.

### §4 Era-Norm Annotation

**communication-era tolerated:**

- **B-3 (conditions not optimum)**: In 1972 JACS communications, the disclaimer "experimental conditions are not necessarily optimum" was an accepted hedge; optimization tables were not required for a first announcement of a new preparative method; the disclaimer was understood as a promissory note for the follow-up Full Paper.
- **B-4 (homo-coupling not quantified)**: In 1972, selectivity in a preparative communication was routinely established by reporting product yield alone; GC quantification of byproduct profiles was not a standard requirement; the absence of R–R data was genre-conventional.
- **B-5 (bromide/iodide not shown)**: Leaving-group comparison tables were not standard in 1972 JACS two-page communications; footnote 18 acknowledging that [REDACTED: verbatim source quotation; see the source paper via its DOI] was sufficient genre disclosure.
- **B-6 (THF vs ether not tabulated)**: Qualitative solvent preference claims ("definitely superior") without tabular data were acceptable in 1972 communications; such a claim was understood as an empirical observation to be documented in the follow-up Full Paper.
- **B-7 (characterization ambiguity, "and/or")**: The "and/or" formula for product characterization was acceptable genre shorthand in 1972 JACS communications; per-entry characterization tables were not standard practice at this stage of a program.
- **B-8 (no replication data)**: Single-experiment scope tables were the norm for 1972 JACS communications introducing new preparative methods; error bars were not required for a first announcement.

**modern-era deficient:**

- **B-4 (homo-coupling not quantified)**: A paper claiming "selective" cross-coupling in a current submission must report byproduct profiles by calibrated GC-MS or quantitative NMR; the self-contradictory absence (U-1 — eq 3 explicitly predicts R–R release) makes this deficiency structurally acute in any modern assessment.
- **B-7 (characterization ambiguity)**: Modern submissions require complete characterization data (¹H and ¹³C NMR with chemical shifts and coupling constants, HRMS, IR) for each new compound in a dedicated data table; "and/or" is not acceptable in any current journal.
- **U-1 (R–R yield absent despite mechanistic prediction)**: The RI-08 internal-inconsistency subtype — that cross-coupling yield and homo-coupling yield from intermediate 1 are mathematically related via eq 3 but only one is reported — would be flagged by modern reviewers and would require resolution before acceptance.
- **B-1 + RI-07 (sp² restriction without failure data)**: Modern scope papers require representative negative controls; a sentence in footnote 17 citing a general textbook is insufficient to establish a scope boundary for a specific catalyst system.
- **B-8 (no replication)**: Modern synthetic papers require ≥3 independent replicates for any entry claimed as preparatively representative; single-run yields presented as "generally very high" are not acceptable without uncertainty quantification.

**Items in both tags (tolerated then, deficient now):**
- **B-4** (homo-coupling not quantified): tolerated by 1972 communication convention, modern-era deficient by current selectivity reporting norms.
- **B-8** (no replication): tolerated in 1972 communication, modern-era deficient by current reproducibility standards.

### §6 Contribution to institutional design

**1. Labeling mismatch confirmed (co-occurs with RI-06).**
The paper provides a textbook instance of the labeling mismatch pattern. The hypothesis label appears only in the final sentence of the mechanistic section: [REDACTED: verbatim source quotation; see the source paper via its DOI] The preceding narrative uses factual verbs throughout — "reacts with," "forms," "is subsequently converted to," "is released," "is regenerated to complete the catalytic cycle." By the time the hypothesis label appears, the reader has processed the catalytic cycle as established fact. Position of hypothesis label: concentrated in the final sentence of the mechanistic section, after the entire factual-verb narrative. Co-occurs with RI-06: the same narrative that presents the cycle as fact is the one that imports it by analogy from a different Ni complex system; the labeling mismatch and the model import operate together to frame an unvalidated analogy as a confirmed mechanism.

**2. Automatic-resolution pattern via communication→Full Paper transition confirmed.**
RI-07 (sp² restriction without failure data, B-1), RI-01 (mixed yield basis, B-2), and RI-08 sub-components (B-3, B-5, B-6, B-8) all carry the era-norm toleration that corresponds to the communication format. The paper explicitly signals the intended resolution pathway: A-3 ([REDACTED: verbatim source quotation; see the source paper via its DOI]) and A-1 ([REDACTED: verbatim source quotation; see the source paper via its DOI]) are both promissory notes for the Full Paper series. Correspondence with §4 era-norm annotation: B-3, B-5, B-6, B-8 are all communication-era tolerated precisely because A-3 and A-1 function as the promissory notes; these items were designed to auto-resolve in the follow-up Full Paper series (Tamao–Kumada 1974 et seq.). This confirms the automatic-resolution pattern and identifies A-1 and A-3 as the primary carriers of the deferral.

**3. RI co-occurrence pattern: RI-03 + RI-06 in SRG-2 requires both to be resolved.**
In SRG-2, RI-03 (intermediates 1, 2, 3 unobserved) and RI-06 (catalytic cycle imported by analogy from (dipy)Ni stoichiometric model) co-occur. Resolving only RI-06 via crossover experiment confirms the mononuclear selectivity mechanism but does not demonstrate that the proposed intermediates exist or have the proposed properties. Resolving only RI-03 by isolating one intermediate does not demonstrate that the (dipy)Ni-to-(dpe)Ni analogy holds for the complete catalytic cycle. Both must be resolved to certify VQ-3. This is the canonical RI co-occurrence structure where resolving one RI is insufficient: the two RIs address different aspects of the same mechanistic gap (what is the intermediate? and is the analogy valid?), and only their joint resolution closes the mechanism.

**4. New case for RI-08 internal-inconsistency subtype from U-1.**
U-1 (self-contradictory absence) provides a new case-specific application of the RI-08 internal-inconsistency subtype. The subtype note specifies "yield and conversion showing mathematical inconsistency"; U-1 extends this to "mechanism-predicted byproduct yield and reported product yield showing unverified mathematical relationship." Eq 3 of the proposed cycle explicitly produces R–R as a stoichiometric co-product of intermediate 1 being intercepted by R'X''; therefore cross-coupling yield and R–R yield are mathematically linked through the cycle's own stoichiometry, yet only one quantity is reported. This is a more structurally significant case than a simple yield/conversion inconsistency: the missing quantity is predicted by the paper's own mechanism, making its absence self-contradictory rather than merely incomplete. This application should be added to the EP-08a case-specific implementations as the "mechanism-predicted byproduct" subtype: when a proposed mechanism generates a co-product in a specified stoichiometric step, the yield of that co-product must be reported alongside the primary product yield.

**5. PRI-11 (Mechanism–kinetics decoupling) confirmed at a more primitive evidentiary level.**
PRI-11 is defined as "mechanism is supported only by agreement with observed rate orders, without an algebraic derivation of the rate law from the proposed catalytic cycle." This paper represents a case of PRI-11 at a lower evidentiary rung: the mechanism (eqs 2–4) is supported not even by rate-order agreement, but only by analogy to eq 1 (a stoichiometric (dipy)Ni reaction, RI-06) and by the observed substrate-scope pattern. No rate orders are reported. This establishes that PRI-11 exists on a spectrum: (a) mechanism by analogy only (this paper, 1972 communication); (b) mechanism by rate-order agreement without algebraic derivation (Breitenfeld 2014, Pengfei 2024, per PRI-11 provisional record). The unifying feature across the spectrum is the absence of an algebraic derivation of the rate law from the proposed cycle. The current case — mechanism by analogy without rate orders — extends PRI-11's scope and supports its promotion from provisional to confirmed status, since the pattern now covers at least three independent papers across different evidentiary levels.

**6. No new PRI candidate detected.**
All patterns observed — labeling mismatch (confirmed RI-06 co-occurrence), automatic-resolution via communication→Full Paper transition, RI-03 + RI-06 co-occurrence, RI-08 internal-inconsistency subtype (mechanism-predicted byproduct), and PRI-11 spectrum extension — are fully captured by existing codes and subtypes. No pattern requiring a new PRI-NN candidate was identified in this case.
