### §1 Claim Architecture

**Input:** Stage 2 D-1 — **absent from record.** Core claim reconstructed from PDF and item set.

**Core claim:** The paper reports that dihalodiphosphinenickel complexes — in particular [NiCl₂(dpe)] and [NiCl₂(dpp)] — catalyse selective cross-coupling of Grignard reagents with vinyl or aryl halides in diethyl ether. The **Novelty pillar** is the demonstration that bidentate chelating phosphine ligands on Ni(II) permit selective cross-coupling over homo-coupling and disproportionation that are said to plague all prior transition-metal systems, and that the cross-coupling is effective even with aryl chlorides (high-reactivity chlorides) and with β-hydrogen-bearing alkyl Grignard reagents. The **Utility pillar** is the one-step preparative access to otherwise "hardly accessible" unsaturated compounds (alkylbenzenes, styrenes, stilbenes, 1,1-diphenylethylene) from inexpensive, available starting materials.

**Layered structure:** The paper presents five empirical "features" (Items 8–9, 11–13, 14–16, 37–38, 57) and one mechanistic rationalisation (catalytic cycle, eqs 2–4; Items 1–7, 29, 40–41, 47, 49, 52, 55). These layers are **partially independent**: the preparative utility claim (Table I) would survive even if the proposed catalytic cycle (eqs 2–4) were replaced by an entirely different mechanism. Conversely, the mechanistic layer depends on the preparative data for its substrate scope. However, the selectivity claim (selective cross- vs. homo-coupling) is shared across both layers: it belongs simultaneously to the preparative-utility layer (high yields; "seldom employed in synthetic practice" due to byproducts) and to the mechanistic layer (the cycle is designed to explain selectivity). A single refutation of selectivity — e.g., demonstration that homo-coupling products are substantial — would collapse both layers. The remaining features (ligand order, solvent preference, stereospecificity) are independent sub-claims that could be individually refuted without collapsing the core cross-coupling claim.

### §4 Era-Norm Annotation

**Input:** Stage 2 D-2 (format_exploitation, timing_pressure) and D-3 (journal_constraint, field_competition_state): **absent from record.** Annotation derived from PDF (BIB-4 year = 1972; BIB-7 format = JACS communication) and item content.

**communication-era tolerated:**

* **Absent mechanistic intermediate characterisation (SRG-1; Items 2, 3, 4, 40, 52):** JACS communications in 1972 did not require isolation of catalytic intermediates; a full mechanistic paper was the expected successor, and the paper explicitly promises [REDACTED: verbatim source quotation; see the source paper via its DOI] This deferral was an accepted communication-format convention.

* **Absent replicated yields with uncertainty (SRG-3, SRG-12; Items 18, 19, 37):** Single-run glpc yield reporting without error bars or byproduct identification was standard practice for synthetic communications in the early 1970s; reproducibility was an expectation for the full paper, not the communication.

* **Absent failure examples for scope boundaries (SRG-4; Items 9, 21–25):** Negative-outcome reporting in scope-defining experiments was not required in 1972 communications; the restriction to sp² halides was expected to be elaborated in subsequent full papers.

* **Absent direct experimental comparison with Corriu–Masse concurrent communication (SRG-8; Item 30):** The Corriu–Masse communication arrived after the Kumada paper was submitted (Note Added in Proof); experimental comparison with a concurrent publication in a communication was operationally impossible under the rapid-publication timeline.

* **X-ray structural proof of ligand geometry (SRG-6; Items 7, 12, 13):** Routine X-ray crystallography of Ni organometallic intermediates was not standard for organometallic communications in 1972; crystal structure determination was a dedicated study, not a characterisation tool in a synthetic communication.

**modern-era deficient:**

* **Mass balance not closed for any entry (SRG-12; Items 19, 34, 37):** Modern analytical standards require accounting for all carbon for any selectivity claim; the 22-percentage-point yield gap between EtMgBr and n-BuMgBr (98% vs. 76%) with no byproduct identification is not acceptable in a contemporary report of a "selective" reaction.

* **Active catalyst identity not established; black solid uncharacterised (SRG-7; Items 17, 29, 41):** Modern catalysis standards require mercury drop tests, hotfiltering experiments, or comparison with defined Ni(0) precatalysts to confirm homogeneous catalysis — especially when a black solid is observed; this requirement has been standard since the 1990s.

* **Ether superiority "definite" without quantitative comparison (SRG-11; Item 57):** Modern claim language requires quantitative backing; "definitely superior" without a single yield comparison in the alternative solvent is insufficient by contemporary standards.

* **Superiority over organocuprates and Tamura–Kochi system without matched comparison (SRG-8; Items 31, 32, 43, 53):** Contemporary reports of new synthetic methods require at least one experimental benchmark against the best available alternative under matched conditions; citation alone is now insufficient.

* **Scope boundary "only Csp² halides" without negative-outcome data (SRG-4, CLA preparative utility; Items 9, 39):** Modern boundary claims require explicit negative data; the assertion is now classified as RI-07 (Unsupported boundary) by contemporary standards even if it was accepted at the time as a communication-format limitation.

*(Note: Items 30, Items 2–4 fall in both tags as "tolerated then but deficient now" — the absence of mechanistic intermediate characterisation was tolerated at the time; the same absence would be a modern-era deficiency for any VQ-3 claim.)*

### §6 Contribution to institutional design

**1. RI-06 cycle-level import — new representative case with maximal electronic divergence.**
SRG-10 provides a clean, historically significant case of whole-cycle RI-06 import. The imported precedent (dipy-Ni, refs 11–12) differs from the dpe-Ni system in both donor atom (N vs. P) and electronic character (π-acceptor bidentate vs. σ-donor bidentate). The electronic divergence is qualitatively large — large enough to alter Ni(II/0) redox potential, the energetics of oxidative addition, and the stability of the proposed intermediates — making this a stronger RI-06 instance than analogies within a ligand family (e.g., dpe vs. dpp). This case should be added to the RI-06 representative examples in the codebook, specifically under the "cycle-level import" scope note, as a case where the donor-atom change alone makes isomorphism assumption non-trivial.

**2. PRI-11 — earliest known instance, with era-norm-tolerated character explicitly recorded.**
SRG-2 provides an unambiguous PRI-11 instance: the five-step cycle (eqs 2–4) is written with algebraically unspecified steps and all mechanistic conclusions rest on observed product distributions. This is the earliest paper in the corpus exhibiting PRI-11, pre-dating the two instances already cited in the promotion record (Breitenfeld 2014, Pengfei 2024). The 1972 vintage is informationally valuable: it shows that PRI-11 was tolerated by era norms in the communication format (no kinetic data required), which explains why the pattern persisted across decades. Codebook annotation: PRI-11 in this case is era-norm dependent (communication-era tolerated) rather than a contemporary deficiency.

**3. Labeling mismatch pattern — textbook case.**
The paper contains a clean labeling mismatch: the hypothesis label "working hypothesis" appears once, in the final sentence of the mechanism section ("apart from the working hypothesis described above"), while the entire narrative of the mechanism section uses unqualified factual verbs: "reacts with," "is subsequently converted to," "forms a new diorgano complex," "is released by the attack of the organic halide." The hypothesis label is structurally confined to the final sentence of the section; it does not retrospectively govern the factual-verb narration that precedes it. This co-occurs with RI-06 (imported cycle), confirming the co-occurrence pattern noted in the institutional design checklist. This case should be cited as a reference example of the labeling mismatch pattern in the codebook application notes.

**4. Automatic-resolution pattern via communication → Full Paper transition — confirmed.**
The majority of SRG-3 and SRG-4 failures (RI-01: incomplete condition specification; RI-07: absent failure examples) correspond to items that are structurally resolved by the communication-to-full-paper transition. The paper explicitly announces [REDACTED: verbatim source quotation; see the source paper via its DOI] naming the full-paper successor. This matches the automatic-resolution pattern for RI-01 and RI-07 described in §6 of the codebook. The A-classified items (conditions deferred, scope deferred) in the record are the communication-format generation of the same items that RI-01/RI-07 flag — a systematic correspondence that validates the era-norm toleration classification in §4 for this class of paper.

**5. RI co-occurrence — SRG-1 × SRG-10 (RI-03 + RI-06) requires resolving both for VQ-3.**
The most structurally important co-occurrence in this paper is SRG-1 (RI-03: all intermediates unobserved) × SRG-10 (RI-06: entire cycle imported from dipy-Ni). Resolving RI-03 alone (isolating complexes 1, 2, 3 with the dpe ligand) would still leave open whether the dpe intermediates operate by the same mechanism as the dipy precedent — observing the intermediates does not validate the analogy. Conversely, resolving RI-06 by EP-06a (stoichiometric validation of eq 1 for dpe-Ni) addresses only one step of the cycle. The co-occurrence structure means that full VQ-3 certification requires both EPs in sequence: first EP-06a (analogy validation), then EP-03a (direct observation of all intermediates). This is the structurally efficient reinforcement route recorded in §5.

**6. No new PRI candidate detected.** All patterns map to existing RI/PRI codes. The observation that PRI-11 applies to a 1972 communication with era-norm-tolerated character strengthens the case for its promotion (three independent instances now available, spanning 1972–2024).

**7. VQ boundary insight — era-norm-dependent VQ-2 failure as a class-level observation.**
Both VQ-2 failures (RI-01 and RI-07) in this paper are era-norm dependent in the communication format. This means the paper was era-norm adequate at VQ-1 at the time of publication, and VQ-2 failure was implicitly accepted as a communication-format property. The Full Paper transition is the natural mechanism for VQ-2 resolution in this class. This observation generalises across 1970s–1980s synthetic organometallic communications and may warrant a note in the VQ decision matrix: "For papers in the communication format published before approximately 1985, RI-01 and RI-07 failures may be classified as era-norm-dependent VQ-2 failures, with the expectation of automatic resolution in the accompanying full paper."
