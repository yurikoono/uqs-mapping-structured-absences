## Same-Root Groups

The items above share underlying absent data in several clusters. Below the factually shared gaps are identified; items with multiple group memberships are listed accordingly.

---

### SRG-1: Unobserved catalytic intermediates (Complex 1, 2, 3)
Items 2, 3, 4, 7, 40, 52 all derive from the single root gap: no intermediate in the proposed nickel-phosphine catalytic cycle (L₂NiR₂, L₂NiR'X", and the second L₂NiRR') has been isolated or spectroscopically detected under conditions matching the preparative reactions. Evidence needed is fundamentally the same: stoichiometric mechanistic experiments with [NiCl₂(dpe)] and the relevant organic partners.

---

### SRG-2: Missing kinetic data for all proposed steps
Items 1, 5, 6, 40, 42, 47, 49 share the root gap that no rate measurements — for any step, any substrate, any ligand combination, any temperature — are reported. All mechanistic conclusions and all statements about selectivity depend on kinetic analysis that is entirely absent.

---

### SRG-3: Unoptimized and narrowly tested conditions
Items 18, 19, 20, 33, 36, 44, 57 all trace to the footnote admission that "The experimental conditions are not necessarily optimum." Each concerns a parameter (catalyst loading, Grignard excess, temperature, time, solvent, halide identity) that has been set empirically without systematic variation.

---

### SRG-4: Substrate scope not established
Items 21, 22, 23, 24, 25, 50 all reflect the absence of systematic scope determination: only 5 Grignard reagents, only carbocyclic halides, no functional groups, no heteroaryl, no secondary or tertiary alkyl, no aryl–aryl coupling. The root gap is the same: Table I is a selective snapshot, not a scope-defining matrix.

---

### SRG-5: Stereochemical mechanism unresolved
Items 14, 15, 59 share the root gap: the geometry of the key vinyl-Ni complexes and the stereochemical consequences of each mechanistic step (transmetalation, reductive elimination) are entirely undetermined. The non-stereospecific stilbene formation is a symptom; the root cause lies in the uncharacterized vinyl-Ni intermediates.

---

### SRG-6: Ligand activity order untranslated into structural understanding
Items 7, 11, 12, 13, 56 all trace to the root gap that the observed ligand ordering has been measured (with one substrate pair) but has not been correlated with any structural or electronic parameter of the ligands. The same data underlie inferences about cis geometry requirements, bite-angle effects, and phosphine donor strength.

---

### SRG-7: Active catalyst identity and heterogeneous vs. homogeneous nature
Items 17, 29, 41 share the root gap: whether the catalysis is truly homogeneous (with [NiCl₂(dpe)]-derived phosphine-Ni complexes as proposed), involves heterogeneous Ni(0) nanoparticles (consistent with the black coloration), or requires a separate Ni(0) activation step is not determined. The same experimental approach (mercury test, filtration, preformed Ni(0) catalysts) would resolve all three items.

---

### SRG-8: Prior art systems not experimentally compared
Items 30, 31, 32, 43, 53 all trace to a single gap: the paper defines its advantage over prior systems (Tamura–Kochi iron, organocuprates, the simultaneous Corriu–Masse nickel system) entirely by assertion and literature citation, without a single controlled comparison experiment. The claim of selectivity advantage is never demonstrated relative to a contemporary benchmark.

---

### SRG-9: Mechanism of C–C bond formation not established
Items 4, 5, 6, 49 all center on the reductive elimination step: how the C–C bond forms from complex 3 (triggered by organic halide or spontaneous?), what its transition state looks like, what role the ligand plays, and what the rate-determining character is remain fully open.

---

### SRG-10: Mechanistic analogy from dipy-Ni to dpe-Ni system not validated
Items 27, 28, 55 all share the root gap that the three key precedents for the proposed mechanism (refs 11–13) used fundamentally different nickel ligand systems (dipy, Cl-bridged, PEt₃ complexes) and were never validated for the dpe system by any stoichiometric experiment in this paper.

---

### SRG-11: Solvent effects unexplained
Items 16, 38, 57 all reflect the absence of any mechanistic or systematic analysis of solvent effects. Why ether is superior to THF, why the NpMgBr reaction requires a THF component despite the general rule against THF, and why Tamura–Kochi's catalysts prefer THF are all aspects of a single root gap: the interaction between solvent coordination and nickel catalyst speciation/activity.

---

### SRG-12: Unquantified byproduct distribution
Items 19, 34, 37 all trace to the root gap that the mass balance for reactions with <100% yield is never reported. Whether the deficit arises from homo-coupling, β-hydride elimination (alkene + Ni-H side products), catalyst decomposition, or incomplete reaction is unknown for any entry.
