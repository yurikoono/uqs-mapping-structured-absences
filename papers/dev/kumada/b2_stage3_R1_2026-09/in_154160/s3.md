### §1 Claim Architecture

*[Stage 2 D-1 is not present in the record as a formal field. The following core-claim summary is reconstructed from the 68 items and direct reading of the PDF.]*

**Core claim:** The paper advances a two-pillar claim. The **Novelty pillar** is that bidentate diphosphine-ligated Ni(II) halide complexes — specifically NiCl₂(dpe) — catalyze selective cross-coupling of Grignard reagents with vinyl and aryl halides while suppressing homo-coupling products, a problem that had defeated prior transition-metal halide catalysis. The **Utility pillar** is that the method enables one-step synthesis of otherwise difficult-to-access compounds (e.g., *m*-di-*n*-butylbenzene, α-vinylnaphthalene), operates with alkylmagnesium reagents bearing β-hydrogens, exploits the more electronegative and cheaper sp² C–Cl electrophile, and tolerates what the paper describes as "a variety" of coupling partners.

**Layered structure:** Three partially independent layers can be identified. **Layer 1 (Synthetic observation):** high-yield cross-coupling is achieved, as evidenced by isolated or GC yields of 76–98% in Table I. This layer is directly supported by preparative data and is the most robust to mechanistic revision. **Layer 2 (Selectivity claim):** the reaction is "selective," implying that homo-coupling is suppressed — but this layer carries no quantitative byproduct data and rests entirely on assertion. A single experiment showing substantial homo-coupling byproduct would collapse the selectivity claim while leaving Layer 1 intact. **Layer 3 (Mechanistic model):** the four-step Ni(0)/Ni(II) catalytic cycle (equations 2–4) explains both selectivity and the ligand-activity ordering. This layer is entirely analogical (borrowed from dipy-Ni stoichiometric chemistry) and is independently vulnerable: its collapse would not invalidate Layers 1–2, but it would remove the explanatory framework for the ligand SAR and the sp²-selectivity rationale. Inter-layer dependencies run downward: Layer 3 explains Layer 2; Layer 2 motivates Layer 1's practical significance. **A single refutation does not collapse all layers; independent layers exist.** However, because the paper's title word "selective" is the central novelty claim, Layer 2's vulnerability constitutes the most consequential gap.

### §4 Era-Norm Annotation

*[Stage 2 D-2 (format_exploitation, timing_pressure) and Stage 2 D-3 (journal_constraint, field_competition_state) are not present as formal fields in the record. The following annotation draws from record item content and direct reading of the PDF.]*

**communication-era tolerated:**

- **Sparse product characterisation (bp, nD, comparison to literature; Items 29):** A 1972 JACS communication of ≈3 pages could not carry NMR tables or elemental analysis for all products; physical constants compared to authenticated literature values were accepted as sufficient product identification.
- **No mechanistic experiments (Items 1, 2, 3, 47, 48):** First-disclosure communications in organometallic chemistry were expected to establish synthetic utility; mechanistic experiments were reserved for follow-up full papers. The paper itself explicitly announces this deferral: [REDACTED: verbatim source quotation; see the source paper via its DOI]
- **Non-optimal conditions, no parametric optimisation (Items 11, 12, 13, SRG-7):** Priority-disclosure communications routinely hedged yields with "conditions not necessarily optimum"; systematic optimisation was expected in the full paper.
- **No TON/TOF data (CLA-1):** In 1972, catalytic efficiency as a quantifiable metric (turnover number) was not standard reporting practice in preparative organometallic chemistry; the existence of catalytic activity at the stated loading was the reportable finding.
- **No catalyst-free blank (Item 66):** For a known metal/Grignard combination in ether, a background reaction experiment was not standard; visible metallic precipitation was considered self-evidencing of metal involvement.
- **Catalyst synthesis cited to prior literature without batch characterisation (Item 57):** Citing Booth and Chatt (1965) for the synthesis of [NiCl₂(dpe)] was acceptable practice for a literature-described catalyst complex.

**modern-era deficient:**

- **Homo-coupling byproduct not quantified (Items 10, SRG-4):** Modern journals require selectivity ratios reported as quantitative data (e.g., cross:homo molar ratio by GC) for any paper using "selective" in the title; the current paper cannot pass this requirement.
- **No internal comparison with the simultaneous Corriu-Masse system (Items 35, 67, SRG-8):** A simultaneously published, independently developed system acknowledged in proof would require at minimum one comparative run in a modern submission; referee standards now require authors to differentiate their contribution from concurrent work.
- **Inconsistent yield reporting bases (Item 50, SRG-4):** Modern analytical standards require a single, clearly defined yield basis (typically limiting reagent) across all table entries.
- **sp² halide boundary claimed without failure-case evidence (SRG-2):** Modern method papers are required to demonstrate claimed substrate restrictions with negative controls.
- **No replicate measurements or uncertainty estimates (CLA-1, CLA-2):** Modern journals require at least duplicate runs with standard deviation for key entries; the 76% outlier in Table I would require explanation.
- **Ligand activity ordering from single substrate pair presented as general (Items 8, 9, SRG-5):** A modern structure–activity relationship study would require systematic variation across ≥3 substrate combinations before mechanistic conclusions are drawn.

**Dual-classification (tolerated then, deficient now):**

- **Absence of stoichiometric mechanistic experiments (Items 1, 32, 33, SRG-1):** Tolerated in 1972 as communication-format norm; deficient by modern standards because the mechanistic framework is now expected to have at least one stoichiometric verification under the specific ligand system described before a mechanistic claim is published.
- **β-H tolerance asserted without byproduct quantification (Item 5, SRG-3):** Tolerated in 1972 because high isolated yield was considered sufficient evidence; deficient now because β-hydride elimination is quantifiable (GC of alkene byproducts) and modern reviewers would require this measurement before accepting the tolerance claim.

### §6 Contribution to institutional design

**1. RI-06 cycle-level import: representative case confirmed with additional clarity.**
SRG-1 and SRG-10 together provide a historically important and chemically clean example of RI-06 (cycle-level import): the entire Ni(0)/Ni(II) catalytic cycle is lifted wholesale from a dipy-Ni stoichiometric system (ref 11) and transmetalation precedents from monodentate-phosphine Ni complexes (ref 13), and applied to a dpe-Ni catalytic system without any experiment demonstrating isomorphism. The case is analytically clean because the ligand mismatch (bidentate diphosphine vs. bipyridine) is structurally obvious and the absence of verification experiments is total. This strengthens the RI-06 scope note (cycle-level import) with a well-documented case for the codebook.

**2. Labeling mismatch pattern detected — co-occurrence with RI-06 confirmed.**
The paper explicitly labels its mechanistic scheme as a "working hypothesis" in its penultimate paragraph ("apart from the working hypothesis described above") and acknowledges that [REDACTED: verbatim source quotation; see the source paper via its DOI] Yet throughout the mechanistic text, factual verbs prevail: complex **1** "is subsequently converted to," **2** "is regenerated," the organic halide "reacts with." The hypothesis label is concentrated in a single terminal sentence — a classic labeling mismatch. This case confirms the codebook note that labeling mismatch "often co-occurs with RI-06."

**3. Automatic-resolution pattern via communication→Full Paper transition: positively confirmed.**
Multiple RI-01 instances in this paper (non-optimized conditions, incomplete condition descriptions, sparse product characterization, non-standardized yield bases) are structurally mandated by the JACS communication format and were automatically resolved in the companion full paper (Tamao, Sumitani, Hayashi, Kumada, JACS 1976, and earlier full disclosures). Correspondence with §4 A items is confirmed.

**4. RI co-occurrence in SRG-1: RI-06 + RI-03 require joint resolution.**
In SRG-1, RI-06 (Imported model) and RI-03 (Unobserved explanatory) co-occur in the same cluster. Resolving only RI-06 — by demonstrating stoichiometric isomorphism between dipy-Ni and dpe-Ni elementary steps — would still leave RI-03 open (the intermediates remain unobserved). Conversely, direct observation of intermediates **1**–**3** (RI-03 resolution) would simultaneously provide strong evidence for cycle isomorphism (RI-06 resolution). The co-occurrence structure means EP-06a and EP-03a are more powerful when executed together than sequentially, as a single stoichiometric study with in-situ spectroscopy could address both RIs. This is a case-specific illustration of the RI co-occurrence pattern described in the codebook.

**5. PRI-11 (Mechanism–kinetics decoupling) confirmed in an extreme form; PRI-11b candidate proposed.**
PRI-11 is defined as "mechanism is supported only by agreement with observed rate orders, without an algebraic derivation of the rate law." This paper presents a more primitive instance: no kinetic data of any kind are provided (no rate orders, no rate constants, no Hammett correlations, no activation parameters), and the rate-determining step is explicitly acknowledged as unknown (Item 2, SRG-1). The proposed cycle is supported by zero kinetic evidence, and the paper explicitly concedes this: "Further studies are required." This case either (a) represents a clean and extreme confirmatory instance of PRI-11 (total decoupling rather than algebraic gap), or (b) motivates a related code distinction: **PRI-11b candidate — Mechanism-kinetics absence**: a catalytic cycle is proposed with no kinetic data of any kind rather than with kinetic data not algebraically connected to a rate law. The distinction may matter for promotion criteria: PRI-11 was confirmed via agreement with observed rate orders (suggesting kinetics were measured but not connected to the mechanism); PRI-11b cases have no rate data at all. The two-paper isomorphism requirement for promotion should be evaluated across the reaction-path and organometallic corpora to determine whether the "no kinetics at all" pattern is recurrent.

**6. Incomplete negative evidence pattern: not observed.**
The codebook check for "incomplete negative evidence" (radical clocks, TEMPO trapping, or similar negative mechanistic experiments used as positive mechanistic evidence without mass-balance or kinetic-window evaluation) does not apply to this paper. No negative mechanistic experiments of any kind are reported. The absence of negative evidence is total rather than selective; the pattern is not instantiated here.

**7. New case-specific EP-03a implementation for early Ni-phosphine cross-coupling systems.**
EP-03a (Direct-observation/state-assignment pack) in a Ni-diphosphine cross-coupling system should include: (a) in-situ ³¹P NMR monitoring to confirm ligand retention and detect oxidation-state-dependent chemical shift changes (Ni(0) vs Ni(II) complexes have distinct ³¹P shifts); (b) EPR spectroscopy to detect possible Ni(I) radical intermediates, given subsequent discovery that Ni(I)/Ni(III) cycles operate in many Ni-catalysed cross-couplings; and (c) UV-vis monitoring to track Ni(0) formation from NiCl₂(dpe) + Grignard reduction. This implementation is richer than a generic EP-03a entry and constitutes a representative case-specific addition to the EP dictionary for Ni-catalysis papers.
