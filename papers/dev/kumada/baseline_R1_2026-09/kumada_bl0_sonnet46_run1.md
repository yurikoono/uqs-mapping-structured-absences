## Unanswered Questions and Unsupported Claims

### 1. The catalytic intermediates are inferred, not observed

The three-step catalytic cycle (complexes **1**, **2**, **3**) is presented as a working hypothesis built from literature precedents on *different* nickel systems. No intermediate in the actual cross-coupling reaction is isolated or spectroscopically detected.

**Evidence needed:** Isolation or in-situ spectroscopic identification of diorganonickel complex **1** and (halo)(organo)nickel complex **2** under the actual reaction conditions with [NiCl₂(dpe)] and the Grignard/halide substrates used in Table I.

### 2. Why homo-coupling and disproportionation are suppressed is unexplained

The paper opens by noting that prior transition-metal-catalysed cross-couplings fail in practice because of homo-coupling and disproportionation, then presents yields up to 98 % cross-product with no mechanistic account of why the nickel–diphosphine system avoids these side pathways.

**Evidence needed:** Quantification of homo-coupling by-products (R–R and R′–R′) across the substrate range, and a kinetic or mechanistic argument for why complex **3** preferentially undergoes oxidative addition/reductive elimination to give the cross product rather than homo-coupled material.

### 3. The cis-configuration argument for ligand activity order is not demonstrated

The activity trend across five nickel–phosphine catalysts is used to conclude that *cis* geometry of the two organic groups in intermediate **3** is the "first requisite" of an effective catalyst. The argument is plausible but entirely inferential; no structural data on any intermediate is offered, and geometric preference was not varied independently of other electronic/steric ligand properties.

**Evidence needed:** Crystal structures or NMR characterisation of diorganonickel complexes **1** or **3** bearing each ligand; kinetic studies that deconvolute bite-angle effects from electronic and cone-angle contributions to the observed activity order.

### 4. The restriction to sp² carbon–halogen bonds is stated but not explained

The paper reports that only vinylic and aryl halides couple successfully, and calls this "one of the most remarkable features," but offers no mechanistic rationale—e.g. whether it reflects slower oxidative addition of sp³ C–X to nickel(0), faster β-hydride elimination from an sp³ alkyl–Ni species, or a different bottleneck.

**Evidence needed:** Attempted reactions with representative sp³ alkyl halides under identical conditions with quantification of products; rate measurements for oxidative addition of sp² vs. sp³ C–Cl to the Ni(0) species generated in situ.

### 5. Suppression of β-hydride elimination from alkyl–nickel species is unaccounted for

The paper explicitly notes that transition-metal alkyl complexes bearing a β-hydrogen are "commonly unstable," yet claims high yields with ethyl and n-butyl (and n-octyl) Grignard reagents. No explanation is given for why β-elimination is not competitive here.

**Evidence needed:** Detection or quantification of β-elimination products (alkenes, Ni–H species) as a function of temperature, ligand, and chain length; a kinetic comparison of reductive-elimination vs. β-elimination rate from the relevant alkyl–Ni(II) complex.

### 6. The nonstereospecific outcome with 1,2-dichloroethylene is unexplained

Both *cis*- and *trans*-1,2-dichloroethylene give a *cis*-enriched mixture of stilbene rather than the stereochemically retentive product expected from a concerted cycle. The paper describes the result as anomalous but offers no mechanistic hypothesis.

**Evidence needed:** Kinetic isotope experiments, trapping of vinyl–Ni intermediates, or DFT calculations to distinguish radical, ionic, or η²-vinyl pathways that could account for stereochemical scrambling and the *cis* enrichment.

### 7. The solvent superiority of diethyl ether over THF is stated without explanation

Diethyl ether is reported to be "definitely superior" to THF but the basis—whether electronic, coordinative, or related to Grignard speciation—is not addressed.

**Evidence needed:** Comparative rate or yield data across a solvent series; NMR or conductance studies of the Grignard equilibrium and catalyst speciation in ether vs. THF to identify whether the difference originates in catalyst stability, substrate activation, or Schlenk equilibrium effects.

### 8. The scope of the reaction is narrow and the conditions are explicitly non-optimised

Ten entries—all primary alkyl or aryl Grignard reagents with mono- or difunctional aryl/vinyl chlorides—are presented. No secondary or tertiary alkyl Grignard reagents, no heteroatom-bearing partners, and no alkyl halides are included. The table footnote explicitly waives optimality.

**Evidence needed:** Systematic substrate surveys covering secondary/tertiary alkyl-, heteroaryl-, and alkenyl-Grignard reagents; optimised catalyst loadings and temperatures with reproducibility data; reaction-profile data (conversion vs. time) to establish whether yields reflect genuine selectivity or kinetic incompleteness.

### 9. Stereochemical and mechanistic details are explicitly deferred

The authors themselves concede that the mechanistic picture is incomplete and that further study is required before the stereochemical course can be understood—yet the communication presents the catalytic cycle as a settled "working hypothesis."

**Evidence needed:** Stereochemical probes (enantio- or diastereospecific substrates), Hammett correlations across para-substituted aryl chlorides, and kinetic order determinations for each component to establish whether the proposed cycle is operative and which step is rate-limiting.
