## Unanswered Questions and Unsupported Claims in Tamao, Sumitani & Kumada (1972)

---

**1. The catalytic cycle is a working hypothesis without direct evidence for any intermediate**

(1) The paper proposes a three-intermediate catalytic cycle (diorganonickel **1** → haloorganonickel **2** → new diorganonickel **3**), but none of these species are isolated, observed spectroscopically, or independently generated and shown to be catalytically competent in this system. The analogy to reaction (1) from Uchino et al. involves a bipyridyl complex with different ligands and stoichiometry. Evidence needed: isolation or in-situ NMR/IR characterization of intermediates under catalytic conditions; stoichiometric experiments demonstrating each elementary step in the dpe-ligated system.

**2. Why β-hydrogen-containing alkylmagnesium reagents do not undergo β-hydride elimination**

(1) The paper claims this as a notable feature but offers no explanation. Transition metal alkyls bearing β-hydrogen are described in a footnote as "commonly unstable," making the high yields from *n*-BuMgBr surprising. Evidence needed: kinetic studies or deuterium-labelling experiments distinguishing productive transmetalation/reductive elimination from competing β-hydride elimination; characterisation and quantification of any alkene by-products.

**3. The mechanistic basis for the exclusive requirement of sp2 (vinylic or aryl) halides is unstated**

(1) The restriction to sp2 electrophiles is asserted as a fact but not rationalised. Does the sp3 carbon–halide bond fail to oxidatively add to nickel, or does it react but give only homo-coupling or reduction? Evidence needed: direct attempts with representative sp3 halides (e.g. *n*-BuCl, BnCl) with characterisation of all products, and comparison of oxidative-addition rates for sp2 vs. sp3 C–Cl bonds to the NiCl₂(dpe)-derived Ni(0) species.

**4. The claim that cis-coordination geometry is "the first requisite" rests on a ligand-activity correlation, not structural proof**

(1) The order [NiCl₂(dpp)] > [NiCl₂(dpe)] > [NiCl₂(dmpe)] ≫ [NiCl₂(PPh₃)₂] ≈ [NiCl₂(PEt₃)₂] ≈ [NiCl₂(PPh₂Me)₂] is used to infer that cis diorganonickel geometry is essential. The inference conflates bite-angle, electron density, and cone-angle effects. Evidence needed: X-ray or solution structural data on intermediate **3** with different ligands; synthesis of geometrically constrained model complexes to decouple steric and electronic variables.

**5. The anomalous cis-enrichment in stilbene formation from both cis- and trans-1,2-dichloroethylene is unexplained**

(1) Both diastereomers of the electrophile give a product mixture skewed toward *cis*-stilbene (80:20 from *cis*-ClCH=CHCl; 43:57 from *trans*-ClCH=CHCl), yet no mechanism is offered for how stereochemical information is lost or inverted. Evidence needed: labelling experiments to establish whether vinyl–C–Cl bond cleavage is stereoretentive or stereoinvertive; isolation and characterisation of vinyl-nickel intermediates to determine whether isomerisation precedes or follows reductive elimination.

**6. The superiority of diethyl ether over THF is asserted without quantitative comparison**

(1) The solvent preference is stated categorically, but no parallel experiment in THF is reported in the table or text, and no yield or selectivity data in THF are given. Evidence needed: direct head-to-head yield comparisons in both solvents for at least representative substrates; measurement of catalyst speciation or Grignard speciation (Schlenk equilibrium) in each solvent.

**7. The scope of homo-coupling suppression is not quantified**

(1) The paper's central claim is *selective* cross-coupling, contrasting with prior methods that gave homo-coupling in "substantial amounts." However, no yields or detection limits for homo-coupling products (R–R or R'–R') are reported for any entry in Table I. Evidence needed: quantitative determination (e.g. GLC with internal standard) of R–R and R'–R' homo-coupling by-products across several substrate combinations; explicit comparison with iron or copper catalyst results under identical conditions.

**8. The identity and role of the "black mixture" formed in Example 1 are not addressed**

(1) During the 20-hour reflux in Example 1, a "black mixture" forms and "much salt" accumulates. Neither the black material (possibly colloidal nickel or a nickel mirror) nor the salt is characterised. If colloidal nickel is the true catalyst, the mechanistic picture of soluble NiCl₂(dpe) as the active species would need revision. Evidence needed: elemental analysis, TEM, or filtration experiments to determine whether the active catalyst is homogeneous or heterogeneous; turnover-number measurements on filtered vs. unfiltered reaction mixtures.

**9. Conditions are acknowledged as non-optimal, leaving reported yields uninformative about synthetic utility**

(1) The table footnote states that conditions "are not necessarily optimum," yet the paper uses these yields ("generally very high") to argue for synthetic utility. The actual ceiling performance of the reaction under optimised conditions—and which variables (Grignard equivalents, catalyst loading, temperature, time) govern yield—remain open. Evidence needed: systematic optimisation experiments with response-surface or one-variable-at-a-time design, and demonstration that the optimised conditions are reproducible across multiple substrate classes.
