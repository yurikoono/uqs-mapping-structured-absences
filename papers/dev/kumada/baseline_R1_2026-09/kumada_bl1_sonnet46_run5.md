### Item 1 — Proposed catalytic cycle lacks direct evidence for any intermediate
**Question/unsupported claim:** Intermediates **1**, **2**, and **3** (eqs 2–4) are asserted as the operative catalytic species, yet none is isolated, spectroscopically detected, or kinetically characterised in this work. The entire mechanism is presented as inference from separate organometallic precedents, not from observations made on the actual catalytic system.

**Evidence needed:** Isolation or in-situ spectroscopic observation (NMR, IR) of at least one of intermediates 1–3 under the stated conditions; kinetic rate-law measurements establishing the order in Grignard reagent and in organic halide.

### Item 2 — Non-stereospecificity of the reaction with 1,2-dichloroethylenes is unexplained

## Same-Root Groups

**SRG-1: Uncharacterised catalytic intermediates** — Items 1, 6, and the implicit dependency in Item 13 share the same root: no direct observation of any Ni(II) or Ni(0) intermediate in the operative catalytic cycle.

**SRG-2: Stereo-outcome without mechanistic account** — Items 2 and 3 both arise from the absence of a mechanistic model for what controls olefin geometry in the product; the two items are facets of the same missing reductive-elimination or oxidative-addition stereochemical data.

**SRG-3: β-H suppression and sp² selectivity** — Items 4 and 8 derive from the same underlying gap: no experimental evidence for what the Ni–diphosphine framework does or does not activate in terms of C–X bond oxidative addition and subsequent β-H pathways.

**SRG-4: Ligand and solvent effects without rationalisation** — Items 5 and 7 share a root in the absence of any physical-organic or coordination-chemistry measurements to explain why bidentate > monodentate and ether > THF.

**SRG-5: Uncontinued prior-art citations** — Items 9 and 10 both represent cross-coupling systems cited but not mechanistically contrasted; both leave the reader unable to understand why Ni-diphosphine is a different regime from Fe or from Ni(acac)₂.
