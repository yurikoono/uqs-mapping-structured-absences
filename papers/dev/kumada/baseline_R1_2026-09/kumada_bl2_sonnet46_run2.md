## Tamao, Sumitani & Kumada, "Selective Carbon–Carbon Bond Formation by Cross-Coupling of Grignard Reagents with Organic Halides. Catalysis by Nickel–Phosphine Complexes," *JACS* **94**, 4374–4376 (1972)

### Item 1 — The proposed catalytic cycle rests entirely on analogy; no intermediate is directly observed **[A]**

**(1)** The paper proposes a four-step catalytic cycle involving three distinct nickel species (diorganonickel complex **1**, haloorganonickel complex **2**, and a second diorganonickel complex **3**), but none of these intermediates is isolated, spectroscopically characterised, or even independently detected during the catalytic reactions described in the paper. The mechanistic scheme (equations 2–4) is constructed by extrapolating from two stoichiometric precedents in the prior literature rather than from any experiment performed by the authors themselves.

**(2)** Isolation and characterisation (NMR, IR, elemental analysis) of complexes **1**, **2**, and **3** formed under catalytic conditions; in-situ spectroscopic monitoring (e.g., by <sup>31</sup>P or <sup>1</sup>H NMR) of the reaction mixture; kinetic analysis (rate law determination) to identify the rate-limiting step and establish which steps are kinetically competent.

### Item 2 — The rate-determining step of the catalytic cycle is unidentified **[A]**

**(1)** The paper proposes four elementary steps (formation of **1** by double transmetalation; conversion of **1** to **2** by organic halide; transmetalation of **2** to form **3**; reductive elimination from **3** to release product and regenerate **2**), but there is no kinetic data—no rate constants, no activation parameters, no Hammett correlations—that would allow the rate-determining step to be identified. Without that knowledge, claims about what "controls" selectivity cannot be founded on the mechanistic scheme.

**(2)** Kinetic measurements of initial rates as a function of [Grignard], [organic halide], and [catalyst]; isotope labeling (e.g., deuterium-labeled Grignard reagents) to probe transmetalation; competition experiments to measure relative rates of homo vs. cross coupling under varying conditions.

### Item 3 — The identity of the true active catalyst species is not established **[A]**

**(1)** The paper uses NiCl₂(dpe) as the catalyst precursor, but a Grignard reagent in large excess relative to catalyst would rapidly reduce Ni(II) to Ni(0) before any catalytic turnover begins. The appearance of a "black mixture" in Example 1 hints at possible catalyst decomposition or reduction, but this is left unaddressed.

**(2)** Characterisation of the catalyst resting state under turnover conditions by EPR, NMR, or XANES; control experiments with independently synthesised Ni(0) phosphine complexes; tests of whether the black precipitate in Example 1 is catalytically active.

All seven items derive from the absence of systematic Grignard reagent scope data. Only five Grignard types (EtMgBr, n-BuMgBr, n-octylMgCl, PhMgBr, α-NpMgBr) are tested. Items 5 (β-H suppression unexplained), 22 (no secondary/tertiary alkyl), 23 (no functional groups), 24 (no heteroaromatic Grignard), 25 (no alkenyl Grignard), 26 (no alkynyl), and 28 ("a variety" overstated) all stem from the same narrow Grignard reagent dataset.

### Group SRG-4: Selectivity quantification failure (Items 10, 11, 29, 30, 38, 60)

These items all stem from the absence of byproduct quantification and mass-balance accounting. Items 10 (homo-coupling not quantified), 11 (conditions not optimised), 29 (characterisation is insufficient), 30 (double coupling mechanism of Ph₂C=CH₂), 38 (disproportionation products undefined), and 60 (sequential dichlorobenzene couplings not mechanistically dissected) share the root absence of quantitative selectivity data and full mass-balance studies.

### Group SRG-5: Ligand structure–activity relationship (Items 7, 8, 9, 58, 64)

Items 7 (cis configuration not proven), 8 (no mechanistic rationalisation of ligand order), 9 (ordering measured for only one substrate pair), 58 (ligand necessity not tested by poisoning), and 64 (ligand degradation not studied) all stem from the single root absence: the paper reports the empirical ligand activity order but provides no structural, electronic, or steric analysis to account for it, and does not perform any experiment that probes the ligand's role in the catalytic mechanism.

### Group SRG-6: Stereochemistry of alkene coupling (Items 17, 18, 62)

Items 17 (nonstereospecific stilbene formation), 18 (cis enrichment anomaly), and 62 (ortho-di-n-butylbenzene steric effects) share the root absence of mechanistic work on the stereochemical course of reductive elimination or vinyl-nickel geometry. The nonstereospecificity is noted but never examined.
