## Tamao, Sumitani & Kumada (1972) — Unanswered Questions and Unsupported Claims

---

### 1. Catalytic cycle intermediates are inferred, not observed **[A]**

The paper proposes a three-step cycle through diorganonickel complex **1**, (halo)(organo)nickel complex **2**, and diorgano complex **3**, but no intermediate in the dpe-nickel system is isolated, detected spectroscopically, or kinetically trapped in this work. The cited precedents (refs 11–12) involve a bipyridyl complex under stoichiometric conditions, not a catalytic dpe system.

*Evidence needed:* Isolation or in-situ spectroscopic observation (e.g., NMR, UV-vis) of complexes 1–3 under catalytic conditions; kinetic data distinguishing steps 2–4.

### 4. Suppression of β-hydride elimination is claimed but unexplained **[A]**

The paper highlights as a key feature that alkyl Grignard reagents bearing β-hydrogen atoms give high yields, noting that transition-metal alkyls with β-H "are commonly unstable." No explanation is given for why β-hydride elimination is suppressed under these conditions (catalyst loading, temperature, or ligand effect).

*Evidence needed:* Identification of side products (e.g., internal alkene from β-hydride elimination); competition experiments varying temperature or catalyst; mechanistic comparison with systems that do show β-hydride elimination.

### 6. Nonstereospecific reaction with geometric isomers of 1,2-dichloroethylene is unreported mechanistically **[A]**

Both cis- and trans-1,2-dichloroethylene react with PhMgBr to give cis/trans-stilbene mixtures "anomalously enriched" with cis-stilbene (80:20 from cis-substrate; 43:57 from trans), but the paper offers no rationale for the loss of stereospecificity or the systematic cis-enrichment.

*Evidence needed:* Isotope-labelling or crossover experiments to probe whether vinyl-nickel intermediates isomerize; temperature-dependence of the cis:trans ratio; direct observation of vinyl-nickel intermediates.

### 7. Diethyl ether superiority over THF asserted without data **[A]**

The paper states definitively that Et₂O is "definitely superior" to THF, contrasting with Tamura and Kochi's iron-catalyst system. No comparative yield or rate data are presented to support this claim within the nickel system.

*Evidence needed:* Side-by-side yield comparison in Et₂O vs. THF for the same substrate pair; analysis of side products (homo-coupling, reduction) in each solvent.

## Same-Root Groups

**Group α — Mechanism and intermediates (root: absence of any direct observation of Ni complexes 1–3)**
Items 1, 3, 4, 6
All four gaps — the unobserved catalytic intermediates, the unsupported cis-geometry requisite, the unexplained β-hydride suppression, and the unexplained loss of alkene stereospecificity — would be resolved by the same experimental program: isolation or in-situ spectroscopy of diorgano- and (halo)(organo)nickel intermediates under catalytic conditions.

**Group β — Scope and optimisation (root: absence of systematic parameter and substrate variation)**
Items 5, 7, 8, 10
The exclusion of sp³ halides, the unsubstantiated solvent claim, the non-optimised conditions, and the deferred scope extension all trace to the same gap: no parametric or substrate-matrix data were collected beyond the illustrative examples in Table I.

**Group γ — Stereochemical details (root: absence of stereospecificity experiments)**
Items 2, 6
The general deferral of stereochemical details and the specific nonstereospecificity of the stilbene-forming reactions share the same methodological root: no stereochemical probes (chiral substrates, isotope labels, temperature-dependent diastereomer ratios) were applied.
