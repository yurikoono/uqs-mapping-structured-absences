## Unanswered Questions and Unsupported Claims

### 1. The proposed catalytic cycle rests on analogy, not on characterisation of the postulated intermediates

**(1)** The three-step cycle (intermediates **1**, **2**, **3**, eq 2–4) is called a "working hypothesis." None of the intermediates is isolated, spectroscopically characterised, or kinetically probed; the mechanism is inferred by analogy from precedents on bipyridyl-nickel and σ-organonickel stoichiometry, not from evidence internal to the catalytic reaction itself.

**(2)** Isolation or trapping of **1** (diorganonickel) and **2** (haloorganonickel) under catalytic conditions; turnover frequency measurements; kinetic isotope effects or Hammett correlations to distinguish rate-determining transmetalation from reductive elimination.

### 2. Why only Csp2 halides react: the restriction is stated but not mechanistically explained

**(1)** The paper asserts that the reaction requires vinylic or aryl (Csp2) halides and that alkyl halides do not work, but offers no account of why—whether the barrier lies in oxidative addition, in the stability of the resulting alkyl-nickel bond, or in a competing β-hydride elimination from the product side.

**(2)** Attempted reactions with alkyl halides paired with monitoring for β-hydride elimination products; comparison of oxidative-addition rates of Csp2 vs. Csp3 halides to the same nickel(0) complex; or isolation of putative alkyl-nickel intermediates.

### 3. High yields with β-hydrogen-bearing Grignard reagents are asserted as a virtue without explaining how β-hydride elimination is suppressed

**(2)** Detection and quantification of any hydride-elimination byproducts (alkenes, metal hydrides); comparison of yield vs. chain length in the alkyl Grignard reagent; or direct characterisation of the putative *n*-alkyl-nickel intermediate **1**.

### 4. The anomalous *cis*-enrichment in the stilbene products from both *cis*- and *trans*-dichloroethylene is acknowledged but left without explanation

**(1)** Both geometrical isomers of 1,2-dichloroethylene give mixtures of *cis*- and *trans*-stilbene with an excess of the *cis* isomer. This non-stereospecificity contradicts a simple concerted mechanism and indicates either geometric scrambling at the nickel centre or a free-rotation intermediate, but no mechanistic rationale is proposed.

**(2)** Characterisation of the vinyl-nickel intermediate; isotopic labelling of the vinyl group to track geometry; variable-temperature studies to detect inversion or rotation at nickel; comparison with a palladium catalyst known to give fully stereospecific coupling.

### 5. The claim that *cis* geometry in the diorganonickel intermediate is the "first requisite" is inferred from a ligand-activity trend, not from structural evidence

**(1)** The paper argues that bidentate phosphines enforce *cis* disposition of the two organic groups in intermediate **3**, and that this is the indispensable structural feature, because bidentate ligands outperform monodentate ones. However, the cis geometry of intermediate **3** is never demonstrated crystallographically or spectroscopically; the activity trend could equally reflect chelate rigidity, bite angle, electronic donation, or lability differences rather than cis enforcement per se.

**(2)** X-ray or NMR characterisation of isolated diorganonickel complexes bearing each ligand class; preparation of forced-*trans* analogues to test reductive elimination rates; bite-angle correlations across a wider ligand set.

### 6. Within the bidentate ligand series the activity order (dpp > dpe > dmpe) is reported without rationalisation

**(1)** Three bidentate phosphines of different bite angle and electronic character give significantly different catalytic activity ([NiCl₂(dpp)] > [NiCl₂(dpe)] > [NiCl₂(dmpe)]), and dmpe (a purely alkyl-substituted analogue of dpe) is roughly equivalent to the monodentate [NiCl₂(PPh₃)₂]. Whether this trend reflects ring-size, bite angle, donor strength, or some combination is unaddressed.

**(2)** Systematic variation of backbone length and aryl/alkyl substitution at phosphorus with yield measurement; bite-angle calculations or crystal structures; comparison of Ni–P bond lengths or ³¹P NMR shifts as proxies for electronic donation.

### 7. The origin of cross-coupling selectivity over homo-coupling is not explained

**(1)** A central claim is that the nickel-diphosphine catalyst achieves *selective* cross-coupling whereas previously known transition-metal-halide systems produce substantial homo-coupling. The paper does not identify which step in the catalytic cycle prevents homo-coupling, nor why intermediate **3**—which contains two different organic groups—preferentially undergoes cross- rather than homo-reductive elimination.

**(2)** Measurement of relative rates of reductive elimination from symmetric (homo) versus asymmetric (cross) diorganonickel complexes; detection of homo-coupling products as a function of catalyst loading and Grignard equivalents; or experiments with isotopically labelled Grignard reagent to distinguish statistical from selective coupling.

### 8. The superiority of diethyl ether over THF is asserted as "definite" without supporting data

**(1)** Diethyl ether is stated to be "definitely superior" to THF as solvent, but no comparative yield data are presented in the paper for the same reaction in both solvents; the claim cannot be evaluated from the information provided.

**(2)** Side-by-side yield comparison of at least one representative reaction in Et₂O and THF under otherwise identical conditions; kinetic measurements to determine whether THF accelerates catalyst decomposition, alters Grignard speciation, or inhibits an elementary step.

### 9. The step in which the organic halide attacks intermediate **3** to release the cross-coupling product is proposed but uncharacterised

**(1)** Equation 4 shows the organic halide R′X″ attacking the diorganonickel complex **3** to give R–R′ and regenerate **2**; this step—which must be reductive elimination coupled with or preceded by oxidative addition—is mechanistically the least precedented part of the cycle and is supported only by the analogy of eq 1 (a stoichiometric bipyridyl-nickel reaction), not by any direct observation in the catalytic system.

**(2)** Stoichiometric reaction of a well-defined diphosphine diorganonickel complex **3** with various organic halides; trapping or observation of a putative Ni(IV) or η²-complex intermediate; labelling studies to confirm that the C–C bond formed is specifically between the two organic groups already on nickel rather than a radical pathway.
