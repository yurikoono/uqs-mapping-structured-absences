Here is the list of unanswered questions and unsupported claims in the Tamao, Sumitani & Kumada communication (JACS 1972, 94, 4374–4376).

---

## 1. The catalytic cycle (intermediates 1 → 2 → 3) is proposed but not directly evidenced

The three proposed organonickel complexes are invoked as logical inference from two previously published stoichiometric reactions in unrelated systems; none is isolated, detected spectroscopically, or kinetically traced in the actual catalytic runs reported here. Evidence needed: isolation or independent synthesis of each intermediate; Michaelis–Menten or initial-rate kinetics showing the expected rate laws; trapping experiments consistent with the cycle.

## 2. Suppression of β-hydride elimination from alkylmagnesium reagents is claimed but not explained

High isolated yields are reported for couplings using n-BuMgBr and n-C₈H₁₇MgCl, reagents whose derived alkylnickel species would ordinarily undergo rapid β-hydride elimination. No byproduct analysis is provided, and the paper offers no mechanistic account of why elimination is disfavored. Evidence needed: product distributions (including alkene/alkane from β-elimination) as a function of temperature and ligand; determination of whether rate-limiting step precedes or follows alkyl transfer.

## 4. The claim that cis geometry in intermediate 3 is "the first requisite" rests only on a ligand-order correlation

The inference that bidentate ligands enforce cis-diorganonickel geometry, thereby enabling reductive elimination, is offered as the explanation for the observed ligand-activity series. No structural characterization of any diorganonickel complex 3 is provided, and the correlation between bite angle/flexibility and activity is not measured. Evidence needed: X-ray or NMR characterization of model L₂Ni(R)₂ complexes with each ligand; demonstration that an enforced trans complex is inactive.

## 5. The nonstereospecific stilbene result (cis enrichment from both cis- and trans-dichloroethylene) is reported as a fact but left mechanistically open

The paper describes the anomalous outcome as a "feature" and suggests it may provide a route to cis-stilbene, but gives no explanation for why both geometric isomers of the substrate converge on a cis-enriched product. Evidence needed: isotopic or radical-clock probes to determine whether geometry is lost at or after oxidative addition; independent synthesis of the proposed vinyl-nickel intermediates.

## 6. The restriction to sp² halides is stated as a categorical feature without mechanistic rationale

"Only C$_{\text{sp}^2}$ halides" are claimed to be reactive, but no attempted coupling with an sp³ halide is reported and no failure mode (absence of product, competing pathway) is documented. Evidence needed: attempted reactions with representative primary, secondary, and allylic halides; characterization of the oxidative addition step for sp³ vs. sp² substrates under the same conditions.

## 9. The yields are presented from admittedly non-optimized conditions, leaving the method's true performance ceiling unknown

The table footnote states conditions are "not necessarily optimum," but no guidance on which variables to tune or what yields could plausibly be achieved is given. Evidence needed: at least one substrate taken through an optimization showing how yield responds to catalyst loading, temperature, addition rate, and reagent ratio.

## 10. The paper closes by deferring all stereochemical and mechanistic conclusions to future work, leaving its own "working hypothesis" formally unsupported

The authors explicitly acknowledge that the proposed cycle is a hypothesis and that further studies are required, yet the communication presents mechanistic language (catalytic cycle, requisites of the catalyst) as if it were established. Evidence needed: the mechanistic and stereochemical studies deferred to subsequent publications; these are prerequisites for the claims made here to stand as conclusions rather than conjecture.
